# GSPT Coordinate Curvature, Fast-Slow SBL Manifold Bifurcations, and Discrete NWP Architecture

## 1. GSPT Coordinate Curvature and Nieuwstadt Local Scaling

Evaluating vertical profiles of atmospheric stability beyond the shallow surface layer requires abandoning the constant-flux assumption of classical Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory [sheba2002]. In the outer stable boundary layer (SBL), vertical turbulent transport decays continuously, causing momentum and heat fluxes to vary with height [nieuwstadt1984, sheba2002]. To account for this vertical structure, we invoke Nieuwstadt's local scaling hypothesis [nieuwstadt1984]. Under local scaling, the Obukhov length is generalized to a height-dependent scale $L(z)$ [nieuwstadt1984]:

$$L(z) \equiv \frac{-\tau(z)^{3/2}}{\kappa \dfrac{g}{\theta_0} H(z)}$$

where $\tau(z) > 0$ represents local vertical kinematic momentum flux (shear stress), $H(z) \equiv \overline{w'\theta_v'}(z) < 0$ represents local kinematic virtual potential temperature flux, $\kappa \approx 0.40$ is the von Kármán constant, and $g/\theta_0$ is the buoyancy parameter [businger1971].

To avoid numerical singularities near neutral conditions ($H \to 0, L \to -\infty$), we define the *inverse Obukhov length profile* $\chi(z) \equiv 1/L(z)$ [nieuwstadt1984]:

$$\chi(z) = \frac{-\kappa \dfrac{g}{\theta_0} H(z)}{\tau(z)^{3/2}} > 0$$

The local dimensionless similarity coordinate $\zeta(z)$ is mapped directly as:

$$\zeta(z) \equiv \frac{z}{L(z)} = z \chi(z)$$

Assuming active turbulence ($\tau > 0$) and a twice-differentiable inverse profile ($\chi \in C^2$), the first two vertical spatial derivatives of the coordinate mapping with respect to physical height $z$ are:

$$\zeta_z(z) = \chi(z) + z \chi'(z)$$

$$\zeta_{zz}(z) = 2\chi'(z) + z \chi''(z)$$

Expanding the logarithmic derivative yields $\frac{\chi'(z)}{\chi(z)} = \frac{H'(z)}{H(z)} - \frac{3}{2}\frac{\tau'(z)}{\tau(z)}$. Substituting this into $\zeta_z(z)$ expresses the coordinate Jacobian $\zeta_z(z)$ directly in terms of relative vertical flux gradients:

$$\zeta_z(z) = \chi(z) \left[ 1 + z \left( \frac{H'(z)}{H(z)} - \frac{3}{2}\frac{\tau'(z)}{\tau(z)} \right) \right]$$

### Kinematic Folds and Structural Exclusion

A *coordinate fold point* $z_* \in (0, z_{\text{top}})$ represents a physical height where the coordinate Jacobian vanishes ($\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$) [fenichel1979]. The fold is nondegenerate if the coordinate curvature is non-zero ($\zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0$). Under Nieuwstadt local scaling, coordinate folds are physically excluded from constant-flux layers:

> **Proposition 1 (Structural Exclusion of Folds)**
> Let $J \subset (0, z_{\text{top}})$ be a physical vertical subinterval on which turbulent fluxes are spatially uniform ($\tau'(z) = 0, H'(z) = 0$), such that the inverse Obukhov profile is constant ($\chi(z) \equiv \chi_0 > 0$). Then, the coordinate Jacobian satisfies $\zeta_z(z) = \chi_0 \neq 0$ for all $z \in J$; hence, $J$ contains no coordinate fold points.

> *Proof.* On the subinterval $J$, vertical flux gradients vanish identically, forcing $\chi'(z) \equiv 0$. Substituting this into $\zeta_z(z) = \chi(z) + z(0) = \chi_0$. Because $\chi_0 > 0$, the Jacobian never vanishes on $J$, preventing coordinate fold formation. $\blacksquare$

Proposition 1 proves that an observed coordinate fold is a kinematic signature of active vertical flux divergence. Setting $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ yields the generic fold locus condition:

$$1 + z_* \left( \frac{H'(z_*)}{H(z_*)} - \frac{3}{2}\frac{\tau'(z_*)}{\tau(z_*)} \right) = 0$$

This demonstrates that coordinate folds are governed by the **relative differential divergence** between kinetic energy transport ($\tau$) and thermal structure ($H$), rather than absolute heat-flux divergence alone.

### The GSPT Curvature Decomposition

Let $Ri_g(z) = R(\zeta(z))$ represent the gradient Richardson number, where $R(\cdot)$ is a smooth similarity closure in similarity space. Differentiating the Richardson profile twice with respect to physical height $z$ yields the GSPT curvature decomposition:

$$Ri_{g,z} = R'(\zeta) \zeta_z$$

$$Ri_{g,zz} = \underbrace{R''(\zeta) \zeta_z^2}_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{constitutive}}} + \underbrace{R'(\zeta) \zeta_{zz}}_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{mapping}}}$$

This equation partitions the total physical profile curvature into two additive, mathematically distinct geometric components:

1. **Constitutive Curvature ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{constitutive}}$):** The intrinsic curvature of the empirical stability function mapped into physical space, scaling quadratically with the coordinate Jacobian ($\zeta_z^2$).
2. **Mapping Curvature ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{mapping}}$):** The coordinate-induced curvature generated by the non-linear transformation $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ under active vertical flux divergence ($\zeta_{zz} \neq 0$).

> **Theorem 1 (Curvature Signature of a Nondegenerate Fold)**
> Let $z_*$ represent a nondegenerate coordinate fold point ($\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ and $\zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0$) under stable stratification, and assume $R'(\zeta_*) \neq 0$. Then, the physical gradient Richardson profile exhibits a stationary point with non-zero vertical curvature governed entirely by mapping coordinate geometry:
> $$Ri_{g,z}(z_*) = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad Ri_{g,zz}(z_*) = R'(\zeta_*) \zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0$$
>
>

> *Proof.* Evaluating $Ri_{g,z}$ at $z_*$ where $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ yields $Ri_{g,z}(z_*) = R'(\zeta_*)(0) = 0$. Evaluating $Ri_{g,zz}$ at the fold yields $Ri_{g,zz}(z_*) = R''(\zeta_*)(0)^2 + R'(\zeta_*) \zeta_{zz}(z_*) = R'(\zeta_*) \zeta_{zz}(z_*)$. Because $R'(\zeta_*) \neq 0$ and nondegeneracy guarantees $\zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0$, the physical vertical curvature at the fold is strictly non-zero. $\blacksquare$

To quantify whether an observed vertical spatial "knee" ($\vert{}Ri_{g,zz}\vert{} \gg 0$) stems from empirical closure failure or coordinate mapping curvature, we define the non-dimensional **coordinate-induced curvature ratio** $\mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}}(z)$:

$$\mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}}(z) \equiv \frac{\left\vert{} R'(\zeta) \zeta_{zz} \right\vert{}}{\left\vert{} R''(\zeta) \zeta_z^2 \right\vert{} + \left\vert{} R'(\zeta) \zeta_{zz} \right\vert{} + \varepsilon_C}$$

where $\varepsilon_C = 10^{-10}$ is a positive regularization constant. A value of $\mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}} \to 1$ certifies that an apparent physical profile knee is a **"Fold Illusion"** driven by non-linear coordinate mapping, whereas $\mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}} \to 0$ indicates genuine structural curvature in the similarity closure $R(\zeta)$.

## 2. Fast-Slow SBL Manifold Bifurcations and Asymptotic Regularity

Standard boundary-layer closures assume monotonic buoyant damping ($d\mathcal{B}/dE > 0$), forcing a single continuous equilibrium that prevents models from capturing the sudden, discontinuous turbulence collapse observed during nocturnal transitions [cases99]. To model this regime transition dynamically, we introduce a non-monotonic, hump-shaped buoyancy-destruction closure $D_b(E; N^2)$:

$$D_b(E; N^2) = c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha E}{(E+\alpha)^2}$$

where $c_b > 0$ is a dimensionless efficiency factor and $\alpha > 0$ represents the regularized energy scale. Differentiating with respect to TKE yields:

$$D_b'(E; N^2) = c_b N^2 \alpha \frac{\alpha - E}{(E+\alpha)^3}$$

This reveals two physically distinct regimes:

* **Sub-critical mixing ($E \le \alpha$):** As TKE decreases, negative buoyancy fluxes efficiently quench vertical velocity variance ($D_b'(E) > 0$), dampening turbulence.
* **Super-critical mixing ($E > \alpha$):** At higher TKE, energetic turbulent eddies break up localized stratification structures, suppressing the relative efficiency of buoyant destruction ($D_b'(E) < 0$).

Under the corrected 1.5-order prognostic TKE budget, the fast subsystem satisfies:

$$\epsilon \frac{dE}{dt} = f(E, S; N^2) = l \sqrt{E+\delta} \left[ S^2 - \phi N^2 - c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha E}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right] - \frac{E^{3/2}}{l}$$

where $\delta = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$ is the singularity regularization floor. Setting $f(E, S) = 0$ defines the critical manifold $\mathcal{C}_0$ as the equilibrium wind shear surface $S(E; N^2)$:

$$S(E; N^2) = \sqrt{\phi N^2 + c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha E}{(E+\alpha)^2} + \frac{E^{3/2}}{l^2\sqrt{E+\delta}}}$$

An ordinary saddle-node fold point ($E^*, S^*, N^{*2}$) occurs where the equilibrium curve has a turning point ($dS/dE = 0$), corresponding to $f(E, S; N^2) = 0$ and $f_E(E, S; N^2) \equiv \lambda_f = 0$, where the fast-manifold stability eigenvalue $\lambda_f$ is:

$$\lambda_f = \frac{l(S^2 - \phi N^2)}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} - c_b N^2 l \left[ \frac{E}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}(E+\alpha)} + \frac{\alpha\sqrt{E+\delta}}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right] - \frac{3\sqrt{E}}{2l}$$

### Analytical Derivation of the Fold Locus

Substituting the equilibrium condition into the zero-eigenvalue condition eliminates mean shear $S$, mapping the exact regularized **Fold Locus**:

$$N^2_{\text{fold}}(E; \delta) = \frac{(E+\alpha)^3 E^{1/2}(3E+4\delta)}{2 l^2 c_b \alpha (E-\alpha)(E+\delta)^{3/2}} \qquad \forall E > \alpha$$

Because the denominator contains $(E-\alpha)$, a physical fold ($N^2_{\text{fold}} > 0$) strictly requires $E > \alpha$, proving that folds cannot occur within the sub-critical mixing regime.

### The Critical Cusp and Stratification Threshold

The fold pair appears when the fold locus reaches its mathematical minimum with respect to $E$: $N^2_{\text{crit}} \equiv \min_{E > \alpha} N^2_{\text{fold}}(E; \delta)$. In the unregularized limit ($\delta \to 0$), setting the derivative to zero yields:

$$\frac{d}{dE} \left[ \frac{3(E+\alpha)^3}{2 l^2 c_b \alpha (E-\alpha)} \right] = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{2(E+\alpha)^2 (E-2\alpha)}{(E-\alpha)^2} = 0$$

The physical root establishes the exact cusp coordinate $E_{\text{cusp}} = 2\alpha$, yielding the exact critical cusp stratification:

$$N^2_{\text{crit}}(0) = \frac{81\alpha}{2 l^2 c_b}$$

For standard parameters ($l = 1.0\text{ m}$, $c_b = 0.50$, $\alpha = 0.05\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$), $E_{\text{cusp}} = 0.10\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$ and $N^2_{\text{crit}} \approx 4.05\text{ s}^{-2}$.

### Topological Manifold Branches and Regime Transitions

For stratifications exceeding $N^2 > N^2_{\text{crit}}$, the equilibrium manifold contains a folded, three-branch structure:

1. **Weakly Stable Boundary Layer (WSBL) branch ($E > E_{\text{fold,upper}}$):** Attracting branch ($\lambda_f < 0$) with high TKE, strong shear production, and continuous mixing.
2. **Fast-Repelling branch ($E_{\text{fold,lower}} < E < E_{\text{fold,upper}}$):** Unstable branch ($\lambda_f > 0$) acting as the state-space separatrix.
3. **Very Stable Boundary Layer (VSBL) branch ($E < E_{\text{fold,lower}}$):** Attracting branch ($\lambda_f < 0$) characterized by suppressed turbulence, weak surface coupling, and runaway cooling.

### Asymptotic Regularity Exponents at Boundary Transitions

Near the SBL top ($z \to z_c^-$), turbulent fluxes decay into the free atmosphere:

$$\tau(z) \sim A(z_c - z)^a, \qquad H(z) \sim -B(z_c - z)^b \qquad (a, b > 0)$$

This yields an asymptotic scaling $\chi(z) \sim C(z_c - z)^p$, where the local regularity exponent is $p = b - \frac{3}{2}a$.

> **Lemma 1 ($C^2$ Asymptotic Boundary Regularity Threshold)**
> To ensure bounded, non-singular coordinate transformations ($\zeta_z, \zeta_{zz} \in L^\infty$) as $z \to z_c^-$, sub-grid turbulence schemes must satisfy:
> $$p = b - \frac{3}{2}a \ge 2$$
>
>

> *Proof.* Differentiating $\chi(z) \sim C(z_c - z)^p$ twice yields $\chi''(z) \sim C p (p-1) (z_c - z)^{p-2}$. The coordinate derivative $\zeta_{zz} = 2\chi' + z\chi''$ remains bounded at $z = z_c$ if and only if $p - 2 \ge 0$, establishing $p \ge 2$. $\blacksquare$

Linear decay closures ($a = 1, b = 1$) yield $p = -0.5 < 0$, creating an unphysical coordinate singularity ($\zeta_{zz} \to \infty$). Enforcing $a = 2, b = 5$ yields $p = 2.0$, satisfying Lemma 1 and guaranteeing smooth $C^2$-regular numerical projections.

## 3. Discrete Numerical Architecture and Stability Proofs

To translate continuous GSPT formulations into discrete atmospheric models without grid-point coalescence or time-step crashes, we establish an adaptive grid-stretching algorithm alongside an invariant manifold solver [nieuwstadt1984].

### Discrete Equidistribution and Bounded Grid Stretching

Let $z \in [0, H_{\text{SBL}}]$ represent physical vertical height. We define the adaptive physical coordinate density function $\rho_{\text{clamp}}(z)$ as:

$$\rho_{\text{clamp}}(z) \equiv \max\left( \frac{k}{\Delta z_{\text{max}}}, \; \min\left( \frac{k}{\Delta z_{\text{min}}}, \; \left\vert{} \zeta_{zz}(z) \right\vert{} + \epsilon_z \right) \right)$$

Physical vertical levels $z_j$ ($j = 1, \dots, N_z$) are generated by uniform equidistribution of $y \in [0, 1]$: $y_j = \frac{j - 1}{N_z - 1}$, $z_j = y^{-1}(y_j)$.

> **Theorem 2 (Discrete Grid-Spacing Bounds)**
> Under the bounded equidistribution density $\rho_{\text{clamp}}(z)$, the discrete physical grid steps $\Delta z_j \equiv z_j - z_{j-1}$ are globally bounded by:
> $$\Delta z_{\text{min}} \le \Delta z_j \le \Delta z_{\text{max}} \qquad \forall j \in \{2, \dots, N_z\}$$
>
>

> *Proof.* The Mean Value Theorem on $y(z)$ guarantees $\frac{1}{N_z - 1} = \frac{\rho_{\text{clamp}}(\xi)}{C_{\text{max}}} \Delta z_j$. Solving for $\Delta z_j$ and substituting the density bounds $\frac{k}{\Delta z_{\text{max}}} \le \rho_{\text{clamp}}(z) \le \frac{k}{\Delta z_{\text{min}}}$ directly yields $\Delta z_{\text{min}} \le \Delta z_j \le \Delta z_{\text{max}}$. $\blacksquare$

> **Theorem 3 (Asymptotic Grid Convergence)**
> Let $\chi(z) \in C^2$ represent a smooth inverse Obukhov length profile. The discrete vertical coordinates $z_j$ converge asymptotically to the continuous optimal coordinate path $z(y)$ with quadratic accuracy:
> $$\max_{1 \le j \le N_z} \left\vert{} z_j - z(y_j) \right\vert{} = \mathcal{O}\left( N_z^{-2} \right)$$
>
>

> *Proof.* A second-order Taylor expansion of $z(y)$ gives $z(y_j) = z(y_{j-1}) + \frac{dz}{dy}\Delta y + \frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2z}{dy^2}(\eta_j) (\Delta y)^2$. Since $\frac{d^2 z}{dy^2} = -\frac{C_{\text{max}}^2 \rho_{\text{clamp}}'(z)}{\left[ \rho_{\text{clamp}}(z) \right]^3}$ is bounded pointwise by $M > 0$, the error bound simplifies to $\frac{M}{2 (N_z - 1)^2} = \mathcal{O}(N_z^{-2})$. $\blacksquare$

### Fenichel Invariant Manifold Slaving and Stiffness Regularization

Low-order Galerkin projections are prone to spurious finite-time blowup near turning points ($\lambda_f \to 0$) [mcnider1995]. To regularize this stiffness, we apply **Fenichel Invariant Manifold Slaving** [fenichel1979], replacing the prognostic equation $\dot{u}_2$ with:

$$u_2 = \Phi(u_1, S) \equiv -\frac{c_{\text{cross}} u_1^2}{2 \left\vert{} \lambda_f(S) \right\vert{} + \delta_m}$$

where $c_{\text{cross}} > 0$ and $\delta_m \sim 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$ is a hyperbolic regularization parameter.

> **Theorem 4 (Global Boundedness of Slaved Galerkin Trajectories)**
> Substituting the regularized Fenichel slaving relation into the primary slow mode equation eliminates the spurious cubic production feedback term $\sigma u_1^3$ entirely, establishing a globally bounded attractor on $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$.

> *Proof.* Substituting $u_2$ into $\dot{u}_1 = G - c_{\text{shear}} u_1 - \beta u_1 u_2 - K_d u_1^3$ yields effective damping if $K_d > \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{\delta_m}$. This defines a compact invariant trapping region $\mathcal{K} = \left\{ u_1 \in \mathbb{R} : 0 \le u_1 \le \sqrt{\frac{G}{\Lambda_0}} + 1 \right\}$ from which trajectories cannot escape. $\blacksquare$

### Coupled Solver CFL Guarantees

> **Proposition 2 (Global CFL Stability Guarantee)**
> The maximum time step $\Delta t$ required for numerical stability remains strictly bounded from below by a non-zero physical limit:
> $$\Delta t \ge \frac{(\Delta z_{\text{min}})^2}{2 K_{\text{max}}} \equiv \Delta t_{\text{CFL}} > 0$$
>
>

> *Proof.* Stability requires $\Delta t \le \frac{(\Delta z_j)^2}{2 K_j}$. By Theorem 2, $\Delta z_j \ge \Delta z_{\text{min}}$, and Theorem 4 bounds $E \le E_{\text{max}}$ (hence $K \le K_{\text{max}}$), guaranteeing a non-vanishing minimum time step. $\blacksquare$

## 4. SCM Evaluation and NWP Algorithmic Implications

Implementing these geometric and dynamical formulations within operational SCM and NWP architectures provides three critical algorithmic fixes:

### Operational Curvature Residual Partitioning

To prevent compensating errors during model tuning, we establish an additive curvature error partition:

$$\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z) \equiv Ri_{zz}^{\text{model}}(z) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z) = \Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) + \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) + \tau_{\Delta z}(z)$$

1. **Constitutive Closure Failure ($\Delta_{\text{closure}}$):** Isolates stability parameterization errors:

$$\Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{obs}} \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{obs}} \right) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z)$$


2. **Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion ($\Delta_{\text{geom}}$):** Isolates vertical flux-transport curvature errors:

$$\Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{model}} \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{model}} \right) - \left( C_{\text{constitutive}}^{\text{obs}} + C_{\text{mapping}}^{\text{obs}} \right)$$


3. **Discretization Truncation Artifact ($\tau_{\Delta z}$):** Quantifies grid truncation error:

$$\tau_{\Delta z}(z) \approx \frac{(\Delta z)^2}{12} \frac{\partial^4 Ri_g}{\partial z^4}$$



### SCM Calibration Gradient Masking

To prevent parameter drift during automated optimization, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}}(z)$ is converted into a dynamic loss gradient weighting mask $w(z)$:

$$w(z) = 1 - \tanh\left( \gamma \cdot \max\left(0, \; \mathcal{C}_{\text{coord}}(z) - \mathcal{C}_{\text{thresh}}\right) \right)$$

Setting $\mathcal{C}_{\text{thresh}} \approx 0.65$ zeroes out parameter updates in layers where physical curvature is coordinate-induced.

### Asymptotic Regularity Constraints

Enforcing $p = b - \frac{3}{2}a \ge 2$ with flux decay exponents $a = 2$ and $b = 5$ eliminates coordinate singularities ($\chi'' \to \infty$) at the SBL top, guaranteeing smooth transitions into the free atmosphere.

## 5. Conclusions and Future Outlook

We have established a mathematically rigorous, singularity-free framework for stable boundary layer modeling by unifying Geometric Singular Perturbation Theory (GSPT) with profile differential geometry [nieuwstadt1984]. Re-parameterizing Nieuwstadt local scaling via inverse Obukhov length $\chi(z) \equiv 1/L(z)$ resolved neutral singularities and proved that profile "knees" are often **"Fold Illusions"** driven by mapping curvature under active flux divergence [sheba2002].

Dynamically, SBL turbulence collapse was mapped as a slow-passage bifurcation across a folded critical manifold $\mathcal{C}_0$. To stabilize numerical solvers across these regimes, we introduced three structural implementations:

1. **Adaptive Equidistribution Grid Stretching ($\Delta z \propto 1/\vert{}\zeta_{zz}\vert{}$):** Resolves steep coordinate gradients with $\mathcal{O}(N_z^{-2})$ convergence while maintaining CFL bounds [gabls3_2013].
2. **Fenichel Invariant Manifold Slaving ($u_2 = \Phi(u_1, S)$):** Replaces fast-mode ODEs with algebraic slow-manifold parameterizations, eliminating Galerkin blowups [fenichel1979].
3. **Asymptotic Boundary Regularity ($b - \frac{3}{2}a \ge 2$):** Eliminates coordinate singularities at the SBL top via quadratic ($a=2$) and quintic ($b=5$) flux decay envelopes.

### Future Research Directions

* **Doppler Lidar Profile Integration:** Extending Tikhonov splines to handle range-dependent signal-to-noise ratios in remote-sensing profiles.
* **WSINDy Dynamical Discovery:** Feeding non-singular coordinate fields into Weak-form Sparse Identification of Non-linear Dynamics algorithms to extract governing SBL PDEs.
* **Machine Learning Regime Classification:** Training explainable classifiers on GSPT fold trajectories $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ to detect nocturnal regime transitions in real time.
