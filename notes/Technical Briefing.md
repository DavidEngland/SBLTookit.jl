### Technical Briefing: SCM Error Partitioning and Profile Curvature via GSPT

---

### Executive Summary & Physical Anchor

In Stable Boundary Layer (SBL) modeling, the vertical profiles of the gradient Richardson number (\(Ri_g\)) are highly sensitive to both the choice of empirical shear functions (\(\phi_m, \phi_h\)) and the representation of vertical flux profiles. Classic Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) assumes a constant-flux surface layer where the Obukhov length gradient is zero (\(L' = 0\)). To extend this framework across the entire SBL, we invoke **Nieuwstadt’s local scaling hypothesis**. Under this hypothesis, the Monin-Obukhov length becomes a height-dependent scale, \(L(z)\), constructed directly from local, height-varying turbulent fluxes.

When Single-Column Models (SCMs) fail to replicate observed gradients under stable conditions, developers often "tune" Monin-Obukhov stability constants. GSPT prevents this **compensating error** by analyzing the vertical spatial second derivative of the Richardson number profile (\(Ri_{zz} \equiv \partial^2 Ri_g / \partial z^2\)). This approach separates **constitutive closure failures** (empirical stability functions) from **flux-coordinate distortions** (unresolved vertical flux profiles) and **numerical discretization artifacts**.

---

### 1. Corrected Curvature Decomposition Formulation

To mathematically analyze physical-space profile geometry, GSPT defines the mapping from physical height \(z\) to the non-dimensional similarity coordinate \(\zeta\):
\[\zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)} \tag{1}\]

Evaluating the spatial coordinate derivatives with respect tophysical vertical space \(z\) yields:
\[\zeta_z = \frac{1 - \zeta L'}{L} \tag{2}\]
\[\zeta_{zz} = -\frac{L''}{L}\zeta - 2\frac{L'}{L}\zeta_z \tag{3}\]

where \(L'\) and \(L''\) represent the first and second vertical derivatives of the local Obukhov length profile, representing flux divergence.

Applying the multi-variable chain rule to any local similarity-closure relation \(Ri_g(z) = R(\zeta(z))\) decomposes the total vertical spatial curvature into two additive, mathematically distinct components:
\[\boxed{Ri_{zz} = C_{\text{constitutive}} + C_{\text{mapping}}} \tag{4}\]

*   **Constitutive Curvature (\(C_{\text{constitutive}}\)):** Represents the spatial bending of the empirical stability relationship mapped into physical space. Because it scales quadratically with the coordinate Jacobian (\(\zeta_z^2\)), it remains highly sensitive to local grid stretching:
    \[C_{\text{constitutive}} \equiv R_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2 \tag{5}\]
*   **Mapping Curvature (\(C_{\text{mapping}}\)):** Represents the pure geometric bending generated by the non-linear coordinate projection \(z \mapsto \zeta(z)\). It is strictly proportional to coordinate curvature (\(\zeta_{zz}\)):
    \[C_{\text{mapping}} \equiv R_\zeta \zeta_{zz} \tag{6}\]

#### Correcting Mathematical Inconsistencies
Previous unregularized representations contained two key logical errors that compromised diagnostic validity:
1.  **Avoid Double-Counting:** Defining constitutive curvature as the total derivative (\(\partial^2 R / \partial z^2\)) yields \(Ri_{zz}\) itself. To preserve mathematical closure, \(C_{\text{constitutive}}\) must be defined strictly as the partial derivative scaled by the coordinate Jacobian squared (\(R_{\zeta\zeta}\zeta_z^2\)).
2.  **Preserving Cross-Terms:** Representing mapping curvature as \(Ri_g \cdot L''/L\) neglects the crucial cross-term (\(-2\frac{L'}{L}\zeta_z\)) in \(\zeta_{zz}\). Retaining the full analytical expression for \(\zeta_{zz}\) in Equation (3) is mandatory to capture coordinate behavior away from turning points.

### 2. The Additive Error Partitioning Schema

Because the similarity relation \(R(\zeta)\) is non-linear, changing the vertical flux-geometry (\(L\)) shifts the evaluation point (\(\zeta\)) of the stability functions. This non-linear cross-coupling means that the error components are not strictly orthogonal.

Instead, GSPT treats the total model-observation curvature residual (\(\Delta_{\text{SCM}}\)) as an **additive diagnostic decomposition** into **separable error channels**:
\[\boxed{\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z) = \Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) + \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) + \tau_{\Delta z}(z)} \tag{7}\]

Each component of the curvature error budget is operationally defined relative to an observed, high-resolution reference tower grid:

1.  **Constitutive Closure Failure (\(\Delta_{\text{closure}}\)):** Projects the SCM’s empirical similarity closure relation, \(R^{\text{model}}(\zeta)\), onto the observed, well-conditioned coordinate mapping, isolating local stability scaling errors under identical physical forcing:
    \[\Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{obs}} \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{obs}} \right) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z) \tag{8}\]
2.  **Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion (\(\Delta_{\text{geom}}\)):** Projects the observed similarity closure \(R^{\text{obs}}(\zeta)\) onto the model's simulated coordinate mapping to isolate vertical transport and flux divergence errors:
    \[\Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{model}} \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{model}} \right) - \left( C_{\text{constitutive}}^{\text{obs}} + C_{\text{mapping}}^{\text{obs}} \right) \tag{9}\]
3.  **Discretization and Grid-Scale Truncation Artifact (\(\tau_{\Delta z}\)):** Quantifies the purely numerical error introduced by coarse vertical grid spacing. For a standard centered second-difference operator, this is the leading-order Taylor truncation error:
    \[\tau_{\Delta z}(z) \approx \frac{(\Delta z)^2}{12} \frac{\partial^4 Ri_g}{\partial z^4} \tag{10}\]

### 3. SCM Calibration Decision Matrix

By analyzing the relative magnitudes of these partitioned residuals along the vertical coordinate, SCM developers can diagnose structural deficiencies and take targeted corrective action:

| Residual Signature | Physical Interpretation | Targeted Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **\(|\Delta_{\text{closure}}| \gg 0\)** <br> \(\Delta_{\text{geom}} \approx 0\) | **Constitutive Closure Failure:** <br> Local stability functions are structurally incorrect under stable stratification. | Swap the local boundary-layer closure scheme. Transition from standard Businger-Dyer functions to non-singular Grachev et al. (2007) physics. |
| **\(|\Delta_{\text{geom}}| \gg 0\)** <br> \(\Delta_{\text{closure}} \approx 0\) | **Flux-Coordinate Distortion:** <br> Stability functions are correct, but the model over-smooths heat and momentum flux divergence profiles (\(L', L''\)). | **Do not alter the similarity parameters (\(\beta_m, \beta_h\)).** Retune vertical mixing lengths (\(l_m\)), sub-grid TKE dissipation rates, or local transport closures. |
| **\(|\tau_{\Delta z}| \gg 0\)** | **Grid Discretization Artifact:** <br> Coarse vertical grid spacing fails to capture fine-scale vertical gradient non-linearities. | Refine the vertical grid layout. Apply logarithmic grid stretching in the near-surface layer (\(z < 20\text{ m}\)) to resolve intense gradients. |

### 4. Advanced Preprocessing: Pre-Diagnostic Regularization

To prevent observational noise from generating spurious coordinate structures in field data (such as CASES-99 or SHEBA tower profiles), the data pipeline must address operator non-commutation.

Because the discrete difference (\(D_{\Delta z}\)), Tikhonov smoothing (\(M_\delta\)), and non-linear quotient (\(\mathcal{Q}\)) operators do not commute, **regularization must be applied directly to the primitive state variables prior to spatial differentiation and division**:
\[Ri_g^{\text{pre-smooth}} = \mathcal{Q} \circ D_{\Delta z} \circ M_\delta [u, v, \theta_v] \tag{11}\]

Computing a raw, noisy Richardson profile and smoothing it afterward (the post-diagnostic path: \(M_\delta \circ \mathcal{Q} \circ D_{\Delta z}\)) is mathematically invalid. Near zero-shear zones (such as the nose of a Low-Level Jet where \(U_z \to 0\)), gradient noise variance blows up as **\(U_z^{-6}\)**. Post-diagnostic filters are forced to bend aggressively around these low-shear singularities, **manufacturing spurious "profile knees" (Fold Illusions) in physical space**. Primitive-field regularization ensures that noise suppression remains grounded at the linear, Gaussian sensor-variable level, preserving the true physical flux divergence.
