### 1. The Role of the Jacobian in Fast-Slow Stability Analysis

In a singularly perturbed fast-slow system with timescale separation \(0 < \epsilon \ll 1\), the slow variable \(S\) evolves on a slow timescale \(t\). To analyze the fast dynamics, we rescale the system to the fast timescale \(\tau = t/\epsilon\), which yields the **layer problem**:
\[\frac{dE}{d\tau} = f(E,S)\]
\[\frac{dS}{d\tau} = \epsilon g(E,S)\]

In the singular limit \(\epsilon \to 0\), the slow variable \(S\) remains constant (acting as a static parameter). The dynamics are entirely governed by the one-dimensional fast layer equation. The **critical manifold** \(\mathcal{C}_0\) represents the set of equilibria for this fast layer problem, defined by the algebraic constraint \(f(E, S) = 0\).

To evaluate the local stability of any point on \(\mathcal{C}_0\), we linearize the fast subsystem with respect to the fast variable \(E\). This is mathematically equivalent to taking the Jacobian of the 1D fast layer equation:
\[\frac{d(\delta E)}{d\tau} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial E}(E, S) \delta E \equiv \lambda_f(E, S) \delta E\]

Therefore, the **fast eigenvalue** \(\lambda_f(E,S)\) serves as the local stability multiplier for the fast relaxation dynamics. Fenichel's theory guarantees that the sign of \(\lambda_f\) determines the local stability and normal hyperbolicity of the manifold:

* **Attracting Branch (\(\lambda_f < 0\)):** The critical manifold is normally hyperbolic and asymptotically stable. Trajectories perturbed away from the manifold relax exponentially back onto \(\mathcal{C}_0\) on a rapid timescale of \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/|\lambda_f|)\).
* **Repelling Branch (\(\lambda_f > 0\)):** The manifold is normally hyperbolic but unstable. Small perturbations grow exponentially, driving trajectories away from the critical manifold.
* **Fold Boundary (\(\lambda_f = 0\)):** Normal hyperbolicity completely breaks down. These points correspond to a saddle-node bifurcation of the fast subsystem, signifying the tipping points where active turbulence collapses or ignites.

### 2. Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation of \(\lambda_f(E, S)\)

To find the analytical expression for \(\lambda_f(E,S) \equiv \frac{\partial f}{\partial E}(E,S)\), we decompose the fast vector field into two distinct terms:
\[f(E, S) = T_1(E, S) - T_2(E)\]

where:
\[T_1(E, S) = l \sqrt{E+\delta} \left( S^2 - \phi N^2 - c_b N^2 \frac{E}{E+\alpha} \right)\]
\[T_2(E) = \frac{E^{3/2}}{l}\]

#### Step A: Differentiating the Dissipation Term (\(T_2\))

Using the power rule on \(T_2(E) = \frac{1}{l} E^{3/2}\):
\[\frac{\partial T_2}{\partial E} = \frac{1}{l} \left( \frac{3}{2} E^{1/2} \right) = \frac{3\sqrt{E}}{2l}\]

#### Step B: Differentiating the Production/Buoyancy Term (\(T_1\)) using the Product Rule

Let:
\[u(E) = l\sqrt{E+\delta} = l(E+\delta)^{1/2}\]
\[v(E) = S^2 - \phi N^2 - c_b N^2 \frac{E}{E+\alpha}\]

The product rule states that \(\frac{\partial T_1}{\partial E} = u'(E)v(E) + u(E)v'(E)\).

1. **Differentiating \(u(E)\):**
    \[u'(E) = \frac{d}{dE}\left[l(E+\delta)^{1/2}\right] = \frac{l}{2}(E+\delta)^{-1/2} = \frac{l}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}}\]

2. **Differentiating \(v(E)\):**
    Since \(S\) and \(N^2\) are constant with respect to TKE (\(E\)), their derivatives are zero. To differentiate the fraction, we apply the **quotient rule** to \(w(E) = \frac{E}{E+\alpha}\):
    \[\frac{d}{dE}\left(\frac{E}{E+\alpha}\right) = \frac{(1)(E+\alpha) - E(1)}{(E+\alpha)^2} = \frac{\alpha}{(E+\alpha)^2}\]
    Multiplying by the coefficients, we obtain:
    \[v'(E) = -c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha}{(E+\alpha)^2}\]

3. **Combining terms via the Product Rule:**
    \[\frac{\partial T_1}{\partial E} = \left[ \frac{l}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} \right] \left( S^2 - \phi N^2 - c_b N^2 \frac{E}{E+\alpha} \right) + \left[ l\sqrt{E+\delta} \right] \left( -c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right)\]

#### Step C: Algebraic Consolidation

Now, we distribute the first term:
\[\frac{\partial T_1}{\partial E} = \frac{l\left(S^2 - \phi N^2\right)}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} - \frac{l c_b N^2 E}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}(E+\alpha)} - \frac{l c_b N^2 \alpha \sqrt{E+\delta}}{(E+\alpha)^2}\]

We can factor out \(-c_b N^2 l\) from the second and third components:
\[\frac{\partial T_1}{\partial E} = \frac{l\left(S^2 - \phi N^2\right)}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} - c_b N^2 l \left[ \frac{E}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}(E+\alpha)} + \frac{\alpha\sqrt{E+\delta}}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right]\]

#### Step D: Final Synthesis

Assembling the full derivative \(\lambda_f(E, S) = \frac{\partial T_1}{\partial E} - \frac{\partial T_2}{\partial E}\) yields:
\[\lambda_f(E, S) = \frac{l\left(S^2 - \phi N^2\right)}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} - c_b N^2 l \left[ \frac{E}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}(E+\alpha)} + \frac{\alpha\sqrt{E+\delta}}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right] - \frac{3\sqrt{E}}{2l}\]

This completes the derivation of the exact, globally \(C^1\)-continuous fast eigenvalue.
