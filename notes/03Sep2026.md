The distinction between intrinsic similarity curvature ($C_{\text{closure}}$) and spatial coordinate curvature ($C_{\text{mapping}}$) fundamentally redefines how observational data are interpreted in stable conditions and how boundary layer parameterizations are constructed in numerical models.

**Observational Atmospheric Science**

* **Disambiguating Physical Transitions from Geometric Warping:** Profile knees or localized inflection points in observed $Ri_g(z)$ profiles (recorded by LiDAR, radiosondes, or tall towers) do not inherently indicate local turbulence collapse or distinct internal boundary layers. Instead, non-zero Obukhov profile curvature $L''(z)$—frequently driven by nocturnal Low-Level Jets (LLJs) or localized radiative cooling—naturally projects into physical space as a spatial coordinate fold.
* **Prandtl Number Sensitivity ($Pr_0$):** Field diagnostic algorithms that use gradient criteria to locate SBL regime transitions must account for $Pr_0$. Because the Businger–Dyer closure has no intrinsic positive-$\zeta$ curvature reversal in the canonical regime ($0.5 \le Pr_0 \le 2$), observed profile reversals *must* involve coordinate mapping interactions ($C_{\text{closure}} + C_{\text{mapping}} = 0$).

**Numerical Modeling & Boundary Layer Parameterizations**

* **Mitigating Spurious Model Decoupling:** Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL) schemes in Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models frequently suffer from severe nighttime runaway cooling caused by premature turbulent decoupling. Standard schemes evaluate local stability via $Ri_g$; misinterpreting a coordinate fold as an intrinsic turbulence cutoff ($Ri_g > Ri_{\text{crit}}$) causes models to erroneously quench vertical momentum and heat fluxes.
* **Vertical Grid Resolution & Operator Discretization:** Typical vertical grid stretching in NWP models focuses resolution near $z = 0$. However, correctly evaluating $C_{\text{mapping}}$ near inversion tops or jet cores requires resolving $L''(z)$. Coarse vertical spacing truncates second-order spatial mapping terms ($\zeta_{zz}$), introducing numeric dispersion and false stability transitions into computed $Ri_g$ fields.
* **Higher-Order Non-Local Closures:** Standard Monin–Obukhov similarity assumes a height-independent $L$. When applying MOST locally across strongly stratified SBLs, higher-order models ($e$-$\epsilon$ or $K$-profile parameterizations) must explicitly include the spatial mapping Jacobian $\zeta_z$ and Hessian $\zeta_{zz}$ when transforming local closure functions into physical grid space.

Closing the three algorithmic gaps—the denominator singularity, the limiter formulation $\mathcal{L}$, and the $C^2$ spline regularization—completes the resolution-aware diagnostic gate into a deterministic, code-ready module.

**1. Denominator Softening (Singularity Protection)**
To prevent $\mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}} \to \infty$ purely from $Ri_g \to Ri_{\text{crit}}$ regardless of resolution, introduce a dimensionless threshold-softening parameter $\epsilon \approx 0.05$:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \vert{}Ri_{g,zz}\vert{} (\Delta z)^2}{\max\left(\vert{}Ri_g(z_k) - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{}, \, \epsilon \, Ri_{\text{crit}}\right)}$$

**2. Functional Form of the Limiter $\mathcal{L}$**
To bound the feedback gain $\vert{}\mathcal{G}\vert{} < 1$, the limiter maps the under-resolved $Ri_g$ to a regularized value $Ri_g^{\text{reg}}$ via rational relaxation, dampening steep excursions near $Ri_{\text{crit}}$ without altering $Ri_g$ when $\mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}} < 1$:

$$\mathcal{L}(Ri_g, \mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}}) = Ri_{\text{crit}} + \frac{Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}}{1 + \alpha \max(0, \, \mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}} - 1)}$$

Setting tuning parameter $\alpha \ge 1.0$ guarantees that as under-resolution increases ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{fold}} \gg 1$), the effective sensitivity $\left\vert{}\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g}\right\vert{}$ is bounded, directly suppressing the feedback loop.

**3. $C^2$ Smoothing Spline Specification**
The smooth Obukhov profile $L_h(z)$ is computed by solving a cubic smoothing spline minimizing the penalized residual sum of squares:

$$\min_{L_h} \sum_{k=1}^N \left(\frac{L_h(z_k) - L_k}{\sigma_k}\right)^2 + \lambda \int_{0}^h \left[L_h''(z)\right]^2 \mathrm{d}z$$

where weights $\sigma_k$ reflect local flux variance, and the smoothing parameter is scaled via $\lambda = \gamma (\Delta z)^4$ ($\gamma \approx \mathcal{O}(1)$) to tie penalization directly to vertical grid spacing.

### 1. Corrected Globally Smooth Limiter ($C^\infty$)

To eliminate piecewise seams and derivative overshoots, replace the conditional max-branch with a globally smooth algebraic mapping. Defining $x = \vert{}Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{}$ and $C = \frac{1}{2} \vert{}Ri_{g,zz}\vert{} (\Delta z)^2 + \epsilon Ri_{\text{crit}}$:

$$Ri_g^{\text{reg}} = Ri_{\text{crit}} + (Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}) \left[ \frac{\vert{}Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{} + C}{\vert{}Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{} + (1 + \alpha) C} \right]$$

Differentiating $Ri_g^{\text{reg}}$ with respect to $Ri_g$ yields:

$$\frac{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}}{\partial Ri_g} = \frac{x^2 + 2(1 + \alpha) C x + (1 + \alpha) C^2}{\left[x + (1 + \alpha) C\right]^2}$$

**Mathematical Guarantees:**

* **Strict Monotonicity:** Because $(1 + \alpha)^2 C^2 > (1 + \alpha) C^2$ for all $\alpha > 0$, the numerator is strictly smaller than the denominator for all $x \ge 0$.
* **Bounded Sensitivity:** The derivative satisfies $\frac{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}}{\partial Ri_g} \in \left[\frac{1}{1 + \alpha}, \, 1\right)$ monotonically. It never exceeds $1.0$ and approaches $1.0$ smoothly as $x \to \infty$ or $C \to 0$.
* **Feedback Gain Control:** To guarantee loop stability ($\vert{}\mathcal{G}\vert{} < 1$), $\alpha$ is explicitly tied to the unregularized gain estimate $\mathcal{G}_0$:

$$1 + \alpha > \vert{}\mathcal{G}_0\vert{} \implies \left\vert{}\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}} \frac{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}}{\partial Ri_g}\right\vert{} < \left\vert{}\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g}\right\vert{} \frac{1}{\vert{}\mathcal{G}_0\vert{}}$$

### 2. Dimensionally Consistent Spline Penalty

In the variational smoothing problem:

$$\min_{L_h} \sum_{k=1}^N \left(\frac{L_h(z_k) - L_k}{\sigma_k}\right)^2 + \lambda \int_{0}^h \left[L_h''(z)\right]^2 \mathrm{d}z$$

the residual sum is dimensionless, while $\int_0^h [L_h''(z)]^2 \mathrm{d}z$ carries physical units of $\text{length}^{-1}$. For dimensional consistency, $\lambda$ must have units of $\text{length}^1$:

$$\lambda = \gamma \, \Delta z \qquad (\gamma \approx \mathcal{O}(1) \text{ dimensionless})$$

*(Note: If a fourth-order discretization error scaling $\Delta z^4$ is preferred, $\gamma$ must be defined with explicit dimensions of $\text{length}^{-3}$. Scaling via $\lambda = \gamma \Delta z$ maintains true physical dimensional closure.)*

# L_fold occurs where d/dz(z / L(z)) = 0  => L - z*L' = 0
L_true = 15.0 + 0.2 * z + 25.0 * np.exp(-((z - 80.0) / 25.0) ** 2)
noise = np.random.normal(0, 0.5, size=z.shape)
L_noisy = L_true + noise

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. C^2 Smoothing Spline (Dimensionally Consistent \lambda = \gamma * dz)
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
gamma = 1.0
sigma = 0.5  # Noise variance estimate (m)
lambda_param = gamma * dz
# UnivariateSpline smoothing parameter s scales with N * sigma^2 / lambda
s_param = len(z) * (sigma**2) / lambda_param

spline = UnivariateSpline(z, L_noisy, k=4, s=s_param)
L_h = spline(z)
L_p = spline.derivative(1)(z)
L_pp = spline.derivative(2)(z)

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Geometry & Businger-Dyer Closure Derivatives (\beta = 5.0, Pr0 = 1.0)
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
beta = 5.0
Pr0 = 1.0
Ri_crit = 0.25
alpha = 2.0  # Relaxation gain control

zeta = z / L_h
zeta_z = (L_h - z * L_p) / (L_h**2)
zeta_zz = (-z * L_pp * L_h - 2.0 * L_p * (L_h - z * L_p)) / (L_h**3)

# Webb Profile (Pr0 = 1): R(zeta) = zeta / (1 + beta*zeta)
R_val = zeta / (1.0 + beta * zeta)
R_p = 1.0 / ((1.0 + beta * zeta) ** 2)
R_pp = -2.0 * beta / ((1.0 + beta * zeta) ** 3)

Ri_g_raw = R_val
Ri_g_zz = R_pp * (zeta_z**2) + R_p * zeta_zz

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Smooth Algebraic Limiter & Derivative Evaluation
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
epsilon = 0.05
C = 0.5 * np.abs(Ri_g_zz) * (dz**2) + epsilon * Ri_crit
x = np.abs(Ri_g_raw - Ri_crit)

# Smooth regularized Richardson number
Ri_g_reg = Ri_crit + (Ri_g_raw - Ri_crit) * ((x + C) / (x + (1.0 + alpha) * C))

# Analytical derivative d(Ri_g_reg) / d(Ri_g)
d_reg_d_raw = (x**2 + 2.0 * (1.0 + alpha) * C * x + (1.0 + alpha) * (C**2)) / (

print("--- NUMERICAL VERIFICATION OF SMOOTH LIMITER ---")

### Loop-Gain Damping Formalization ($\mathcal{G}$)

The regularized sensitivity derivative introduces a localized diagnostic damping factor $\mathcal{D}_\alpha(Ri_g) \in \left[\frac{1}{1 + \alpha}, \, 1\right)$:

$$\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}} \frac{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}}{\partial Ri_g} = \mathcal{K}_h'(Ri_g^{\text{reg}}) \cdot \mathcal{D}_\alpha(Ri_g)$$

The loop gain decomposes into physical state-dependent terms and the diagnostic regulator:

$$\mathcal{G} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial \theta_z}\right) \left(\frac{\partial \theta_z}{\partial H}\right) \left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial K_h}\right) \mathcal{K}_h'(Ri_g^{\text{reg}})}_{\mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}}} \cdot \mathcal{D}_\alpha(Ri_g)$$

* **Individual Factor Safety:** Because $\mathcal{D}_\alpha(Ri_g) < 1.0$ everywhere, the regularizer is strictly non-amplifying relative to unregularized pass-through diagnostic schemes ($\mathcal{D} \equiv 1.0$).
* **Threshold-Centered Protection:** Damping reaches its maximum factor of $\frac{1}{1 + \alpha}$ exactly at $Ri_g = Ri_{\text{crit}}$, precisely where $\vert{}\mathcal{K}_h'\vert{}$ peaks and $\mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}}$ threatens system decoupling.
* **Empirical Calibration:** Suppressing $\vert{}\mathcal{G}\vert{} < 1.0$ across all thermodynamic regimes requires selecting $\alpha \ge \max\left(1.0, \, \vert{}\mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}}\vert{} - 1\right)$ during model validation to cover the physical factors ($\rho, c_p, \Delta t, \Delta z$).

### Final Closed-Form Diagnostic Pipeline

For any grid cell $z_k$ with spacing $\Delta z$, $C^2$-reconstructed Obukhov derivatives $L_h, L_h', L_h''$, and raw diagnostic $Ri_g$:

$$\zeta = \frac{z_k}{L_h}, \qquad \zeta_z = \frac{L_h - z_k L_h'}{L_h^2}, \qquad \zeta_{zz} = \frac{-z_k L_h'' L_h - 2 L_h'(L_h - z_k L_h')}{L_h^3}$$

$$Ri_{g,zz} = \frac{-2\beta}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^3} \, \zeta_z^2 + \frac{1}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \, \zeta_{zz}$$

$$C = \frac{1}{2} \vert{}Ri_{g,zz}\vert{} (\Delta z)^2 + 0.05 Ri_{\text{crit}}$$

$$Ri_g^{\text{reg}} = Ri_{\text{crit}} + (Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}) \left[ \frac{\vert{}Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{} + C}{\vert{}Ri_g - Ri_{\text{crit}}\vert{} + (1 + \alpha) C} \right]$$

$$K_h = \mathcal{K}_h(Ri_g^{\text{reg}}), \qquad H = -\rho c_p K_h \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$$
