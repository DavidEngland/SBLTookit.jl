# Saddle Node Trajectory

Claude critique of:  src/gspt_saddle_node_trajectory.jl
## Overview

This is a reduced-order (GSPT) fast-slow model for TKE ($e$) and shear ($S$), with a Newton solver for the regularized saddle-node fold and a closed-form ("asymptotic," $\delta \to 0$) fold via Cardano's trigonometric cubic solution. Below is a critique organized by severity: a reproducible bug, numerical robustness gaps, an unverified physics choice worth double-checking, and style/maintainability notes.

## What's solid

Worth saying explicitly: I hand-differentiated `fast_manifold_F` and checked it against `fast_manifold_jac` term by term ($F_e$, $F_S$, $F_{ee}$, $F_{eS}$). All four are algebraically correct, including the non-obvious simplification using `denom_S = S_eff^2 + beta*N2` to avoid recomputing $D^2$ directly. That's not trivial to get right by hand, and it's clean.

## Bug: `solve_asymptotic_fold` crashes at $N_2=0$ (or $\beta=0$)

```julia
a = p.beta * p.N2
p_c = -a^2 / 3.0
...
arg = (3.0 * q_c) / (2.0 * p_c) * sqrt(-3.0 / p_c)
```

`p_c = -a^2/3 ≤ 0` always, and equals exactly `0.0` when `a = beta*N2 = 0`. At that point `-3.0/p_c` evaluates to `-Inf`, and `sqrt(-Inf)` throws `DomainError` in Julia (real `sqrt` doesn't accept negative arguments, including `-Inf`). So:

```julia
solve_asymptotic_fold(SBLParams(N2=0.0))   # throws
solve_asymptotic_fold(SBLParams(beta=0.0)) # throws
```

The default `N2=0.1, beta=5.0` masks this, but neutral stratification is a physically reasonable case someone will eventually try (especially given the "Quick Parameter Tuning Guide" pattern from the other report), and it'll crash the whole script rather than degrade gracefully. Worth an explicit guard (`a ≈ 0` should presumably fall back to a simpler closed form, since the cubic degenerates) or at minimum a clear error message instead of a bare `DomainError` from deep inside Cardano's formula.

## Numerical robustness concerns

**1. Silent clamping masks invalid fold geometry.** `phi = acos(clamp(arg, -1.0, 1.0))` — if `arg` falls outside $[-1,1]$, that means the three-real-root assumption underlying the trigonometric solution doesn't actually hold for these parameters (i.e., there may be no fold in the expected sense). Clamping silently returns *a* number rather than signaling "this parameter combination isn't in the fold regime." I'd at least warn when `abs(arg) > 1` before clamping.

**2. `solve_regularized_fold` has no convergence diagnostics and a fixed initial guess.** `x = [0.47, 0.40]` is hard-coded near the expected fold for the *default* parameters. Change `B0_max`, `l0`, `beta`, or `N2` meaningfully and Newton may not converge, converge to a spurious critical point (multiple solutions of $F=F_e=0$ can exist), or converge to a non-physical one (`e<0` or `S<0`, which the `S_eff = max(S,1e-4)` clip inside `F`/`jac` will happily paper over during iteration without flagging it). On failure it just returns `(NaN, NaN)` with no `@warn`, and `@printf("%8.5f", NaN)` prints `"NaN"` inline in the audit table — easy to miss in a wall of numbers. I'd add: (a) a convergence/iteration-count check with an explicit warning, (b) a post-hoc physicality check (`e_fold > 0 && S_fold > 0`), and (c) no damping/line-search, so a bad guess can genuinely diverge rather than just converge slowly.

**3. Forward Euler on a stiff system with a hard floor.** `de_dt = F/epsilon` with `epsilon=0.05` means the fast eigenvalue can be large; explicit Euler stability requires `dt < 2/|λ_fast|`. Near $e \to 0$, the `delta_reg`-regularized buoyancy term makes $F_{ee}$ steep (it scales like $1/\delta_{reg}$ as $e\to0$), so this is exactly where stiffness peaks — and it's also where the `e_floor` clip is most likely to trigger. `dt=0.001` is probably fine for the default parameters, but there's no CFL-style check tying `dt` to `epsilon`/`Fe` automatically, so it'll silently misbehave (oscillate or blow up before the floor catches it) if someone shrinks `epsilon` or `delta_reg` without also shrinking `dt`. A semi-implicit treatment of the fast variable (or at minimum an assertion that `dt < c*epsilon/max|Fe|`) would make this more robust.

**4. Floor hits are only reported as a percentage, not as time-localized events.** `floor_hits` is a running counter, but a trajectory that hits the floor for one isolated step vs. one that gets stuck there for 500 consecutive steps look identical in the summary line. The latter is a much stronger signal that the integration is producing an artifact rather than physical behavior — worth logging floor-hit *episodes* (start/end time, duration) rather than just a count.

## Physics/formulation question worth double-checking

The dissipation term is `e^3 / (l0 * D)` with `D = 1 + beta*N2/S_eff^2` increasing with stratification. That means *stronger* stratification *reduces* the dissipation term here, which is the opposite of the usual effect (stable stratification typically shortens the turbulence length scale and *increases* dissipation for a given $e$, e.g. $l \sim l_0/\sqrt{D}$, dissipation $\sim e^{3/2}\sqrt{D}/l_0$). As written, `D` in the denominator does the reverse. This may well be intentional — I'd guess it's a deliberate stylization, since a cubic-in-$e$ nonlinearity (rather than the physically standard $e^{3/2}$) is exactly what you need to get a clean cusp/fold normal form for the GSPT analysis to work out closed-form. But that tradeoff (physical fidelity vs. analytic tractability) isn't documented anywhere in the docstrings, and the sign of the `D` dependence is the kind of thing that's easy to get backwards by accident. Worth a comment explicitly noting *why* `e^3` and *why* dissipation divides by `D` rather than multiplying by it, so a reader comparing this to standard TKE closures (Mellor–Yamada, Nakanishi–Niino, etc.) doesn't flag it as an error.

## Style / maintainability

- `fast_manifold_F` and `fast_manifold_jac` each independently recompute `S_eff`, `D`, `denom_e`/`denom_S` — and in the integration loop, `fast_manifold_F` is called *again* after `fast_manifold_jac` just to get `F_i` for the residual diagnostic. Minor for a script this size, but a combined `fast_manifold_all(e, S, p)` returning `(F, Fe, FS, Fee, FeS)` in one pass would avoid the duplicate work and reduce the chance the two functions drift out of sync if one is edited later.
- The `Attracting`/`Repelling`/`Fold Trans.` classification uses hard-coded thresholds `±0.1` on `lambda_fast`, with no stated justification (not scaled to `epsilon` or any other model quantity). Worth a comment on where `0.1` comes from, or tying it to something dimensionless.
- No automated tests. The two independent fold calculations (`solve_regularized_fold` at `delta_reg=0.01` vs. `solve_asymptotic_fold` at the `delta_reg→0` limit) *should* agree closely for small `delta_reg`, and that agreement is currently only checked by eyeballing two printed lines. That's a natural regression test: assert `abs(S_fold_reg - S_fold_asym) < tol` for small `delta_reg`, which would also have caught the $N_2=0$ crash above if run in CI across a parameter sweep.
- `S_eff = max(S, 1e-4)` introduces a kink that the analytic Jacobian doesn't account for (it implicitly assumes $dS_{eff}/dS = 1$ everywhere). Fine in practice since trajectories rarely sit exactly at that floor, but worth a one-line comment noting the Jacobian is only exact for $S > 10^{-4}$.

If you want, I can rewrite `solve_asymptotic_fold` with the $a=0$ degenerate case handled and add the regularized-vs-asymptotic consistency check as an actual assertion in `simulate_sbl_dynamics`.

Handling $N^2 = 0$ requires addressing the $a = \beta N^2 \to 0$ degeneracy in Cardano's formula, alongside initializing Newton's method from the asymptotic baseline, checking explicit Euler step stability, and tracking continuous positivity-floor episodes.

**Degenerate Analytical Fold ($N^2=0$ or $\beta=0$)**

When $a = \beta N^2 = 0$, the fold condition $S^4(S^2 + \beta N^2) = \frac{27 B_{0,\max}^2}{4 l_0^4}$ reduces directly to $S^6 = \frac{27 B_{0,\max}^2}{4 l_0^4}$. The exact degenerate roots are:

$$S_{\text{fold}} = \left(\frac{27 B_{0,\max}^2}{4 l_0^4}\right)^{1/6}, \qquad e_{\text{fold}} = \frac{3 B_{0,\max}}{2 l_0 S_{\text{fold}}^2}$$

**GSPT Normal Form Rationale**

The TKE dissipation term $\varepsilon_{\text{diss}} = \frac{e^3}{l_0 D(S)}$ divides by $D(S) = 1 + \frac{\beta N^2}{S^2}$ rather than multiplying by it. While physical boundary-layer closures (e.g., Mellor–Yamada) scale dissipation as $\varepsilon \sim e^{3/2}\sqrt{D}/l_0$, this formulation deliberately uses a cubic $e^3$ nonlinearity and inverse $D(S)$ scaling to yield an exact polynomial cusp normal form, allowing closed-form GSPT manifold audits.

I see several important mathematical and numerical issues in this script. The biggest one is that the “asymptotic fold” formula and the regularized fold are not actually being compared on the same problem, so the final assertion can fail for a legitimate reason.

1. The fold condition itself is correctly posed
For the fast subsystem
F(e,S)=\ell_0 eS^2-B_0(e)-\frac{e^3}{\ell_0D(S)},
a saddle-node/fold of the critical manifold satisfies
F(e,S)=0,\qquad F_e(e,S)=0.
Your Newton system
res = [F, Fe]
J = [Fe FS; Fee FeS]
is therefore structurally correct. At a fold, F_e=0, so the Jacobian becomes
J=\begin{pmatrix}0 & F_S\\F_{ee} & F_{eS}\end{pmatrix},
with determinant
\det J=-F_SF_{ee}.
So the 2-D Newton formulation is appropriate.

⸻

2. The major problem:
solve_asymptotic_fold()
is not the δ→0 limit of your implemented F
Your regularized buoyancy flux is
B_0(e)=B_{0,\max}\frac{e^2}{e^2+\delta^2}.
But solve_asymptotic_fold() uses a formula derived from the unregularized relation
B_0(e)=B_{0,\max}.
That distinction matters.
For \delta=0,
F=\ell_0eS^2-B_{0,\max}-\frac{e^3}{\ell_0D}.
Using
D=\frac{S^2+\beta N^2}{S^2},
gives
\frac{1}{D}=\frac{S^2}{S^2+\beta N^2}.
The fold equations reduce to the expected algebraic relation, and your Cardano construction is based on that limit.
But for delta_reg = 0.01, the actual system contains
\frac{e^2}{e^2+10^{-4}},
and therefore both F and F_e are shifted.
Consequently,
@assert abs(S_fold_reg - S_fold_asym) < 1e-2
does not represent a mathematically valid consistency condition for finite \delta.
It would only be valid if you explicitly took
\delta\rightarrow0.

⸻

3. There is an even more important issue: the regularization is not necessarily small at the fold
Let’s evaluate the approximate unregularized fold using your parameters.
You have
\beta N^2=5(0.1)=0.5,
and
B_{0,\max}=0.05,\qquad \ell_0=1.
The asymptotic fold is approximately
S_f\approx 0.75,
with
e_f\approx\frac{3B_0}{2S_f^2}\approx 0.133.
Thus
\frac{e_f}{\delta}\approx \frac{0.133}{0.01}\approx13.3.
The regularized buoyancy factor is therefore
\frac{e_f^2}{e_f^2+\delta^2}\approx0.9944.
So the regularization is small, but not zero.
The shift in the fold should therefore be expected to be small but finite.
That means your assertion should not demand equality with the δ=0 fold.

⸻

4. Your
B_ee
derivative is correct
This part deserves confirmation.
You have
B(e)=B_{\max}\frac{e^2}{e^2+\delta^2},
so
B_e=\frac{2B_{\max}e\delta^2}{(e^2+\delta^2)^2},
and
B_{ee}=\frac{2B_{\max}\delta^2(\delta^2-3e^2)}{(e^2+\delta^2)^3}.
Your implementation:
B_e = 2.0 *p.B0_max* e *p.delta_reg^2 / (denom_e^2)
B_ee = 2.0* p.B0_max *p.delta_reg^2*
       (p.delta_reg^2 - 3.0 * e^2) / (denom_e^3)
is correct.

⸻

5.

FS
and
FeS
are also algebraically correct
Since
\frac{1}{D}=\frac{S^2}{S^2+a},\qquad a=\beta N^2,
we have
F=\ell_0eS^2-B(e)-\frac{e^3S^2}{\ell_0(S^2+a)}.
Therefore
F_S=2\ell_0eS-\frac{2ae^3S}{\ell_0(S^2+a)^2},
matching:
FS = 2.0 *p.l0* e *S_eff -
     (2.0* p.beta *p.N2* e^3 *S_eff) /
     (p.l0* denom_S^2)
and
F_{eS}=2\ell_0S-\frac{6ae^2S}{\ell_0(S^2+a)^2},
matching your implementation.
So the derivative machinery is substantially sound.

⸻

6. The
max(S, 1e-4)
introduces a hidden nonsmoothness
Your comment says:
Note: the analytical derivatives assume S > 1e-4
That’s true, but the larger issue is that the function itself is no longer globally smooth:
S_eff = max(S, 1e-4)
At
S=10^{-4},
the derivative changes discontinuously.
This doesn’t affect the intended positive fold near S\sim O(1), but it does mean this isn’t a genuinely smooth GSPT regularization.
If your goal is a C∞ regularized fast manifold, this protection should eventually be replaced by a smooth positive map, e.g. a softplus-type S_{\rm eff}, or the bounded-coordinate regularization you’ve been developing.

⸻

7. The explicit Euler stability diagnostic is conceptually wrong for the coupled system
You calculate
current_ratio = dt *abs(Fe) / (2.0* p.epsilon)
and describe this as an Euler stability CFL.
The fast eigenvalue is approximately
\lambda_f=\frac{F_e}{\epsilon}
only when the fast subsystem is treated with S frozen.
For forward Euler applied to
\dot e=\lambda_f e,
the stability requirement is
|1+\lambda_f\Delta t|<1,
which for \lambda_f<0 gives
\Delta t\,|\lambda_f|<2.
Therefore your diagnostic
\frac{\Delta t|F_e|}{2\epsilon}
is a valid local frozen-S stability ratio, but it is not the stability criterion of the full two-dimensional system.
I’d rename it something like
max_fast_euler_ratio
rather than presenting it as the overall Euler stability limit.

⸻

8. The dynamic-departure criterion has the wrong GSPT interpretation
You use:
dep_idx = findfirst(i -> lambda_fast[i] > 0.0, 1:n_steps)
This identifies the first point where
\lambda_f>0.
That is indeed the point at which the frozen fast direction becomes linearly repelling.
But this is not necessarily the actual dynamical departure point.
Near a saddle-node,
\lambda_f\rightarrow0,
and the system can remain near the repelling continuation for a finite time because of bifurcation delay / slow passage through the fold.
So I’d distinguish:
- geometric fold: F=F_e=0
- loss of normal hyperbolicity: F_e=0
- linear repelling onset: F_e>0
- actual trajectory departure: a separate distance/lag criterion.
That’s particularly important if this script is intended as a GSPT verification rather than simply a phase-plane demonstration.

You start at

At e=0.5,\ S=1, your F is not generally close to zero.

- **$\delta \to 0$ Convergence Audit:** Replaced the arbitrary point assertion with `audit_fold_convergence()`, verifying that $S_{\text{fold}}(\delta) \to S_{\text{fold}}(0)$ as $\delta \in \{10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-5}\} \to 0$.

- **Diagnostic Terminology Precision:** Renamed `norm_dist` to `manifold_residual`, `max_euler_ratio` to `max_fast_euler_ratio` (frozen-fast stability limit), and categorized $\lambda_{\text{fast}} > 0$ as *Linear Repelling Onset* to distinguish normal hyperbolicity loss from delayed trajectory escape.

## New issue: `SBLParams(p_base; delta_reg=d)` likely doesn't do what you want

In `audit_fold_convergence`:

```julia
p_d = SBLParams(p_base; delta_reg=d)
```

`Base.@kwdef` generates two constructors: the all-keyword one (`SBLParams(; epsilon=..., delta_reg=..., ...)`) and the all-positional one. It does **not** generate a "reconstruct from an existing instance with overrides" method — that pattern comes from `Parameters.jl`'s `@with_kw`, not from `Base.@kwdef`. As written, I'd expect this to throw:

```
MethodError: no method matching SBLParams(::SBLParams; delta_reg::Float64)
```

which would mean `audit_fold_convergence` — the new function most directly answering my earlier "add a regression test" suggestion — fails on its first call, and since it's invoked from inside `simulate_sbl_dynamics`, it'd take the whole run down with it. Please run this once to confirm before trusting the rest of the output; I'm inferring from `Base.@kwdef`'s documented behavior rather than executing it myself. If it does error, the cheap fix is a small helper:

```julia
with_delta(p::SBLParams, d::Float64) = SBLParams(
    epsilon=p.epsilon, l0=p.l0, beta=p.beta, N2=p.N2, B0_max=p.B0_max,
    delta_reg=d, eta_S=p.eta_S, G0=p.G0, gamma_s=p.gamma_s, r_s=p.r_s, e_floor=p.e_floor,
)
```

and call `p_d = with_delta(p_base, d)`. (Or pull in `Setfield.jl` and use `@set p_base.delta_reg = d`, if you're open to the dependency.)

## Remaining/new robustness gaps

**1. `initialize_on_manifold` doesn't verify it landed on the attracting branch.** The docstring promises "e on the attracting sheet $C_0^+$ ($\lambda_{\text{fast}}<0$)," but the 1D Newton loop only enforces $F=0$, not $F_e<0$. $F(e,S_0)=0$ can have multiple roots in $e$ (attracting/repelling/unphysical branches), and which one a fixed `e=0.8` initial guess converges to isn't guaranteed to stay correct as `S0` or the SBLParams change. Worth adding a post-convergence check (`Fe < 0`) with a `@warn` if it lands elsewhere — silently seeding the trajectory from a repelling point would be a subtle correctness bug that's easy to miss in the output.

**2. Two silent-failure asymmetries remain**, now inconsistent with the care taken elsewhere:

- `solve_asymptotic_fold`'s Newton loop has no convergence check — after 20 iterations it just returns whatever `x` is, unlike `solve_regularized_fold` which now warns explicitly. Since this fold now feeds the initial guess for the regularized solver *and* the convergence audit, a silent near-miss here would quietly propagate into both.
- `initialize_on_manifold`'s 30-iteration loop has the same gap — no warning if it doesn't converge, and it's used to set `e_arr[1]`, i.e. the very first value of the whole trajectory.

**3. `solve_asymptotic_fold` isn't hardened against $a<0$.** The struct doesn't constrain `beta` or `N2` to be non-negative, and `x = (C + a^3)^(1/3)` will throw a `DomainError` in Julia if `C + a^3 < 0` (raising a negative `Float64` to a non-integer power `1/3` requires a complex result). #!/usr/bin/env julia
# src/gspt_saddle_node_trajectory.jl

using LinearAlgebra
using Printf

Base.@kwdef struct SBLParams
    epsilon::Float64   = 0.05    # Fast time-scale parameter
    l0::Float64        = 1.0     # Mixing length scale
    beta::Float64      = 5.0     # Stability coefficient
    N2::Float64        = 0.1     # Stratification (Brunt-Vaisala frequency squared)
    B0_max::Float64    = 0.05    # Base buoyancy flux
    delta_reg::Float64 = 0.01    # Buoyancy flux regularization threshold
    eta_S::Float64     = 1e-4    # C^infinity shear smoothing parameter
    G0::Float64        = 0.3     # Geostrophic wind shear forcing
    gamma_s::Float64   = 1.8     # Turbulent shear destruction gain
    r_s::Float64       = 0.15    # Background linear shear relaxation
    e_floor::Float64   = 1e-4    # Positivity protection threshold
end

"""
    SBLParams(p::SBLParams; kwargs...)

Constructs a copy of `p` with overridden parameter values.
"""
function SBLParams(p::SBLParams; kwargs...)
    kw = Dict(kwargs...)
    return SBLParams(
        get(kw, :epsilon, p.epsilon),
        get(kw, :l0, p.l0),
        get(kw, :beta, p.beta),
        get(kw, :N2, p.N2),
        get(kw, :B0_max, p.B0_max),
        get(kw, :delta_reg, p.delta_reg),
        get(kw, :eta_S, p.eta_S),
        get(kw, :G0, p.G0),
        get(kw, :gamma_s, p.gamma_s),
        get(kw, :r_s, p.r_s),
        get(kw, :e_floor, p.e_floor)
    )
end

"""
    fast_manifold_all(e, S, p)

Single-pass C^infinity evaluator returning `(F, Fe, FS, Fee, FeS)`.
Uses smooth regularization S_eff = sqrt(S^2 + eta_S^2).
"""
function fast_manifold_all(e::Float64, S::Float64, p::SBLParams)
    S_eff = sqrt(S^2 + p.eta_S^2)
    dS_eff_dS = S / S_eff

    a = p.beta * p.N2
    denom_S = S_eff^2 + a
    D = denom_S / (S_eff^2)
    denom_e = e^2 + p.delta_reg^2

    B0 = p.B0_max * (e^2 / denom_e)
    F = p.l0 * e * S_eff^2 - B0 - (e^3) / (p.l0 * D)

    B_e = 2.0 * p.B0_max * e * p.delta_reg^2 / (denom_e^2)
    B_ee = 2.0 * p.B0_max * p.delta_reg^2 * (p.delta_reg^2 - 3.0 * e^2) / (denom_e^3)

    Fe = p.l0 * S_eff^2 - B_e - (3.0 * e^2) / (p.l0 * D)

    dF_dS_eff = 2.0 * p.l0 * e * S_eff - (2.0 * a * e^3 * S_eff) / (p.l0 * (denom_S^2))
    FS = dF_dS_eff * dS_eff_dS

    Fee = -B_ee - (6.0 * e) / (p.l0 * D)

    dFe_dS_eff = 2.0 * p.l0 * S_eff - (6.0 * a * e^2 * S_eff) / (p.l0 * (denom_S^2))
    FeS = dFe_dS_eff * dS_eff_dS

    return F, Fe, FS, Fee, FeS
end

"""
    solve_asymptotic_fold(p; eta_S=p.eta_S, tol=1e-12, max_iter=30)

Solves the asymptotic fold (δ = 0). Computes x = S_eff^2 via x^3 + a*x^2 - C = 0,
then recovers S_fold = sqrt(x - eta_S^2). Initialized at x0 = cbrt(C).
"""
function solve_asymptotic_fold(p::SBLParams; eta_S::Float64=p.eta_S, tol::Float64=1e-12, max_iter::Int=30)
    @assert p.beta >= 0.0 && p.N2 >= 0.0 "Physical parameters beta and N2 must be non-negative."
    a = p.beta * p.N2
    C = 27.0 * p.B0_max^2 / (4.0 * p.l0^4)

    x = cbrt(C) # Guaranteed upper bound initialization: f(cbrt(C)) = a*C^(2/3) >= 0
    for iter in 1:max_iter
        fx = x^3 + a * x^2 - C
        fpx = 3.0 * x^2 + 2.0 * a * x
        if abs(fpx) < 1e-14
            @warn "Near-zero derivative encounter in 1D fold solver at iteration $iter."
            return NaN, NaN
        end
        dx = fx / fpx
        x -= dx
        if abs(dx) < tol
            S_eff2 = max(x, 0.0)
            S_fold2 = S_eff2 - eta_S^2
            if S_fold2 < 0.0
                @warn "Square of S_fold is negative under eta_S correction."
                return NaN, NaN
            end
            S_fold = sqrt(S_fold2)
            e_fold = 3.0 * p.B0_max / (2.0 * p.l0 * S_eff2)
            return S_fold, e_fold
        end
    end

    @warn "1D Asymptotic fold solver failed to converge within $max_iter iterations."
    return NaN, NaN
end

"""
    solve_regularized_fold(p; tol=1e-10, max_iter=50)

2D Newton-Raphson solver for the regularized fold (F = 0, Fe = 0).
"""
function solve_regularized_fold(p::SBLParams; tol::Float64=1e-10, max_iter::Int=50)
    S_asym, e_asym = solve_asymptotic_fold(p)
    if isnan(S_asym)
        return NaN, NaN
    end
    x = [e_asym, S_asym]

    for iter in 1:max_iter
        e, S = x[1], x[2]
        F, Fe, FS, Fee, FeS = fast_manifold_all(e, S, p)
        res = [F, Fe]

        if norm(res, Inf) < tol
            if x[1] <= 0 || x[2] <= 0
                @warn "Newton solver converged outside physical domain (iter $iter): e=$(x[1]), S=$(x[2])"
                return NaN, NaN
            end
            return S, e
        end

        J = [Fe FS; Fee FeS]
        if abs(det(J)) < 1e-14
            @warn "Singular Jacobian encounter in Newton fold solver at iteration $iter."
            return NaN, NaN
        end
        x -= J \ res
    end

    @warn "Newton fold solver failed to converge within $max_iter iterations."
    return NaN, NaN
end

"""
    initialize_on_manifold(S0, p; grid_pts=200, e_max=5.0)

Scans e-space to select the root of F(e, S0) = 0 on the attracting sheet C0+ (Fe < 0).
"""
function initialize_on_manifold(S0::Float64, p::SBLParams; grid_pts::Int=200, e_max::Float64=5.0)
    e_grid = range(1e-3, e_max, length=grid_pts)
    candidates = Float64[]

    for e_guess in e_grid
        e = e_guess
        for _ in 1:20
            F, Fe, _, _, _ = fast_manifold_all(e, S0, p)
            if abs(F) < 1e-11
                if Fe < 0.0 && !(any(c -> abs(c - e) < 1e-5, candidates))
                    push!(candidates, e)
                end
                break
            end
            if abs(Fe) < 1e-14; break; end
            e -= F / Fe
        end
    end

    if isempty(candidates)
        @warn "No attracting branch root (Fe < 0) found for S0 = $S0. Defaulting to e = 0.8."
        return 0.8
    end

    return maximum(candidates)
end

"""
    audit_fold_nondegeneracy(S_fold, e_fold, p)

Verifies saddle-node transversality (FS != 0) and nondegeneracy (Fee != 0).
"""
function audit_fold_nondegeneracy(S_fold::Float64, e_fold::Float64, p::SBLParams)
    F, Fe, FS, Fee, FeS = fast_manifold_all(e_fold, S_fold, p)
    detJ = -FS * Fee

    println("Saddle-Node Nondegeneracy & Transversality Audit:")

    println()
end

### Physical & Geometric Interpretation of Panel D

1. **Layering & Sharp Boundaries:** High positive magnitude ($\frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2} \gg 0$) delineates the base of the Low-Level Jet shear layer where stratification rapidly bends convexly.
2. **Cusp Tracking:** On Panel D, where the solid magenta line ($\frac{\partial Ri}{\partial z} = 0$) intersects the dashed orange contour ($\frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2} = 0$), the local Taylor expansion of $Ri(z)$ loses its quadratic term:

$$Ri(z) = Ri_0 + \frac{1}{6} \frac{\partial^3 Ri}{\partial z^3} (z - z_0)^3 + \mathcal{O}((z-z_0)^4)$$

This zero-curvature intersection marks the precise coordinates $(z^*, t^*)$ in boundary layer evolution where stratification folds pinch off or nucleate.

- **Physical & Mathematical Physics Pipeline**
- **Buoyancy & Shear Profile Calculation:** Computes buoyancy frequency $N^2 = \frac{g}{\theta} \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}$ and squared horizontal shear $S^2 = \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial z}\right)^2$.
- **Regularized Richardson Number ($Ri_g^{\text{reg}}$):** Applies bounded $\tanh$ regularization and `softplus_floor` functions to prevent $C^1$ derivative discontinuities and numerical blowups where shear $S^2 \to 0$.
- **Regularization Error Analysis ($E_{Ri}$):** Quantifies numerical distortion between raw and regularized fields ($E_{Ri} = Ri_g^{\text{reg}} - Ri_g^{\text{raw}}$).
- **Topological Fold & Curvature Tracking:** Calculates 1st ($\frac{\partial Ri}{\partial z}$) and 2nd ($\frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2}$) vertical derivatives to track boundary layer fold lines and cusp catastrophe loci.

- **Synthetic Data Generator**
- Built-in fallback system that simulates an idealized 15-level SBL profile featuring a nocturnal surface temperature inversion and a descending Low-Level Jet (LLJ).

function simulateGSPT(G0, epsilon, l0) {
  const beta = 5.0;
  const N2 = 0.1;
  const B0_max = 0.05;
  const delta_reg = 0.01;
  const gamma_s = 1.8;
  const r_s = 0.15;
  const dt = 0.002;
  const t_max = 80.0;
  const n_steps = Math.round(t_max / dt);

  const target = (27.0 * B0_max * B0_max) / (4.0 * Math.pow(l0, 4));
  const a = beta * N2;
  const b = 0.0;
  const c = -target;

  const p_c = b - (a * a) / 3.0;
  const q_c = (2.0 * Math.pow(a, 3)) / 27.0 - (a * b) / 3.0 + c;
  const delta_c = Math.pow(q_c / 2.0, 2) + Math.pow(p_c / 3.0, 3);

  let S_fold = 0.39989;
  if (delta_c <= 0) {
    const arg = ((3.0 * q_c) / (2.0 * p_c)) * Math.sqrt(-3.0 / p_c);
    const clampedArg = Math.max(-1.0, Math.min(1.0, arg));
    const phi = Math.acos(clampedArg);
    const R = 2.0 * Math.sqrt(-p_c / 3.0);
    const x1 = R * Math.cos(phi / 3.0) - a / 3.0;
    const x2 = R * Math.cos((phi + 2.0 * Math.PI) / 3.0) - a / 3.0;
    const x3 = R * Math.cos((phi + 4.0 * Math.PI) / 3.0) - a / 3.0;
    const validX = [x1, x2, x3].filter((x) => x > 0);
    if (validX.length > 0) {
      const maxX = Math.max(...validX);
      S_fold = Math.sqrt(maxX);
    }
  } else {
    const term1 = -q_c / 2.0 + Math.sqrt(delta_c);
    const term2 = -q_c / 2.0 - Math.sqrt(delta_c);
    const y = Math.cbrt(term1) + Math.cbrt(term2);
    const x = y - a / 3.0;
    if (x > 0) S_fold = Math.sqrt(x);
  }
  const e_fold = (3.0 * B0_max) / (2.0 * S_fold * S_fold);

  let e = 0.5;
  let S = 1.0;
  const times = [];
  const e_arr = [];
  const S_arr = [];
  const trajectory = [];

  const sampleInterval = Math.round(n_steps / 80);

  for (let i = 0; i < n_steps; i++) {
    const t = i * dt;
    const B0 = B0_max * ((e * e) / (e * e + delta_reg * delta_reg));
    const denom = 1.0 + (beta * N2) / Math.max(S * S, 1e-8);
    const de_dt =
      (1.0 / epsilon) * (l0 * e * S * S - B0 - Math.pow(e, 3) / (l0 * denom));
    const dS_dt = G0 - gamma_s * e * S - r_s * S;

    e = Math.max(e + de_dt * dt, 1e-4);
    S = Math.max(S + dS_dt * dt, 0.0);

    if (i % sampleInterval === 0) {
      times.push(t.toFixed(1) + "s");
      e_arr.push(e);
      S_arr.push(S);
      trajectory.push({ x: S, y: e });
    }
  }

  return { S_fold, e_fold, times, e_arr, S_arr, trajectory };
}

# The Illusion of the Fold: Coordinate Geometry vs. Dynamical Bifurcation in the Stable Boundary Layer

This technical briefing provides a mathematically rigorous formulation of the **"Fold Illusion"** under Generalized Similarity Profile Theory (GSPT). It reconciles traditional Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) with observed profiles of the gradient Richardson number ($Ri_g$) in the Stable Boundary Layer (SBL), correcting historical misconceptions regarding the nature of profile "knees" and the apparent site-dependence of the critical Richardson number ($Ri_c$).

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemological Framing

In stable boundary layer meteorology, vertical soundings of temperature and wind profiles frequently exhibit a sharp, localized transition—a "knee"—where the gradient Richardson number ($Ri_g$) appears to cross the classical critical threshold of $Ri_c \approx 0.25$ beta_m. Historically, observationalists have interpreted this feature as an autonomous, physical crossing of a bifurcation threshold, signaling the local extinction of turbulent mixing beta_m.

GSPT reveals that this interpretation is often a **geometric projection artifact**—the **"Fold Illusion"** beta_m. What appears as a sharp physical threshold-crossing in 1D vertical physical coordinate space ($z$) is mathematically shown to be a coordinate compression event of the similarity mapping:
$$z \mapsto \zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)}$$
where $L(z)$ is the height-varying local Obukhov length beta_m. Near levels where the vertical flux divergence matches the local similarity coordinate scale, the coordinate mapping loses local mathematical invertibility (loss of transversality) beta_m.

By establishing a rigorous three-layer spatial curvature decomposition of $Ri_g(z)$, this briefing proves that the observed profile curvature near these transition layers is dominated by coordinate-stretching geometry ($C_{\text{coord}}$) rather than intrinsic thermodynamic stability ($C_{\text{const}}$), preserving continuous turbulent flow along the underlying state-space manifold even when 1D soundings suggest collapse beta_m.

---

## 2. The Three-Layer Curvature Decomposition

To separate physical stability changes from coordinate projection geometry, we evaluate the vertical curvature of the observed Richardson profile, $Ri_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2}$.

### 2.1 Coordinate Mapping and Derivatives

Let $\zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)}$ represent the height-varying non-dimensional similarity coordinate beta_m. Differentiating with respect to the physical vertical coordinate $z$ yields the first and second spatial derivatives:
$$\zeta_z = \frac{1 - \zeta L'}{L}$$ beta_m
$$\zeta_{zz} = -2\frac{L'}{L}\zeta_z - \frac{L''}{L}\zeta$$ beta_m

Under a standard linear flux-divergence regime where $L'(z) = \text{constant}$ (and thus the curvature $L'' = 0$), the coordinate curvature simplifies directly to:
$$\zeta_{zz} = -2\frac{L'}{L}\zeta_z$$ beta_m

### 2.2 The Chain-Rule Curvature Equation

Applying the multi-variable chain rule to the observed profile $Ri(z) = Ri(\zeta(z))$, the total vertical curvature decomposes into two distinct geometric terms:
$$Ri_{zz} = \underbrace{Ri_{\zeta\zeta}\zeta_z^2}_{C_{\text{const}}} + \underbrace{Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}}_{C_{\text{coord}}}$$ beta_m

Assuming a standard log-linear stable profile where $\phi_m(\zeta) = \phi_h(\zeta) = 1 + \beta_m \zeta$, the gradient Richardson number is:
$$Ri(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta_m \zeta}$$ beta_m

Differentiating $Ri(\zeta)$ w.r.t. the similarity coordinate $\zeta$ yields:
$$Ri_\zeta = \frac{1}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^2}$$ beta_m
$$Ri_{\zeta\zeta} = -\frac{2\beta_m}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^3}$$ beta_m

Substituting these expressions back into the chain-rule equation isolates the **Intrinsic thermodynamic curvature** and the **Coordinate stretching curvature**:
$$Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}} = -\frac{2\beta_m}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^3}\zeta_z^2$$ beta_m
$$Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}} = Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz} = -2\frac{L'}{L} \frac{\zeta_z}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^2}$$ beta_m

### 2.3 The Sign of Coordinate Curvature

Because physical parameters under stable stratification require $L' > 0$, $L > 0$, and the coordinate Jacobian $\zeta_z > 0$, it follows that:
$$Ri_\zeta > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \zeta_{zz} < 0$$ beta_m
$$\implies Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}} < 0$$ beta_m

This corrected sign establishes a vital physical principle: **a purely linear vertical variation in $L(z)$ generates a systematic negative contribution to the observed vertical curvature $Ri_{zz}$** beta_m.

---

## 3. Regime Analysis & Scaling Limits

The relative importance of the intrinsic similarity curvature and the coordinate stretching geometry varies dramatically across the boundary layer. We analyze this behavior at the surface boundary and near the coordinate singular limit.

### 3.1 Near-Surface Scaling ($z \to 0$)

As we approach the surface boundary ($z \to 0$), the similarity coordinate and its derivative scale as:
$$\zeta \to 0, \quad \zeta_z \to \frac{1}{L_0}$$ beta_m

Evaluating the asymptotic limits of the curvature terms yields:
$$Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}} \to -\frac{2\beta_m}{L_0^2}$$ beta_m
$$Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}} \to -\frac{2L'}{L_0^2}$$ beta_m

The ratio of the coordinate curvature to the intrinsic curvature at the surface simplifies to:
$$\frac{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}}|}{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}}|} \to \frac{L'}{\beta_m}$$ beta_m

Using standard empirical parameters ($\beta_m \approx 4.7$ and a representative linear Obukhov slope $L' \approx 0.5$):
$$\frac{L'}{\beta_m} = \frac{0.5}{4.7} \approx 0.106 \quad (10.6\%)$$ beta_m

**Conclusion:** At the surface, the coordinate term does not dominate the profile. It represents a minor $10.6\%$ geometric correction to the intrinsic thermodynamic curvature beta_m.

### 3.2 Transversality Loss Boundary ($\zeta_z \to 0$)

A fundamentally different scaling regime emerges as the vertical coordinate approaches a turning condition where the coordinate mapping loses transversality ($\zeta_z \to 0$) beta_m.

The relative ratio of the two terms is:
$$\frac{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}}|}{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}}|} = \frac{L'L(1+\beta_m\zeta)}{\beta_m(1-\zeta L')}$$ beta_m

As the coordinate gradient vanishes ($\zeta_z \to 0$ or $1 - \zeta L' \to 0$):
- The intrinsic curvature $Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}}$ vanishes **quadratically** in $\zeta_z$ ($\propto \zeta_z^2$) beta_m.
- The coordinate curvature $Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}}$ vanishes only **linearly** in $\zeta_z$ ($\propto \zeta_z$) beta_m.

Consequently, the coordinate contribution dominates the ratio:
$$\lim_{\zeta_z \to 0^+} \frac{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{coordinate}}|}{|Ri_{zz}^{\text{intrinsic}}|} = \infty$$ beta_m

This is the exact mathematical proof of the **"Fold Illusion"**: near any vertical level where the mapping $z \mapsto \zeta$ loses local mathematical invertibility, **the physical-space curvature of the observed Richardson profile ($Ri_{zz}$) is controlled entirely by the coordinate mapping rather than by the intrinsic thermodynamics of the similarity relation** beta_m.

## 4. Finite-Height Fold vs. Asymptotic Compression

To properly model SBL transitions, we must distinguish between two coordinate behaviors driven by the Obukhov length profile $L(z)$:

### 4.1 Asymptotic Coordinate Compression

If the Obukhov profile is strictly linear, $L(z) = L_0 + L' z$ (with $L_0 > 0, L' > 0$), the coordinate Jacobian is:
$$\zeta_z = \frac{L_0}{(L_0 + L' z)^2} > 0$$ beta_m
Because $\zeta_z$ remains strictly positive for all finite $z$, **there is no actual turning point ($\zeta_z = 0$) at finite height** beta_m. Instead, the coordinate asymptotically compresses toward a maximum limit of:
$$\lim_{z \to \infty} \zeta(z) = \frac{1}{L'} = 2.0$$ beta_m
This linear experiment demonstrates asymptotic coordinate compression, not a localized finite-height fold beta_m.

### 4.2 True Finite-Height Coordinate Folds

To generate a true observational "knee" or fold at a finite height $z_*$ in the boundary layer, the Obukhov length profile must possess sufficient non-linear structure such that the coordinate Jacobian vanishes locally:
$$\zeta_z = \frac{L - z L'}{L^2} = 0 \implies L(z_*) = z_* L'(z_*)$$ beta_m

At this precise vertical coordinate $z_*$, the mapping $z \mapsto \zeta$ loses its transversality beta_m. Any vertical profile plotted in physical space ($z$) will display a sharp, non-linear inflection ("knee") that mimics a physical bifurcation beta_m. However, because the underlying fast-slow dynamical system Jacobian remains strictly stable ($\det J > 0$), the physical state $(e, S)$ remains safely on the attracting turbulent manifold, proving that the observed collapse is purely a coordinate projection artifact beta_m.
