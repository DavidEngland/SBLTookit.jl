### 3.1 State Variables and Governing Fast-Slow Equations

Consider a reduced 2D phase-space governing nocturnal turbulence interaction:

* **Fast Variable ($e$):** Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE, $e \sim \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$), evolving on timescales of seconds to minutes ($\epsilon \sim 0.01 - 0.05$).
* **Slow Variable ($S$):** Vertical Mean Shear ($S = |\partial \mathbf{u}/\partial z| \sim \text{s}^{-1}$), evolving under geostrophic forcing $G_0$ and frictional drag on timescales of hours.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon \frac{de}{dt} &= F(e, S) = l_0 e S^2 - B_0(e) - \frac{e^{3/2}}{l_0 D(S)} \quad &\text{(Fast TKE Balance)} \\
\frac{dS}{dt} &= G_0 - (\gamma_s e + r_s) S \quad &\text{(Slow Shear Adjustment)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Where:
* $l_0 S^2 e$: Mechanical Shear Production of TKE.
* $B_0(e)$: Buoyancy flux damping under stable stratification, regularized by baseline threshold $\delta_{\text{reg}}$:
  $$B_0(e) = B_{0,\text{max}} \left( \frac{e^2}{e^2 + \delta_{\text{reg}}^2} \right)$$
* $\frac{e^{3/2}}{l_0 D(S)}$: Kolmogorov viscous dissipation rate ($\epsilon_{\text{TKE}}$), modified by stratification factor $D(S) = 1 + \frac{\beta N^2}{S^2}$.
* $G_0$: Large-scale geostrophic pressure gradient forcing ($G_0 \sim \text{s}^{-2}$).
* $\gamma_s e S$: Turbulent momentum extraction eroding the mean shear profile.

### 3.2 Singular Fold Inversion and Saddle-Node Thresholds

The fast manifold nullcline $F(e,S) = 0$ forms a characteristic S-shaped (or cubic) curve in $(S, e)$ phase space. Setting $\partial F / \partial e = 0$ isolates the critical fold boundary $(S_{\text{fold}}, e_{\text{fold}})$:

1. **Upper Attracting Branch ($e > e_{\text{fold}}$):** $\partial F / \partial e < 0$. The boundary layer is fully turbulent, well-mixed, and dynamically stable.
2. **Lower Attracting Branch ($e \approx 0$):** $\partial F / \partial e < 0$. The boundary layer is laminar, radiatively decoupled from the surface, and prone to extreme nocturnal cooling.
3. **Fold Boundary ($S_{\text{fold}}$):** If radiative cooling or frictional drag reduces vertical shear below $S_{\text{fold}}$, normal hyperbolicity is destroyed ($\lambda_{\text{fast}} > 0$). The trajectory drops catastrophically from the turbulent branch to the laminar attractor.

#### Analytical Cubic Fold Expression
In the asymptotic limit ($\delta_{\text{reg}} \to 0$), the fold condition satisfies a depressed cubic equation for $x = S^2$:

$$
x^3 + (\beta N^2) x^2 - C_0 = 0, \quad \text{where } C_0 = \frac{27 B_{0,\text{max}}^2}{4 l_0^4}
$$

The largest real root yields the critical shear boundary $S_{\text{fold}} = \sqrt{x_{\text{max}}}$, establishing a mathematically rigorous threshold for turbulence collapse.

## 4. Parameterization and the Turbulence Closure Problem

### 4.1 The Closure Problem in Atmospheric Fluid Dynamics

Applying Reynolds Averaging to the Navier-Stokes equations splits velocity and thermodynamic variables into mean ($\overline{u_i}, \overline{\theta}$) and fluctuating ($u_i', \theta'$) components:

$$
u_i = \overline{u_i} + u_i', \quad \theta = \overline{\theta} + \theta'
$$

The resulting Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) momentum and heat conservation equations contain unknown second-moment correlations:

$$
\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial t} + \overline{u_j} \frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{1}{\rho_0} \frac{\partial \overline{p}}{\partial x_i} - g_i \beta_v \overline{\theta_v} - \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \left( \overline{u_i' u_j'} \right)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial \overline{\theta}}{\partial t} + \overline{u_j} \frac{\partial \overline{\theta}}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \left( \overline{u_j' \theta'} \right)
$$

The terms $\overline{u_i' u_j'}$ (Reynolds Stresses) and $\overline{u_j' \theta'}$ (Turbulent Heat Fluxes) represent unresolved momentum and scalar transport by subgrid-scale eddies. Because the governing equations are non-linear, deriving conservation equations for $n$-th order moments introduces $(n+1)$-th order unknowns. **Turbulence Parameterization (Closure)** is the formal mathematical procedure used to express unresolved higher-order fluxes in terms of resolved grid-scale mean fields.

---

### 4.2 Taxonomy of Parameterization Schemes

| Scheme Level | Closure Type | Primary Formulations | Strengths & Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First-Order (1.0)** | Local Gradient Transport | $K$-theory, Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST), Richardson-dependent $K_m(Ri)$ | Computational efficiency; fails in convective boundary layers and collapses prematurely in strongly stable regimes. |
| **1.5-Order (TKE)** | Prognostic Equation for TKE ($e$) | Mellor-Yamada Level 2.5, BouGeault-Lacarrère (BouLac), MYNN2.5 | Captures fast TKE storage and transport; requires explicit empirical mixing length ($l_m, l_\epsilon$) parameterizations. |
| **Second-Order (2.0)** | Full Reynolds Stress Budgets | Mellor-Yamada Level 3, LRR, SSG schemes | Solves differential transport equations for all 6 $\overline{u_i' u_j'}$ components; highly accurate but computationally expensive and numerically stiff. |

---

### 4.3 First-Order Closures and Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST)

First-order schemes parameterize turbulent fluxes using the Boussinesq eddy viscosity hypothesis:

$$
\overline{u' w'} = -K_m \frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial z}, \quad \overline{w' \theta'} = -K_h \frac{\partial \overline{\theta}}{\partial z}
$$

In surface layer parameterizations, eddy exchange coefficients ($K_m, K_h$) are parameterized via MOST stability functions $\phi_m(\zeta)$ and $\phi_h(\zeta)$, where $\zeta = z/L$ is the non-dimensional height scaled by the Monin-Obukhov length $L$:

$$
L = -\frac{u_*^3}{\kappa \frac{g}{\theta_0} \overline{w' \theta_v'}_0}
$$

$$
K_m = \frac{\kappa u_* z}{\phi_m(z/L)}
$$

#### Limitations and Singularities in Stable Regimes
Under strong nocturnal cooling ($\zeta \gg 0$), classical empirical stability functions (e.g., Businger-Dyer or Webb formulations $\phi_m \approx 1 + 5\zeta$) assume monotonic damping.

When applied to gradient Richardson numbers $Ri = \frac{g}{\theta_v} \frac{\partial \theta_v / \partial z}{(\partial u/\partial z)^2}$, classical schemes impose a sharp critical cutoff ($Ri_c \approx 0.25$). Below jet cores where vertical wind shear vanishes ($\partial u/\partial z \to 0$), $Ri \to \infty$, causing division-by-zero singularities and artificial decoupling in regional weather models (e.g., WRF).

---

### 4.4 1.5-Order TKE Closures and Non-Local Extensions

To resolve the memory and non-local transport of turbulent bursts, 1.5-order schemes retain an explicit prognostic transport equation for TKE ($e$):

$$
\frac{\partial e}{\partial t} + \overline{u_j} \frac{\partial e}{\partial x_j} = \underbrace{-\overline{u' w'} \frac{\partial \overline{u}}{\partial z} - \overline{v' w'} \frac{\partial \overline{v}}{\partial z}}_{\text{Shear Production } P_s} + \underbrace{\frac{g}{\theta_0} \overline{w' \theta_v'}}_{\text{Buoyancy Production/Damping } P_b} - \underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left( \overline{w' e'} + \frac{\overline{w' p'}}{\rho_0} \right)}_{\text{Turbulent Transport } D_e} - \underbrace{\epsilon_{\text{TKE}}}_{\text{Dissipation}}
$$

Eddy viscosity is parameterized using the local TKE scale:

$$
K_m = C_k l_m \sqrt{e}, \quad \epsilon_{\text{TKE}} = C_\epsilon \frac{e^{3/2}}{l_\epsilon}
$$

By combining $e$ as a fast prognostic variable, 1.5-order closures form an exact physical bridge to GSPT phase-space dynamics.

### 5.1 Topological Coordinate Manifold Closures ($R_{\text{coord}}$)

Geometric Surface Pattern Theory introduces non-local topological coordinate metrics $R_{\text{coord}}$ to replace monotonic stability functions. Rather than parameterizing $K_m$ purely as a local function of $Ri(z)$, $K_m$ is evaluated relative to the manifold distance from the critical fold boundary $\mathcal{S}_0$:

$$
K_m\left(R_{\text{coord}}\right) = K_0 \cdot \Phi\left(R_{\text{coord}} - R_{\text{fold}}\right)
$$

Where $R_{\text{coord}}$ integrates vertical profile curvature, potential temperature gradient inflections, and non-uniform grid metric terms:

$$
R_{\text{coord}}(z) = \int_0^z \sqrt{ \left( \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{u}}{\partial z^2} \right)^2 + \gamma^2 \left( \frac{g}{\theta_0} \frac{\partial \theta_v}{\partial z} \right) } \, dz
$$

### 5.2 Dynamic Hysteresis and Intermittent Turbulence Revivals

When embedded in prognostic mesoscale models, manifold-consistent fast-slow parameterizations reproduce physical intermittent behavior naturally:

1. **Decoupling Phase:** Surface layer drag drops as TKE collapses along the lower branch ($e \to 0$).
2. **LLJ Acceleration Phase:** Aloft, decoupling removes frictional drag, allowing geostrophic imbalance to accelerate winds into a Low-Level Jet (LLJ).
3. **Shear Buildup:** The expanding LLJ core increases vertical shear $S$ beneath the jet nose.
4. **Resumption Jump:** Once $S$ surpasses the saddle-node threshold $S_{\text{fold}}$, normal hyperbolicity is lost. The fast subsystem jumps upward to the turbulent branch, mixing momentum downward, eroding the jet, and re-initiating the cycle.
