### Section I: The "Fold Illusion" Mechanism

In Stable Boundary Layer (SBL) meteorology, vertical profiles of the gradient Richardson number (\\(Ri_g\\)) frequently exhibit sharp, localized inflection features, commonly referred to as "knees," where the profile crosses the classical critical stability threshold (\\(Ri_c \approx 0.25\\)). Historically, researchers have interpreted these threshold-crossing inflections as autonomous physical bifurcations signaling the local extinction or sudden "death" of turbulent mixing.

Generalized Similarity Profile Theory (GSPT) reveals that these inflections are frequently **"Fold Illusions"**—non-linear coordinate projection artifacts arising from the mapping between physical height (\\(z\\)) and the non-dimensional similarity coordinate:
\\[\zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)}\\]
where \\(L(z)\\) is the height-varying Obukhov length.

To mathematically isolate this illusion, GSPT decomposes the vertical spatial curvature of the gradient Richardson number (\\(Ri_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2}\\)) using the multi-variable chain rule:
\\[Ri_{zz} = \underbrace{Ri_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2}_{C_{\text{constitutive}}} + \underbrace{Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}}_{C_{\text{mapping}}}\\]

*   **Constitutive Curvature (\\(C_{\text{constitutive}}\\)):** Represents the bending of the empirical similarity relation mapped into physical space. Since it scales as \\(O(\zeta_z^2)\\), it is highly dependent on local coordinate stretching.
*   **Mapping Curvature (\\(C_{\text{mapping}}\\)):** Represents the pure geometric bending generated by the non-linear coordinate transformation \\(z \mapsto \zeta(z)\\). It scales as \\(O(\zeta_z)\\).

#### Curvature Behavior Near Zero-Shear Surfaces
Near the nose of a Low-Level Jet (LLJ), wind shear weakens and approaches zero (\\(U_z \to 0\\)). This forces the local Obukhov length to collapse (\\(L \to 0\\)), which drives the similarity coordinate gradient toward zero (\\(\zeta_z \to 0\\)). Evaluating the relative magnitudes of the curvature terms as \\(\zeta_z \to 0\\) reveals that the mapping term asymptotically dominates:
\\[\frac{|C_{\text{mapping}}|}{|C_{\text{constitutive}}|} \to \infty\\]

Crucially, this coordinate dominance of relative curvature does *not* imply that the absolute curvature \\(Ri_{zz}\\) diverges. At a local coordinate turning point where \\(\zeta_z(z_*) = 0\\):
\\[Ri_z(z_*) = Ri_\zeta(\zeta_*) \zeta_z(z_*) = 0\\]
\\[Ri_{zz}(z_*) = Ri_\zeta(\zeta_*) \zeta_{zz}(z_*)\\]

Because \\(Ri_\zeta = (1+\beta_m\zeta)^{-2} > 0\\) under standard linear similarity closures, the physical profile exhibits a finite, smooth, quadratic turning point (\\(Ri_{zz}(z_*) \neq \infty\\)) rather than a singular physical collapse.

#### The Linear Obukhov Profile Test
A major analytical safeguard of the GSPT framework is the **Linear Obukhov Profile Test**. Under a strictly linear Obukhov profile \\(L(z) = L_0 + L'z\\) (where \\(L'' = 0\\)):
\\[\zeta_z = \frac{L_0}{(L_0 + L'z)^2} > 0\\]

Because \\(\zeta_z\\) is strictly positive for all finite \\(z\\), **no finite coordinate fold can exist under a linear Obukhov profile**. Instead, the similarity coordinate asymptotically compresses toward \\(1/L'\\) as \\(z \to \infty\\). Therefore, a visually sharp knee in physical space cannot be classified as a coordinate fold unless independent evidence establishes an actual zero of \\(\zeta_z\\) at a finite height (\\(L(z_*) = z_* L'(z_*)\\)) paired with non-zero curvature (\\(L''(z_*) \neq 0\\)).

#### Tri-Singularity Classification
GSPT formally separates these features into three distinct mathematical domains to prevent physical misinterpretations:

1.  **Constitutive Geometry [\\(Ri = R(\zeta)\\)]:** The empirical similarity curve closed by local momentum and heat stability functions.
2.  **Observation-Coordinate Geometry [\\(\zeta = \zeta(z)\\)]:** Kinematic turning points in physical 1D space occurring when \\(\zeta_z(z_*) = 0\\) and \\(\zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0\\).
3.  **Dynamical State-Space Geometry [\\(\dot{X} = F(X, \mu)\\)]:** True dynamical saddle-node bifurcations characterized by a zero fast eigenvalue (\\(\lambda_f = 0\\)) and a singular Jacobian (\\(\det D_X F = 0\\)), corresponding to the physical extinction of the Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE) manifold.

Mathematically, **a coordinate fold does not imply a dynamical fold** (\\(\zeta_z = 0 \centernot\implies \det D_X F = 0\\)). They represent singularities in entirely different mathematical spaces. A threshold crossing of \\(Ri_g = Ri_c\\) is neither a coordinate-fold criterion nor a saddle-node criterion.

### Section III: Methodology

To reconstruct vertical gradients without introducing unphysical mathematical artifacts, GSPT employs a rigorous multi-stage preprocessing and inversion pipeline:

#### 1. Primitive-Field Tikhonov-Morozov Spline Regularization
Calculating vertical gradients (\\(\partial \theta / \partial z\\), \\(\partial U / \partial z\\)) using discrete, noisy tower measurements is an ill-posed inverse problem. Spatial differentiation acts as a high-pass filter, multiplying noise amplitudes by wavenumber \\(k\\) and amplifying high-frequency noise variance by \\(k^2\\).

To prevent this noise amplification from corrupting downstream calculations, GSPT fits a Tikhonov-regularized cubic smoothing spline to the primitive variables (\\(u, v, \theta\\)) before performing analytical differentiation. Crucially, because the non-linear quotient operator (\\(\mathcal{Q}\\)) does not commute with spatial filters (\\(M_\delta\\)) or discrete difference operators (\\(D_{\Delta z}\\)), **regularization must be applied directly to the primitive fields prior to differentiation**:
\\[Ri_g^{(\delta)} = \frac{\frac{g}{\theta_v} D_{\Delta z}(M_\delta \theta_v)}{[D_{\Delta z}(M_\delta u)]^2 + [D_{\Delta z}(M_\delta v)]^2}\\]
Applying the spatial smoother directly to calculated Richardson profiles (\\(M_\delta \circ \mathcal{Q}\\)) rather than the primitive fields (\\(\mathcal{Q} \circ D_{\Delta z} \circ M_\delta\\)) silently manufactures artificial coordinate structures around low-shear regions.

#### 2. In-Situ Noise Floor Extraction (\\(\delta\\))
To prevent the spline from over-smoothing sharp, physical boundary-layer features (like the tops of thin nocturnal inversions), GSPT isolates true instrument electronic noise (\\(\delta^2\\)) from microscale turbulent variance. This is achieved using two complementary spectral/real-space methods:

1.  **Inertial Subrange Spectral Fitting:** The power spectral density of the high-frequency wind/temperature series is computed. While true turbulence follows Kolmogorov's universal power-law slope (\\(S(f) \propto f^{-5/3}\\)), white instrument noise exhibits a flat spectrum. Identifying the frequency \\(f_n\\) where the observed spectrum departs from the \\(-5/3\\) slope allows researchers to integrate the flat noise floor, yielding a precise, run-specific in-situ noise variance.
2.  **Structure Function Analysis:** The second-order structure function is evaluated:
    \\[D(r) = \langle [x(t+r) - x(t)]^2 \rangle\\]
    For inertial-range turbulence, \\(D(r)\\) scales as \\(r^{2/3}\\). Extrapolating this curve back to \\(r \to 0\\) isolates the constant offset contributed by uncorrelated sensor noise, where the intercept directly defines the noise variance: \\(D(0^+) \approx 2\delta^2\\).

These dynamic noise parameters are passed to the bisection-driven Tikhonov solver, ensuring that the spline fit satisfies **Morozov's Discrepancy Principle**:
\\[\tau_1 \delta \le \Vert F(x_\alpha) - y^\delta \Vert \le \tau_2 \delta\\]
This guarantees that the mathematical solver does not fit the regularized profile tighter than the known, in-situ precision of the observing network.

#### 3. Uncertainty-Aware Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS)
Transforming the regularized profiles into the similarity stability parameter \\(\zeta\\) via the gradient Richardson number introduces a double-amplification error loop. As wind shear approaches zero (\\(U_z \to 0\\)), gradient noise variance blows up as \\(U_z^{-6}\\).

To resolve this downstream ill-conditioning, GSPT deploys a **Downstream-Weighted Morozov** scheme via an IRLS loop. At each iteration \\(k\\), the diagonal entries of the spline weight matrix (\\(W\\)) are scaled by the propagated local uncertainty \\(\sigma_{\zeta}\\):
\\[w_i^{(k)} = \frac{1}{1 + \sigma_{\zeta,i}^2 / \sigma_{\zeta,\text{ref}}^2}\\]

where the analytical standard deviation is computed using the local sensitivity Jacobians:
\\[\sigma_{\zeta,i}^2 \approx \left(\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial\theta_z}\right)^2 \sigma_{\theta_z,i}^2 + \left(\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial U_z}\right)^2 \sigma_{U_z,i}^2\\]

This reweighting scheme has the desired statistical behavior: in regions where wind shear is weak or the coordinate mapping is highly sensitive, \\(\sigma_{\zeta}\\) spikes, causing the weights to drop (\\(w_i \downarrow\\)). This automatically forces the spline to smooth aggressively in poorly conditioned layers while preserving tight gradient fidelity where the coordinate transformation is well-conditioned.

#### 4. Newton-Raphson Inversion of Grachev et al. (2007)
Standard Monin-Obukhov formulations (like Businger-Dyer) assume a hard critical Richardson cutoff at \\(Ri_{cr} = 0.20\\), causing the coordinate conditioning metric (\\(\kappa_\zeta \equiv |d\zeta/dRi_g|\\)) to diverge as \\((1-5Ri_g)^{-2}\\).

GSPT sidesteps this coordinate singularity by swapping Businger-Dyer with the SHEBA-calibrated **Grachev et al. (2007) formulation**:
\\[\phi_m(\zeta) = 1 + a_m \frac{\zeta(1+\zeta)^{1/3}}{1 + b_m \zeta}, \qquad \phi_h(\zeta) = 1 + \frac{a_h \zeta + b_h \zeta^2}{1 + c_h \zeta + \zeta^2}\\]
where \\(a_m = 5\\), \\(b_m = 5/6.5 \approx 0.7692\\), \\(a_h = 5\\), \\(b_h = 5\\), and \\(c_h = 3\\). Because the Grachev formulation has no critical Richardson cutoff (at large \\(\zeta\\), the mapping function \\(R(\zeta) = \zeta \phi_h / \phi_m^2\\) grows as \\(\zeta^{1/3}\\)), there are no coordinate singularities under very stable conditions. A robust 1D Newton-Raphson solver is utilized to invert the non-monotonic relation \\(R(\zeta) - Ri_g = 0\\).

### Section IV: SCM Implications and Conclusions

#### 1. Preventing the "Compensating Error" of NWP Tuning
When Single-Column Models (SCMs) or numerical weather prediction (NWP) schemes misrepresent observed vertical profiles in the SBL, model developers typically attempt to tune the empirical similarity constants (\\(\beta_m, \beta_h\\)) within the stability functions (\\(\phi_m, \phi_h\\)).

GSPT demonstrates that this is frequently a **compensating error**. When a model's simulated profile diverges from observations, GSPT partitions the total model-observation spatial curvature residual (\\(\Delta_{\text{SCM}}\\)) to isolate the root cause:
\\[\Delta_{\text{SCM}} \equiv (C_{\text{const}}^{\text{SCM}} + C_{\text{coord}}^{\text{SCM}}) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}} = \Delta_{\text{closure}} + \Delta_{\text{geom}} + \tau_{\Delta z}\\]

*   **If \\(|\Delta_{\text{closure}}| \gg 0\\) and \\(\Delta_{\text{geom}} \approx 0\\):** The local stability functions (\\(\phi_m, \phi_h\\)) are physically inaccurate. The appropriate action is to adjust the local stability parameters or swap the closure (e.g., transitioning from Businger-Dyer to Grachev 2007).
*   **If \\(|\Delta_{\text{geom}}| \gg 0\\) and \\(\Delta_{\text{closure}} \approx 0\\):** The stability functions are perfectly fine; the error is driven by **flux-profile geometry**. Excessive vertical mixing is over-smoothing the heat and momentum flux divergence profiles (\\(L', L''\\)), or the vertical grid spacing is too coarse to capture local gradients. In this case, developers must adjust local mixing lengths (\\(l_m\\)), vertical grid stretching, or non-local transport—**leaving the local stability functions completely untouched**.

By providing this rigorous, testable error-partitioning framework, GSPT prevents SCM developers from corrupting local physical closures to compensate for vertical transport or grid resolution deficiencies.

#### 2. Conclusions
*   **Inflection Points are Not Bifurcations:** Physical-space inflections ("knees") are primarily coordinate projection features driven by vertical flux divergence (\\(L' \neq 0\\)) and zero-shear surfaces (\\(U_z \to 0\\)) rather than state-space dynamical bifurcations.
*   **Grid and Flux Resolution Matter:** Reconstructing the SBL similarity coordinate \\(\zeta\\) is highly sensitive to vertical resolution and spatial differentiation methods. Applying Tikhonov-Morozov smoothing spline filters directly to primitive fields prior to differentiation is mandatory to suppress artificial numerical gradients.
*   **Grachev (2007) Prevents Singularity:** Transitioning from Businger-Dyer to the SHEBA-calibrated Grachev (2007) closure removes Businger-Dyer's asymptotic coordinate singularity near \\(Ri_g \to 0.20\\), keeping the inverse mapping well-conditioned under strongly stable stratification.

\section{Introduction}

The representation of the stable atmospheric boundary layer (SBL) in numerical weather prediction (NWP) and single-column models (SCMs) remains one of the most enduring challenges in boundary-layer meteorology \cite{gabls3_2013}. Under stable stratification, the vertical structure of the boundary layer is governed by a delicate and highly non-linear balance between mechanical shear production, buoyant consumption, and molecular dissipation. SBLs are conventionally categorized into two distinct physical regimes: the Weakly Stable Boundary Layer (WSBL), characterized by continuous mechanical shear production and robust vertical coupling with the surface, and the Very Stable Boundary Layer (VSBL), where turbulent mixing is strongly suppressed, leading to surface-radiation decoupling, rapid land-surface temperature drops, and the development of strong nocturnal ground inversions \cite{businger1971, grachev2007}.

Historically, the transition between these two regimes has been diagnosed using local stability parameters, most notably the gradient Richardson number ($Ri_g$). The classical transition is assumed to occur at a universal critical threshold ($Ri_c \approx 0.25$), above which mechanical shear can no longer sustain vertical turbulent fluxes, resulting in an abrupt "turbulent collapse" or Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) breakdown \cite{businger1971}. However, field campaigns (such as CASES-99 and SHEBA) and high-resolution Large-Eddy Simulations (LES) demonstrate that SBL transitions are highly non-stationary, exhibiting significant hysteresis, intermittency, and a distinct lack of a universal critical Richardson number \cite{cases99, sheba2002}. This lack of universality arises because a single scalar Richardson number represents a projection of a higher-dimensional dynamical state; different atmospheric columns can reach the same physical transition at vastly different apparent values of $Ri_g$ \cite{30Aug2029}.

In this paper, we establish an analytical and geometric framework grounded in Geometric Singular Perturbation Theory (GSPT) to analyze SBL transitions. We model the stable boundary layer as a coupled slow-fast dynamical system, wherein the microscale Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE), $E$, adjusts rapidly on a fast timescale, while the mesoscale vertical wind shear, $S$, evolves slowly on a mesoscale forcing timescale \cite{BelowFastSlow}. To resolve the numerical singularities that plague standard unregularized boundary layer formulations near the laminar state ($E \to 0$), we introduce a velocity-scale regularization floor into the governing equations, restoring $C^1$-continuity of the fast vector field across zero-energy limits. Furthermore, we outline the Fenichel slow-manifold invariance equations to characterize the quasi-equilibrium states where turbulence "rides" a slow manifold, and prove that regime transitions correspond to a loss of normal hyperbolicity at nondegenerate saddle-node folds of the critical manifold rather than unphysical threshold crossings.

\subsection{Fast-Slow SBL System Formulation}

The multiscale nature of SBL turbulence is mathematically formalized by parameterizing the timescale separation between fast microscale turbulence and slow mesoscale wind shear using a small dimensionless ratio $0 < \epsilon \ll 1$ \cite{BelowFastSlow}. Let $E \ge 0$ denote the turbulent kinetic energy per unit mass ($\text{m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$), and let $S \ge 0$ denote the vertical wind shear magnitude ($\text{s}^{-1}$). The prognostic equations for the coupled zero-dimensional SBL TKE budget are given by:
\begin{align}
    \epsilon \frac{dE}{dt} &= f(E, S; N^2) = \mathcal{P}(E,S) - \mathcal{B}_{\text{bg}}(E,N^2) - \mathcal{B}_{\text{mod}}(E,N^2) - \varepsilon(E), \label{eq:fast_tke} \\
    \frac{dS}{dt} &= g(E, S) = G - c_1 E S - c_2 S, \label{eq:slow_shear}
\end{align}
where $N^2 > 0$ is the squared Brunt-Väisälä frequency ($\text{s}^{-2}$), representing ambient atmospheric stability, and $G > 0$ is the geostrophic forcing driving the wind speed aloft ($\text{s}^{-2}$) \cite{BelowFastSlow, CoreMathRevisions}.

Each physical component in the fast TKE budget \eqref{eq:fast_tke} is closed using 1.5-order turbulence schemes where vertical transport is parameterized via an eddy viscosity $K_m = l\sqrt{E+\delta}$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, CleanRestatement}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mechanical Shear Production ($\mathcal{P}$):} Represents the extraction of energy from the mean velocity gradient by turbulent eddies, scaled by the mixing length $l$ ($\text{m}$):
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{P}(E,S) = l\sqrt{E+\delta}\,S^2,
    \end{equation}
    where a small regularization scale $\delta = 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$ is added to the velocity-scale denominator of the production Jacobian term \cite{BelowFastSlow, CoreMathRevisions}. In classical unregularized formulations ($\delta = 0$), the TKE production term contains $\sqrt{E}$, yielding a singular Jacobian derivative at zero energy:
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{E \to 0^+} \frac{\partial}{\partial E}\left(l\sqrt{E}S^2\right) = +\infty.
    \end{equation}
    This singularity triggers severe numerical instabilities and prevents standard linear stability analysis near the laminar state. The regularization floor $\delta$ ensures that the fast vector field $f(E, S; N^2)$ remains $C^1$-continuous on the physically admissible domain $\mathcal{D} = \{(E, S) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : E \ge 0\}$ while preserving exact dimensional consistency ($\text{m}^2\text{s}^{-3}$) \cite{BelowFastSlow, CoreMathRevisions}.

    \item \textbf{Background Buoyant Loss ($\mathcal{B}_{\text{bg}}$):} Parameterizes the conventional stable-stratification penalty associated with the positive mean potential-temperature gradient, governed by a background stability coefficient $\phi$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{B}_{\text{bg}}(E,N^2) = l\sqrt{E+\delta}\,\phi N^2.
    \end{equation}
    Because $\mathcal{B}_{\text{bg}}$ is strictly monotonic in $E$, it cannot by itself generate a fold or regime transition \cite{CleanRestatement}.

    \item \textbf{Non-Monotonic Buoyant Destruction ($\mathcal{B}_{\text{mod}}$):} To capture the wave-turbulence interactions and turbulent flux suppression observed in the VSBL, we introduce a hump-shaped, non-monotonic heat flux saturation function $D_b(E; N^2)$ scaled directly by $N^2$ and a buoyant sink efficiency factor $c_b$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, CoreMathRevisions, CleanRestatement}:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{B}_{\text{mod}}(E,N^2) = l\sqrt{E+\delta}\,D_b(E; N^2) = l\sqrt{E+\delta}\,c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha E}{(E+\alpha)^2},
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha > 0$ is the regularized energy scale ($\text{m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$) at which buoyant destruction efficiency peaks \cite{CoreMathRevisions, CleanRestatement}. The derivative of this modified closure is:
    \begin{equation}
        D_b'(E) = c_b N^2 \alpha \frac{\alpha - E}{(E+\alpha)^3},
    \end{equation}
    which is positive for $E < \alpha$, zero at $E = \alpha$, and negative for $E > \alpha$ \cite{CleanRestatement}. This change in sign of $D_b'(E)$ provides the necessary non-monotonicity in the fast vector field to permit the SBL equilibrium manifold to fold, a behavior that standard monotonic similarity closures (e.g., Businger-Dyer or Louis functions) fail to capture \cite{CleanRestatement, StableBuoyancy}.

    \item \textbf{Viscous Dissipation ($\varepsilon$):} Parameterized via the conventional 1.5-order Kolmogorov-type dissipation rate:
    \begin{equation}
        \varepsilon(E) = \frac{E^{3/2}}{l}.
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

In the slow equation \eqref{eq:slow_shear}, geostrophic shear forcing $G$ is balanced by background relaxation (with coefficient $c_2 \in \text{s}^{-1}$) and turbulent momentum mixing (with damping coefficient $c_1 \in \text{s}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}$) \cite{BelowFastSlow, CoreMathRevisions}. This forms a closed, self-reinforcing feedback loop:
\begin{equation*}
    S \;\longrightarrow\; \text{TKE Production } (\mathcal{P}) \;\longrightarrow\; E \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Turbulent Momentum Mixing } (c_1 E S) \;\longrightarrow\; S.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Fenichel Slow-Manifold Invariance Equations}

In GSPT, the singular limit $\epsilon \to 0$ collapses the fast TKE dynamics onto the critical manifold $\mathcal{S}_0$, defined as the set of quasi-steady turbulent equilibria for a given shear state \cite{BelowFastSlow, CleanRestatement}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_0 = \left\{ (E, S) \in \mathcal{D} : f(E, S; N^2) = 0 \right\}. \label{eq:critical_manifold}
\end{equation}
On $\mathcal{S}_0$, the local relaxation rate of trajectories in the fast direction is governed by the fast eigenvalue $\lambda_f(E, S) \equiv \frac{\partial f}{\partial E}$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, CleanRestatement}:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_f(E, S) = \frac{l(S^2 - \phi N^2)}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}} - c_b N^2 l \left[ \frac{E}{2\sqrt{E+\delta}(E+\alpha)} + \frac{\alpha\sqrt{E+\delta}}{(E+\alpha)^2} \right] - \frac{3\sqrt{E}}{2l}. \label{eq:lambda_f}
\end{equation}
Under Fenichel's Theorem, any compact, normally hyperbolic branch of the critical manifold—where the fast eigenvalue remains bounded away from zero ($\lambda_f \neq 0$)—persists for sufficiently small $\epsilon > 0$ as a smooth, invariant slow manifold $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, Fenichel1979}. This slow manifold is $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$-close to $\mathcal{S}_0$ and completely governs the long-term, coupled evolution of the boundary layer state.

To mathematically define the geometry of the invariant slow manifold, we parameterize $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$ as a function of the slow variable: $E = \Phi(S; \epsilon)$. Substituting this parameterization into the fast prognostic relation \eqref{eq:fast_tke} and applying the chain rule yields the \textit{Fenichel slow-manifold invariance equation} \cite{BelowFastSlow, Fenichel1979}:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon \frac{\partial \Phi(S; \epsilon)}{\partial S} \frac{dS}{dt} = f(\Phi(S; \epsilon), S; N^2). \label{eq:fenichel_invariance}
\end{equation}
Substituting the slow shear tendency \eqref{eq:slow_shear} into \eqref{eq:fenichel_invariance} yields the closed algebraic-differential invariance relation:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon \frac{\partial \Phi(S; \epsilon)}{\partial S} \left[ G - c_1 \Phi(S; \epsilon) S - c_2 S \right] = f(\Phi(S; \epsilon), S; N^2). \label{eq:fenichel_closed}
\end{equation}
Because $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$ is $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$-close to $\mathcal{S}_0$, we can express the slow manifold parameterization as a regular perturbation expansion in powers of $\epsilon$:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(S; \epsilon) = \Phi_0(S) + \epsilon \Phi_1(S) + \epsilon^2 \Phi_2(S) + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^3). \label{eq:perturbation}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:perturbation} into the closed invariance relation \eqref{eq:fenichel_closed} and equating powers of $\epsilon$ allows us to systematically resolve the manifold's geometry:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{At $\mathcal{O}(1)$ (singular limit):}
    \begin{equation}
        f(\Phi_0(S), S; N^2) = 0.
    \end{equation}
    This confirms that the leading-order term $\Phi_0(S)$ is exactly the critical manifold $\mathcal{S}_0$, representing the instantaneous turbulent equilibrium of the SBL.

    \item \textbf{At $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$ (first-order correction):}
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\partial \Phi_0(S)}{\partial S} \left[ G - c_1 \Phi_0(S) S - c_2 S \right] = \frac{\partial f(\Phi_0(S), S; N^2)}{\partial E} \Phi_1(S).
    \end{equation}
    Solving for the first-order slow-manifold perturbation $\Phi_1(S)$ yields:
    \begin{equation}
        \Phi_1(S) = \left[ \lambda_f(\Phi_0(S), S) \right]^{-1} \frac{\partial \Phi_0(S)}{\partial S} g(\Phi_0(S), S), \label{eq:fenichel_correction}
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda_f$ is the local fast eigenvalue defined in \eqref{eq:lambda_f}.
\end{itemize}

Equation \eqref{eq:fenichel_correction} highlights the crucial role of the fast eigenvalue in slow-manifold tracking. In normally hyperbolic regions where the attracting branch is stable ($\lambda_f < 0$), the first-order correction $\Phi_1(S)$ is bounded, and the physical trajectory rapidly relaxes onto $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$ on a fast timescale $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon/|\lambda_f|)$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, Fenichel1979}.

However, Fenichel's normal hyperbolicity condition breaks down entirely at the candidate fold boundaries where the fast eigenvalue vanishes ($\lambda_f = f_E = 0$) \cite{BelowFastSlow, CleanRestatement}. At these points, the inverse eigenvalue in \eqref{eq:fenichel_correction} diverges, and the slow manifold $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$ ceases to exist. This breakdown marks the transition threshold where a slowly cooling boundary layer can no longer track the stable WSBL branch. Instead, the trajectory encounters a dynamic saddle-node fold and is forced to undergo an explosive, fast-timescale jump across the unstable repelling branch ($\lambda_f > 0$) toward the low-energy attracting VSBL branch, providing a rigorous, state-space explanation for abrupt SBL regime transitions and hysteresis \cite{CleanRestatement}.

A central tenet of boundary-layer meteorology is that vertical profile shapes directly reflect the underlying physics of turbulent transport. However, when vertical profiles of gradient Richardson numbers ($Ri_g$) are plotted in physical vertical space ($z$), visual features such as sharp inflection points ("knees") or threshold-crossing layers are frequently assumed to represent physical state-space bifurcations \cite{152}. By decoupling these effects, we show that visual knees can arise as pure coordinate-mapping features (referred to here as "Fold Illusions") rather than dynamical bifurcations of the Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE) manifold \cite{152, 153, 273}.

\subsection{The GSPT Curvature Decomposition}

Let $z$ denote physical vertical height ($\text{m}$), and let $L(z)$ represent the height-varying Monin-Obukhov length ($\text{m}$), which dynamically parameterizes local thermodynamic stability as a function of height-varying turbulent fluxes \cite{153, 205}. The non-dimensional Monin-Obukhov similarity coordinate $\zeta(z)$ is defined by the mapping \cite{153, 258}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)}. \label{eq:zeta_def}
\end{equation}
To evaluate how vertical gradients and curvatures are projected from similarity space into physical observation space, we compute the first two spatial derivatives of the coordinate mapping $\zeta(z)$ with respect to physical height $z$. Applying the quotient rule directly to \eqref{eq:zeta_def} yields the coordinate Jacobian $\zeta_z \equiv \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial z}$ \cite{153, 259}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_z = \frac{L(z) - z L'(z)}{L(z)^2} = \frac{1}{L(z)}\left(1 - \frac{z L'(z)}{L(z)}\right) = \frac{1 - \zeta L'(z)}{L(z)}, \label{eq:zeta_z}
\end{equation}
where $L'(z) \equiv \frac{dL}{dz}$ represents the vertical gradient of the local Obukhov length profile \cite{153, 259}. Differentiating \eqref{eq:zeta_z} a second time with respect to $z$ yields the vertical coordinate curvature $\zeta_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 \zeta}{\partial z^2}$ \cite{153, 259}:
\begin{align}
    \zeta_{zz} &= \frac{d}{dz}\left( \frac{L - z L'}{L^2} \right) \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{(L' - L' - z L'')L^2 - (L - z L') \cdot 2 L L'}{L^4} \nonumber \\
    &= -\frac{z L''(z)}{L(z)^2} - \frac{2 L'(z)}{L(z)} \left( \frac{L(z) - z L'(z)}{L(z)^2} \right) \nonumber \\
    &= -\frac{z L''(z)}{L(z)^2} - 2\frac{L'(z)}{L(z)}\zeta_z, \label{eq:zeta_zz_derivation}
\end{align}
which can be expressed in terms of the similarity coordinate $\zeta$ as \cite{259}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{zz} = -\frac{L''(z)}{L(z)}\zeta - 2\frac{L'(z)}{L(z)}\zeta_z. \label{eq:zeta_zz}
\end{equation}
Now, let $Ri_g(z) = R(\zeta(z))$ represent a general Monin-Obukhov flux-profile closure relation mapping similarity stability to the gradient Richardson number, where $R \in C^2(\mathbb{R})$ \cite{258, 271}. Applying the multi-variable chain rule to differentiate the physical Richardson profile $Ri_g(z)$ twice with respect to the vertical height $z$ yields \cite{258}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_z = R_\zeta \zeta_z, \label{eq:Ri_z}
\end{equation}
and \cite{153, 258}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_{zz} = R_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2 + R_\zeta \zeta_{zz}. \label{eq:Ri_zz_decomp}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:Ri_zz_decomp} defines the canonical GSPT curvature decomposition \cite{153, 258}. It partitions the total observed vertical spatial curvature of the Richardson profile into two mathematically distinct geometric components \cite{198}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_{zz} = C_{\text{constitutive}} + C_{\text{mapping}}, \label{eq:Ri_zz_partition}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{align}
    C_{\text{constitutive}} &\equiv R_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2, \label{eq:C_const} \\
    C_{\text{mapping}} &\equiv R_\zeta \zeta_{zz}. \label{eq:C_map}
\end{align}
The \textit{constitutive curvature} ($C_{\text{constitutive}}$) represents the physical bending of the empirical similarity relation itself mapped into physical space \cite{198}. Because it scales quadratically with the coordinate Jacobian ($\zeta_z^2$), it is highly sensitive to local coordinate stretching \cite{154, 198}. The \textit{mapping curvature} ($C_{\text{mapping}}$) represents the pure geometric curvature generated by the non-linear coordinate projection $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ \cite{154, 198}.

\subsection{Scaling Behavior at the Turning Limit ($\zeta_z \to 0$)}

To understand the visual emergence of profile knees near the boundary of shear-dominated turbulent layers (such as the nose of a Low-Level Jet), we analyze the asymptotic scaling behavior of \eqref{eq:C_const} and \eqref{eq:C_map} as the coordinate Jacobian approaches zero ($\zeta_z \to 0$) \cite{154, 155, 214}.

Assuming a standard linear flux-profile closure on the stable branch ($\phi_m(\zeta) = \phi_h(\zeta) = 1 + \beta_m \zeta$) \cite{154, 207}, the similarity relation maps to the gradient Richardson number via \cite{154, 207}:
\begin{equation}
    R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta \phi_h}{\phi_m^2} = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta_m \zeta}. \label{eq:R_linear_closure}
\end{equation}
Its respective derivatives in similarity space are given by \cite{197}:
\begin{equation}
    R_\zeta = \frac{1}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^2} > 0, \qquad R_{\zeta\zeta} = -\frac{2\beta_m}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^3}. \label{eq:R_derivatives}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:R_derivatives} into the GSPT partition \eqref{eq:Ri_zz_partition} yields \cite{260}:
\begin{equation}
    C_{\text{constitutive}} = -\frac{2\beta_m}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^3}\zeta_z^2, \qquad C_{\text{mapping}} = \frac{\zeta_{zz}}{(1+\beta_m\zeta)^2}. \label{eq:C_terms_explicit}
\end{equation}
Evaluating these terms in the limit of vanishing coordinate Jacobian ($\zeta_z \to 0$) under non-zero local coordinate curvature ($\zeta_{zz} = \mathcal{O}(1)$) reveals a clear scaling disparity \cite{214, 271}:
\begin{align}
    C_{\text{constitutive}} &= \mathcal{O}(\zeta_z^2), \label{eq:scaling_const} \\
    C_{\text{mapping}} &= \mathcal{O}(\zeta_z). \label{eq:scaling_map}
\end{align}
Taking the ratio of relative magnitudes under these asymptotic conditions yields \cite{155, 201, 272}:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{\zeta_z \to 0} \frac{|C_{\text{mapping}}|}{|C_{\text{constitutive}}|} = \lim_{\zeta_z \to 0} \frac{|R_\zeta \zeta_{zz}|}{|R_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2|} \propto \lim_{\zeta_z \to 0} \frac{1}{|\zeta_z|} = \infty. \label{eq:ratio_divergence}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:ratio_divergence} mathematically demonstrates the coordinate dominance of relative curvature at the turning limit: as the similarity grid compresses, the observed spatial profile curvature is governed entirely by the mapping coordinate geometry rather than the underlying empirical physics of the closure function \cite{202, 214, 272}.

However, this relative divergence does \textit{not} imply that the absolute curvature of the physical Richardson profile explodes. At a local turning point $z_*$ where $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ \cite{155, 199, 260}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_z(z_*) = R_\zeta(\zeta_*) \zeta_z(z_*) = 0, \label{eq:Ri_z_turning}
\end{equation}
and the second derivative simplifies to \cite{155, 200, 260}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_{zz}(z_*) = R_\zeta(\zeta_*) \zeta_{zz}(z_*) = \frac{\zeta_{zz}(z_*)}{(1+\beta_m\zeta_*)^2}. \label{eq:Ri_zz_turning}
\end{equation}
Because the similarity derivative $R_\zeta(\zeta_*)$ remains strictly positive and bounded for all finite physical stability values, the observed profile exhibits a finite, smooth, quadratic turning point ($Ri_z = 0$, $Ri_{zz} \neq \infty$) rather than a singular physical collapse \cite{155, 200}.

\subsection{The Linear Obukhov Profile Test}

To establish a mathematical boundary between pure coordinate grid stretching and genuine vertical coordinate folding, we prove the \textit{Linear Obukhov Profile Test}. This analytical safeguard determines whether a localized profile "knee" can be mathematically attributed to a finite-height coordinate fold \cite{156, 203, 262}.

\begin{theorem}[Linear Obukhov Profile Test]
Under a strictly linear vertical profile of the Monin-Obukhov length $L(z) = L_0 + a z$ (where $L_0 > 0$ represents the surface boundary value and $a = L' > 0$ represents a constant vertical flux divergence), no finite-height coordinate fold can exist, and the similarity coordinate mapping $\zeta(z)$ is globally monotone for all finite $z \ge 0$ \cite{156, 202, 262}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume the local Obukhov length profile is linear in $z$ \cite{262}:
\begin{equation}
    L(z) = L_0 + a z, \qquad L_0 > 0, \ a > 0. \label{eq:linear_L}
\end{equation}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:linear_L} with respect to $z$ yields the constant gradient and vanishing curvature profiles \cite{156, 205}:
\begin{equation}
    L'(z) = a, \qquad L''(z) = 0. \label{eq:linear_L_derivs}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:linear_L} and \eqref{eq:linear_L_derivs} into the coordinate Jacobian equation \eqref{eq:zeta_z} yields \cite{262}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_z = \frac{L(z) - z L'(z)}{L(z)^2} = \frac{(L_0 + a z) - a z}{(L_0 + a z)^2} = \frac{L_0}{(L_0 + a z)^2}. \label{eq:linear_jacobian}
\end{equation}
Because the surface boundary value is strictly positive ($L_0 > 0$) and the squared denominator is strictly positive for all physical heights, we have \cite{156, 262}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_z > 0 \qquad \forall z \in [0, \infty). \label{eq:strictly_positive_jacobian}
\end{equation}
Since the coordinate Jacobian is strictly positive everywhere, the mapping $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ is globally monotone increasing. Thus, no turning point ($\zeta_z = 0$) can be realized at any finite height \cite{156, 262}.

As $z \to \infty$, the similarity coordinate asymptotically compresses toward a fixed upper bound \cite{156, 262}:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{z \to \infty} \zeta(z) = \lim_{z \to \infty} \frac{z}{L_0 + a z} = \frac{1}{a}, \label{eq:asymptotic_compression}
\end{equation}
and the Jacobian vanishes only in the asymptotic limit \cite{262}:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{z \to \infty} \zeta_z = \lim_{z \to \infty} \frac{L_0}{(L_0 + a z)^2} = 0. \label{eq:asymptotic_jacobian}
\end{equation}
Therefore, under a linear Obukhov profile, the similarity coordinate undergoes asymptotic coordinate compression rather than a finite-height fold \cite{156, 215}.
\end{proof}

The practical implication of the \textit{Linear Obukhov Profile Test} is highly consequential for observational diagnostics: a visually sharp vertical inflection in an observed $Ri_g(z)$ profile cannot be mathematically classified as a similarity coordinate fold unless independent flux measurements establish that the local Monin-Obukhov length profile exhibits non-zero vertical curvature ($L''(z) \neq 0$) \cite{203, 215, 262}.

\subsection{Nondegenerate Finite-Height Folds}

By utilizing the results of the \textit{Linear Obukhov Profile Test}, we can formalize the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of nondegenerate, finite-height coordinate folds in physical vertical space \cite{157, 158, 204, 261}.

\begin{proposition}[Generic Coordinate-Fold Criteria]
Let $F(z) \equiv L(z) - z L'(z)$ define the coordinate turning function, such that the Jacobian is represented by $\zeta_z = \frac{F(z)}{L(z)^2}$ \cite{157, 261]. A nondegenerate, finite-height coordinate fold exists at a vertical coordinate $z_* > 0$ if and only if the Obukhov length profile $L(z) \in C^2$ satisfies \cite{157, 261}:
\begin{align}
    L(z_*) &= z_* L'(z_*), \label{eq:turning_cond} \tag{Turning Condition} \\
    L''(z_*) &\neq 0. \label{eq:nondeg_cond} \tag{Nondegeneracy Condition}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First, we establish the turning condition. For a coordinate fold to exist at $z_*$, the coordinate Jacobian must vanish, representing a loss of local transversality in the coordinate map \cite{157, 199, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_z(z_*) = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{F(z_*)}{L(z_*)^2} = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad F(z_*) = 0. \label{eq:turning_proof}
\end{equation}
Substituting $F(z_*) = L(z_*) - z_* L'(z_*) = 0$ yields the turning condition $L(z_*) = z_* L'(z_*)$ \cite{157, 261}.

Second, we evaluate the nondegeneracy condition. For the coordinate fold to be nondegenerate (representing a generic quadratic turning point in the singularity-theory sense), the second spatial derivative of the mapping must remain non-zero at the turning point \cite{157, 204, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0. \label{eq:nondeg_proof}
\end{equation}
Differentiating the turning function $F(z) = L(z) - z L'(z)$ with respect to $z$ yields \cite{157, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    F'(z) = L'(z) - [ L'(z) + z L''(z) ] = -z L''(z). \label{eq:F_prime}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the second derivative of the coordinate mapping at the turning point $z_*$ where $F(z_*) = 0$ yields \cite{204, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{zz}(z_*) = \left. \frac{d}{dz}\left( \frac{F(z)}{L(z)^2} \right) \right|_{z_*} = \frac{F'(z_*)L(z_*)^2 - F(z_*) \cdot 2 L(z_*) L'(z_*)}{L(z_*)^4} = \frac{F'(z_*)}{L(z_*)^2}. \label{eq:zeta_zz_turning}
\end{equation}
Substituting $F'(z_*) = -z_* L''(z_*)$ into \eqref{eq:zeta_zz_turning} yields the exact coordinate curvature at the fold locus \cite{204, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{zz}(z_*) = -\frac{z_* L''(z_*)}{L(z_*)^2}. \label{eq:zeta_zz_turning_final}
\end{equation}
For $z_* > 0$ and $L(z_*) \neq 0$, \eqref{eq:zeta_zz_turning_final} proves that \cite{157, 261}:
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad L''(z_*) \neq 0. \label{eq:nondeg_equivalence}
\end{equation}
This completes the proof. A nondegenerate coordinate fold requires both the local turning condition to be satisfied and the local Obukhov length profile to possess non-zero curvature ($L''(z_*) \neq 0$) \cite{157, 204, 261}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Operator Non-Commutation Hazard and Preprocessing Regularization}

Evaluating stability coordinates and profile curvature in the Stable Boundary Layer (SBL) relies heavily on reconstructing the vertical gradient Richardson number ($Ri_g$) and its vertical spatial derivatives from discrete, noisy observations \cite{158, 159}. However, because $Ri_g$ is a non-linear quotient of spatial gradients, the mathematical operators used for filtering, differencing, and division do not commute \cite{182, 239}. In this section, we formally analyze the spectral properties of gradient noise amplification, prove the non-commutation of the regularization and quotient operators, and demonstrate that post-diagnostic smoothing (filtering the calculated $Ri_g$ profile) manufactures artificial coordinate folds ("knees") near low-shear regions such as Low-Level Jet (LLJ) noses \cite{182, 239}.

\subsection{Spectral Gradient Noise Amplification}

Differentiating discrete observational tower or profiler measurements is a classically ill-posed inverse problem \cite{158, 159}. Let $q(z) = q_{\text{true}}(z) + \epsilon(z)$ represent an observed primitive atmospheric field (e.g., $u$, $v$, or $\theta_v$), where $q_{\text{true}}(z)$ is the smooth physical state and $\epsilon(z)$ represents high-frequency, uncorrelated instrument noise \cite{159, 161}. In Fourier space, the spatial derivative operator maps the spectral representation $\widehat{q}(k)$ as \cite{159, 189}:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{q'}(k) = i k \widehat{q}(k) = i k \widehat{q_{\text{true}}}(k) + i k \widehat{\epsilon}(k), \label{eq:fourier_1st_deriv}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the spatial wavenumber and $i = \sqrt{-1}$ \cite{159}. Equation \eqref{eq:fourier_1st_deriv} demonstrates that taking a first-order vertical spatial derivative multiplies the noise amplitude by $|k|$ and amplifies the noise power/variance by $k^2$ \cite{159, 189}.

For the second spatial derivative, the spectral mapping is given by \cite{189}:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{q''}(k) = -k^2 \widehat{q}(k) = -k^2 \widehat{q_{\text{true}}}(k) - k^2 \widehat{\epsilon}(k). \label{eq:fourier_2nd_deriv}
\end{equation}
Here, the derivative noise amplitude scales quadratically as $k^2$, while the noise power scales as $k^4$ \cite{189}.

On irregular, non-uniform boundary-layer grids (such as Cabauw or CASES-99), applying naive, unweighted local finite-difference stencils to compute vertical gradients amplifies these high-frequency spectral components \cite{159, 160}. This noise amplification frequently causes random sensor jitter to appear as spurious turbulence transitions or false gradient inversions, corrupting downstream stability analyses \cite{160}.

\subsection{The Non-Commutation of Inversion Operators}

To resolve noise amplification, we introduce three distinct mathematical operators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Discrete Spatial Difference Operator ($D_{\Delta z}$):} A discrete operator representing vertical spatial differentiation \cite{182, 239}.
    \item \textbf{The Tikhonov-Morozov Regularization Filter ($M_\delta$):} A smoothing operator parameterized by a regularization scale $\alpha$ (chosen via Morozov's Discrepancy Principle to match the instrument noise floor $\delta$) that maps noisy data to a smooth reconstructed profile \cite{160, 162, 182}.
    \item \textbf{The Non-Linear Quotient Operator ($\mathcal{Q}[A, B] \equiv \frac{A}{B}$):} A division mapping that takes two vertical fields and evaluates their point-by-point ratio \cite{182, 239}.
\end{enumerate}

These operators exhibit two critical non-commutation properties that govern SBL profile analysis \cite{182, 239}:

\begin{proposition}[Differential-Smoothing Non-Commutation]
The discrete spatial difference operator $D_{\Delta z}$ does not commute with the Tikhonov-Morozov regularization filter $M_\delta$ on arbitrary, non-uniform grids \cite{182, 239, 295}:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\Delta z}M_\delta \neq M_\delta D_{\Delta z}. \label{eq:diff_smooth_noncomm}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Quotient-Smoothing Non-Commutation]
The non-linear quotient operator $\mathcal{Q}$ does not commute with the Tikhonov-Morozov regularization filter $M_\delta$ on any non-trivial field domain \cite{182, 239, 296}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{Q}[M_\delta A, M_\delta B] \neq M_\delta \mathcal{Q}[A, B]. \label{eq:quot_smooth_noncomm}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
For the Richardson number calculation, this quotient non-commutation translates directly to \cite{239, 296}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{M_\delta N^2}{M_\delta S^2} \neq M_\delta\left(\frac{N^2}{S^2}\right), \label{eq:Ri_noncomm}
\end{equation}
where $N^2$ is the squared buoyancy frequency and $S^2 = u_z^2 + v_z^2$ is the squared vertical wind shear \cite{163, 214}.

\subsection{Spurious Fold Generation via Post-Diagnostic Smoothing}

When processing campaign observations, a common methodological error is to compute a raw, noisy Richardson profile ($Ri_g^{\text{raw}}$) via naive finite differences and subsequently apply a smoothing filter ($M_\delta$) to the computed output \cite{182, 239}. This "post-diagnostic" smoothing path is represented by the composite operator sequence \cite{182, 195}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_g^{\text{post-smooth}} = M_\delta \circ \mathcal{Q} \circ D_{\Delta z} [u, v, \theta_v]. \label{eq:post_smooth_path}
\end{equation}
The post-diagnostic path \eqref{eq:post_smooth_path} manufactures spurious coordinate fold geometries due to two coupled numerical mechanisms:

\subsubsection{1. Non-Linear Noise Transformation and Jump Discontinuities}
The raw gradient Richardson number, $Ri_g = \frac{g}{\theta_v}\frac{\theta_{v,z}}{U_z^2}$, is a non-linear mapping of primitive variables \cite{163, 215}. This non-linear mapping transforms linear, Gaussian instrument measurement errors in $u$, $v$, and $\theta_v$ into strongly **non-Gaussian, non-linear, state-dependent noise** in $Ri$-space \cite{163}.

Under stable stratification, this state-dependent noise introduces sharp jump discontinuities in the residual discrepancy function as the smoothing parameter $\alpha$ varies \cite{163, 164}. Because of these "discrepancy jump gaps," standard Morozov root-finding algorithms fail, as no stable $\alpha$ can be resolved within the classical discrepancy interval \cite{162, 164}. The filter is forced to toggle unstably between under-smoothing and over-smoothing, generating artificial "jumps" and knees in the filtered profile \cite{77, 164}.

\subsubsection{2. Low-Shear Noise Blowup}
Near the nose of a Low-Level Jet, vertical wind shear weakens and approaches zero ($U_z \to 0$) \cite{43, 202}. Propagating primitive gradient errors ($\sigma_{U_z}^2$) into the gradient Richardson number variance yields the sensitivity relation \cite{43, 52}:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{Ri_g, U_z}^2 = \left( \frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial U_z} \right)^2 \sigma_{U_z}^2 = 4 \left( \frac{g}{\theta_v} \right)^2 \frac{\theta_{v,z}^2}{U_z^6} \sigma_{U_z}^2. \label{eq:noise_blowup}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:noise_blowup} proves that gradient uncertainty is amplified elementwise as **$U_z^{-6}$** in the local variance \cite{43, 52}.

If we calculate the Richardson profile naively before smoothing, this $U_z^{-6}$ scaling causes measurement noise to explode near zero-shear zones, creating massive unphysical spikes in the raw $Ri_g$ profile \cite{34, 190}. Applying the smoothing filter $M_\delta$ afterward to these extreme spikes causes the regularized curve to "bend" artificially to satisfy the discrepancy fit, manufacturing a smooth, quadratic "knee" in physical space \cite{190}. This visual knee is a pure numerical artifact of the post-diagnostic filter's struggle to smooth out low-shear singularities, rather than a physical transition or a coordinate fold of the underlying boundary layer \cite{190, 213}.

\subsection{Operational Primitive-Field Regularization}

To eliminate these non-commutation artifacts and ensure that the noise model remains physically grounded at the **sensor-variable level** (where instrument errors are well-characterized), the GSPT pipeline must execute the **pre-diagnostic regularization path** \cite{182, 191, 195}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_g^{\text{pre-smooth}} = \mathcal{Q} \circ D_{\Delta z} \circ M_\delta [u, v, \theta_v]. \label{eq:pre_smooth_path}
\end{equation}
Under the pre-diagnostic path \eqref{eq:pre_smooth_path}, the regularization filter $M_\delta$ is applied directly to the observed primitive variables prior to spatial differentiation and quotient division \cite{182, 239}. The regularized Richardson profile is constructed as \cite{182, 239, 296}:
\begin{equation}
    Ri_g^{(\delta)}(z) = \frac{\displaystyle \frac{g}{M_\delta\theta_v(z)} D_{\Delta z}\left( M_\delta \theta_v(z) \right)}{\left[ D_{\Delta z}\left( M_\delta u(z) \right) \right]^2 + \left[ D_{\Delta z}\left( M_\delta v(z) \right) \right]^2}, \label{eq:Ri_g_regularized}
\end{equation}
where $M_\delta u$, $M_\delta v$, and $M_\delta \theta_v$ are the Tikhonov-smoothed primitive profiles, each solved independently using Morozov's discrepancy principle based on their respective instrument standard deviations ($\sigma_U, \sigma_\theta$) \cite{191, 195}.

By restricting the smoothing operator to the primitive fields, we ensure that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The noise filtering is applied to variables where instrument errors are linear and Gaussian, avoiding non-Gaussian discrepancy jumps in $Ri$-space \cite{161, 163}.
    \item The analytical derivatives are computed directly from the regularized primitive splines, avoiding finite-difference discretization stencils that amplify noise near zero-shear regions \cite{24, 160}.
    \item Downstream diagnostic sensitivity ($\kappa_\zeta$) and coordinate fold ratios remain uncoupled from visual filtering artifacts, isolating true physical SBL transitions from numerical processing distortions \cite{64, 71}.
\end{enumerate}

A chronic issue in the development of Single-Column Models (SCMs) and numerical weather prediction (NWP) boundary-layer schemes is the practice of parameter tuning under stable conditions \cite{149}. When a model's simulated profiles of wind and temperature diverge from observations, developers typically adjust the empirical constants (e.g., $\beta_m, \beta_h$) within the Monin-Obukhov stability functions ($\phi_m, \phi_h$) to minimize the profile misfit \cite{149, 164}.

In many cases, the physical-space curvature misfit is not driven by an incorrect local stability closure, but rather by unresolved vertical flux divergence ($L'(z) \neq 0$, $L''(z) \neq 0$) or coarse vertical grid spacing that fails to capture fine-scale tower gradients \cite{164, 168}. \begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\subsection{The Total Curvature Residual ($\Delta_{\text{SCM}}$)}

Let $Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z)$ represent the vertical spatial second derivative of the gradient Richardson number profile reconstructed from observations using the primitive-field Tikhonov-Morozov regularization pipeline detailed in Section III \cite{158}. Let $Ri_{zz}^{\text{SCM}}(z)$ represent the corresponding vertical second derivative extracted from SCM output mapped onto the same vertical coordinate grid \cite{168}.

The \textit{Total Curvature Residual} at height $z$ is defined as \cite{158, 194}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z) \equiv Ri_{zz}^{\text{SCM}}(z) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z). \label{eq:total_residual_def}
\end{equation}
To determine whether a non-zero $\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z)$ represents a failure of the local turbulence parameterization or a geometric projection artifact, we must decompose this residual into independent, operational components \cite{158, 195}.

\subsection{Operational Definitions of the Partitioned Components}

To make \eqref{eq:scm_residual_partition} mathematically testable rather than merely descriptive, each error term must be defined operationally relative to a reference coordinate mapping \cite{195}. We define the following three geometric objects on the tower coordinate grid \cite{208}:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{G}_{\zeta} &\equiv \left( \zeta(z), \zeta_z(z), \zeta_{zz}(z) \right), \label{eq:geo_coord} \\
    \mathcal{G}_{Ri} &\equiv \left( R_\zeta(\zeta), R_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta), C_{\text{constitutive}}, C_{\text{mapping}} \right), \label{eq:geo_const} \\
    \mathcal{O}_{Ri} &\equiv \left( Ri(z), Ri_{zz}(z), \tau_{\Delta z}(z) \right). \label{eq:geo_obs}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{1. Constitutive Closure Failure ($\Delta_{\text{closure}}$)}
The \textit{Constitutive Closure Failure} measures the direct error introduced by the empirical similarity functions of the model \cite{168, 195}. It is operationally defined by projecting the model's similarity closure relation, $R^{\text{model}}(\zeta)$, onto the **observed, well-conditioned coordinate mapping** $\mathcal{G}_{\zeta}^{\text{obs}}(z)$, and subtracting the observed curvature \cite{195}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}(z)\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{obs}}(z) \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{model}}\left(\zeta^{\text{obs}}(z)\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z) \right) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{obs}}(z). \label{eq:delta_closure_operational}
\end{equation}
By keeping the coordinate geometry fixed to the observed state ($\zeta^{\text{obs}}, \zeta_z^{\text{obs}}, \zeta_{zz}^{\text{obs}}$), $\Delta_{\text{closure}}(z)$ isolates the performance of the local stability functions (e.g., comparing Businger-Dyer against Grachev et al. (2007)) under identical physical forcing \cite{168}. If $|\Delta_{\text{closure}}(z)| \approx 0$, the empirical stability functions are structurally correct \cite{168}.

\subsubsection{2. Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion ($\Delta_{\text{geom}}$)}
The \textit{Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion} measures the error generated by the model's failure to resolve the vertical structure of turbulent fluxes \cite{168, 195}. The vertical profiles of momentum and heat fluxes govern the local Obukhov length profile, $L(z)$, and its spatial derivatives, which in turn define the coordinate mapping curvature $\zeta_{zz}$ \cite{153, 189}.

We define $\Delta_{\text{geom}}(z)$ by projecting the **observed similarity closure** $R^{\text{obs}}(\zeta)$ onto the SCM's simulated coordinate mapping $\mathcal{G}_{\zeta}^{\text{model}}(z)$, and subtracting the observed reference mapping \cite{195}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) \equiv \left( R_{\zeta\zeta}^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}(z)\right) \left[ \zeta_z^{\text{model}}(z) \right]^2 + R_\zeta^{\text{obs}}\left(\zeta^{\text{model}}(z)\right) \zeta_{zz}^{\text{model}}(z) \right) - \left( C_{\text{constitutive}}^{\text{obs}}(z) + C_{\text{mapping}}^{\text{obs}}(z) \right), \label{eq:delta_geom_operational}
\end{equation}
which, near coordinate turning points where mapping curvature dominates ($C_{\text{mapping}} \gg C_{\text{constitutive}}$), simplifies to the GSPT transition-surface difference \cite{167, 209}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) \approx \mathcal{R}_{\text{coord}}^{\text{model}}(z) - \mathcal{R}_{\text{coord}}^{\text{obs}}(z), \label{eq:delta_geom_simple}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}_{\text{coord}} \equiv R_\zeta \zeta_{zz}$ represents the mapping curvature profile \cite{145, 185}. If $|\Delta_{\text{geom}}(z)| \gg 0$, it indicates that the model's simulated vertical flux divergence or mixing length profile is over-smoothed, distorting the coordinate grid transformation and shifting the height of the coordinate fold locus ($z_{\text{fold}}$) \cite{168}.

\subsubsection{3. Discretization and Grid-Scale Artifact ($\tau_{\Delta z}$)}
Coarse vertical grid spacing in SCMs introduces systematic truncation errors when approximating continuous spatial derivatives \cite{168, 206}. The \textit{Discretization Artifact} is the residual difference between the discrete approximation of curvature on the model's vertical grid and its continuum limit resolved on the high-resolution tower grid \cite{168, 195}:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\Delta z}(z) \equiv Ri_{zz}^{\Delta z}(z) - Ri_{zz}^{\text{continuum}}(z), \label{eq:tau_discretization_operational}
\end{equation}
where $Ri_{zz}^{\Delta z}(z)$ is evaluated using the model's discrete spatial difference operator $D_{2,\Delta z}$ \cite{206}. We formalize the localized truncation error using a leading-order Taylor expansion on a vertical grid spacing of $\Delta z$ \cite{168, 206}:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\Delta z}(z) = \frac{(\Delta z)^2}{12} \frac{\partial^4 Ri_g}{\partial z^4} + \mathcal{O}\left( (\Delta z)^4 \right). \label{eq:taylor_truncation_error}
\end{equation}
By computing $\tau_{\Delta z}(z)$ directly from the fourth spatial derivative of our regularized primitive splines, we can quantitatively assess whether a curvature misfit is simply a numerical grid-resolution artifact rather than a physical parameterization error \cite{168}.

**Richardson number vertical curvature (\\(Ri_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2}\\)) is an explicit diagnostic that can be used to improve current numerical weather prediction (NWP) and Single-Column Models (SCMs) by preventing "compensating errors" during model calibration.**

When simulated SBL profiles of wind and temperature misrepresent observations, developers conventionally attempt to "tune" the empirical constants (e.g., \\(\beta_m, \beta_h\\)) in their Monin-Obukhov similarity functions (\\(\phi_m, \phi_h\\)). However, profile inflections—visual "knees" in physical space—are frequently coordinate-mapping features ("Fold Illusions") driven by vertical flux divergence and zero-shear limits rather than true parameterization failures.

Applying **Generalized Similarity Profile Theory (GSPT)** to decompose vertical Richardson profile curvature allows SCM developers to systematically isolate the precise physical subsystems requiring calibration:

### 1. The Curvature Error-Partitioning Schema

GSPT operationally partitions the total model-observation curvature residual (\\(\Delta_{\text{SCM}}\\)) into three independent, testable components to guide SCM modifications:
\\[\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z) = \Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) + \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) + \tau_{\Delta z}(z)\\]

* **Constitutive Failure (\\(\Delta_{\text{closure}}\\)):** Measures the direct inadequacy of the model's empirical similarity functions.
  * _Mathematical Signature:_ \\(|C_{\text{const}}^{\text{obs}} - C_{\text{const}}^{\text{model}}| \gg 0\\) while \\(\Delta_{\text{geom}} \approx 0\\).
  * _Root Physical Cause:_ An incorrect asymptotic stability limit or a missing critical Richardson cutoff in the model.
  * _Corrective SCM Action:_ Swap the local boundary-layer closure scheme, such as transitioning from standard Businger-Dyer functions to non-singular Grachev et al. (2007) physics.
* **Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion (\\(\Delta_{\text{geom}}\\)):** Measures how poorly the model represents the vertical structure of turbulent fluxes.
  * _Mathematical Signature:_ \\(|C_{\text{coord}}^{\text{obs}} - C_{\text{coord}}^{\text{model}}| \gg 0\\) while \\(\Delta_{\text{closure}} \approx 0\\).
  * _Root Physical Cause:_ Excessive vertical mixing over-smooths heat and momentum flux divergence profiles (\\(L', L''\\)).
  * _Corrective SCM Action:_ **Do not alter the local similarity functions (\\(\phi_m, \phi_h\\)).** Instead, retune vertical mixing lengths (\\(l_m\\)), sub-grid turbulent dissipation rates, or non-local transport closures to restore the correct physical flux-divergence curvature.
* **Discretization Artifact (\\(\tau_{\Delta z}\\)):** Quantifies the purely numerical errors introduced by SCM grid resolution.
  * _Mathematical Signature:_ Model error scales with vertical grid spacing.
  * _Root Physical Cause:_ Coarse vertical grid spacing fails to capture fine-scale non-linearities in near-surface gradients.
  * _Corrective SCM Action:_ Refine the vertical grid layout, or apply a Taylor truncation correction (\\(\frac{(\Delta z)^2}{12}\frac{\partial^4 Ri_g}{\partial z^4}\\)) to the observation grid prior to comparison.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation of Component Diagnostics}

The curvature terms are computed directly from the log-space regularized primitive fields $\tilde{u}, \tilde{v}, \tilde{\theta}_v$ and their corresponding stability scale $L(z)$:

\begin{align}
C_{\text{const}}(z) &\equiv \frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} \left[ \frac{z}{L(z)} \phi_h\left(\frac{z}{L(z)}\right) \phi_m^{-2}\left(\frac{z}{L(z)}\right) \right] \label{eq:C_const} \

\end{align}

Where $L'(z)$ and $L''(z)$ represent the first and second vertical derivatives of the local Obukhov length, isolating the physical geometric distortion from the local stability closure response.

### The Two Critical Mathematical Mismatches

#### 1. The \\(C_{\text{const}}(z)\\) Derivative Mismatch

In your draft, you define the constitutive curvature as:

* **The Issue:** Taking the _total_ second derivative with respect to the physical coordinate \\(z\\) evaluates to the entire physical Richardson curvature \\(Ri_{zz}\\). Expanding this total derivative via the multi-variable chain rule yields:
    \\[\frac{d^2 Ri_g}{dz^2} = R_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2 + R_\zeta \zeta_{zz}\\]
* **The Consequence:** By defining \\(C_{\text{const}}(z)\\) as the total physical derivative, you make it identical to \\(Ri_{zz}\\). * **The GSPT Correction:** In GSPT, the constitutive curvature is strictly the _partial_ curvature of the empirical similarity relation in coordinate space, mapped to physical space via the coordinate Jacobian squared:
    \\[C_{\text{const}}(z) \equiv R_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta) \left[ \zeta_z(z) \right]^2\\]

#### 2. The \\(C_{\text{coord}}(z)\\) Mapping Approximation

Vertical profile curvature—specifically the second vertical derivative of the gradient Richardson number ($Ri_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2}$)—is a diagnostic in atmospheric boundary layer (ABL) meteorology used to isolate numerical and physical error sources in Single-Column Models (SCMs) and Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) parameterizations.

**The "Compensating Error" Pitfall**
When simulated stable boundary layer (SBL) wind and temperature profiles misalign with observations, model developers traditionally adjust empirical Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) parameters ($\beta_m, \beta_h$ in $\phi_m, \phi_h$). However, profile inflections ("knees") in physical space are frequently coordinate-mapping artifacts ("Fold Illusions") driven by vertical flux divergence ($L', L''$) and zero-shear limits rather than true parameterization failures. Adjusting local similarity functions to fix these inflections introduces compensating errors elsewhere in the boundary layer.

**GSPT Curvature Error-Partitioning Framework**
Generalized Similarity Profile Theory (GSPT) operationally decomposes the total SCM curvature residual $\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z)$ into three orthogonal error mechanisms:

$$\Delta_{\text{SCM}}(z) = \Delta_{\text{closure}}(z) + \Delta_{\text{geom}}(z) + \tau_{\Delta z}(z)$$

**Curvature Diagnostic Components**

* **Constitutive Failure ($\Delta_{\text{closure}}$):** Direct inadequacy in the local similarity functions ($\phi_m, \phi_h$).
* _Signature:_ $\vert{}C_{\text{const}}^{\text{obs}} - C_{\text{const}}^{\text{model}}\vert{} \gg 0$ while $\Delta_{\text{geom}} \approx 0$.
* _Physical Cause:_ Incorrect asymptotic stability limits or a missing critical Richardson cutoff ($Ri_c$).
* _Corrective Action:_ Swap the local closure scheme (e.g., transition from Businger-Dyer to non-singular Grachev et al. functions).

* **Flux-Coordinate Geometric Distortion ($\Delta_{\text{geom}}$):** Misrepresentation of the vertical structure of turbulent fluxes.
* _Signature:_ $\vert{}C_{\text{coord}}^{\text{obs}} - C_{\text{coord}}^{\text{model}}\vert{} \gg 0$ while $\Delta_{\text{closure}} \approx 0$.
* _Physical Cause:_ Excessive vertical mixing over-smoothing local Obukhov length profiles ($L(z)$) and flux divergence ($L', L''$).
* _Corrective Action:_ **Do not alter $\phi_m$ or $\phi_h$.** Retune mixing lengths ($l_m$), TKE dissipation rates, or non-local transport closures.

* **Discretization Artifact ($\tau_{\Delta z}$):** Purely numerical truncation error.
* _Signature:_ Residual scales directly with vertical grid resolution $\Delta z$.
* _Physical Cause:_ Coarse vertical spacing failing to resolve sharp near-surface non-linear gradients.
* _Corrective Action:_ Refine the vertical grid or apply Taylor truncation corrections ($\frac{(\Delta z)^2}{12}\frac{\partial^4 Ri_g}{\partial z^4}$) to observations.

**Mathematical Formulation**
GSPT isolates physical geometry from closure physics by expanding the curvature diagnostic into:

$$C_{\text{const}}(z) = R_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta) \left[ \zeta_z(z) \right]^2, \quad C_{\text{coord}}(z) = R_\zeta(\zeta) \zeta_{zz}(z)$$

where $R(\zeta) \equiv \zeta \phi_h \phi_m^{-2}$ is the empirical closure function, $\zeta = z / L(z)$, and the coordinate derivatives ($\zeta_z, \zeta_{zz}$) depend analytically on local Obukhov scale gradients ($L', L''$).

This formulation provides a systematic roadmap for model tuning by distinguishing between local turbulence physics, vertical flux geometry, and grid resolution errors.

**Nieuwstadt’s local scaling hypothesis (1984)** is a foundational physical framework used to extend similarity theory beyond the shallow, constant-flux surface layer across the entire vertical extent of the stably stratified atmospheric boundary layer (SBL).

To understand its physical significance and mathematical consequences for stable boundary layers, it is best analyzed by contrasting it with classical Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST):

### 1. From Constant-Flux to Height-Dependent Scaling

Classic Monin-Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) is strictly formulated for the surface layer, where turbulent fluxes of heat and momentum are assumed to be approximately constant with height. In this near-surface layer, the vertical gradient of the Monin-Obukhov length is zero (\\(L' = 0\\)), implying a spatially uniform scaling length.

To parameterize the outer regions of the stable boundary layer, Nieuwstadt proposed replacing surface-layer scaling parameters with **local turbulent fluxes** measured at height \\(z\\). Under local scaling, the Monin-Obukhov length becomes a dynamically varying, height-dependent physical scale, \\(L(z)\\), defined as:
\\[L(z) = \frac{-\tau(z)^{3/2}}{\kappa \frac{g}{\theta_0} \overline{w'\theta_v'}(z)}\\]
where:

* \\(\tau(z)\\) is the local vertical shear stress (representing local momentum flux),
* \\(\overline{w'\theta_v'}(z)\\) is the local kinematic virtual potential temperature flux,
* \\(\kappa\\) is the von Kármán constant, and
* \\(g/\theta_0\\) is the buoyancy parameter.

### 2. Geometrical Impact on Vertical SBL Profiles

Under Nieuwstadt’s local scaling, the local gradient Richardson number (\\(Ri_g\\)) can still be related to a non-dimensional coordinate via the similarity relation \\(Ri_g = R(\zeta)\\). However, this relation must be evaluated using the **local similarity coordinate**, \\(\zeta(z) = z/L(z)\\).

Because turbulent fluxes systematically decay with height under stable stratification, the local Monin-Obukhov length varies continuously with altitude. This introduces non-zero vertical flux divergence (\\(L'(z) \neq 0\\)) and vertical flux curvature (\\(L''(z) \neq 0\\)) across the boundary layer.

Differentiating the local coordinate mapping twice with respect to physical height \\(z\\) yields:
\\[\zeta_z = \frac{1 - \zeta L'}{L}\\]
\\[\zeta_{zz} = -\frac{L''}{L}\zeta - 2\frac{L'}{L}\zeta_z\\]

These equations demonstrate that **vertical coordinate curvature (\\(\zeta_{zz} \neq 0\\)) is a direct, physically genuine consequence of vertically varying fluxes and flux divergence**.

### 3. Implications for SCM Diagnostics and MOST Breakdown

The height-dependence of the local Obukhov length has profound implications for numerical weather prediction (NWP) and Single-Column Models (SCMs):

* **Decoupling Visual Knees from Physical Transitions:** Because local fluxes vary vertically, mapping the local similarity coordinate back to physical vertical space warp profiles, manufacturing sharp, visual inflections ("knees") in physical space. GSPT curvature decomposition reveals that near zero-shear zones, these inflections are often coordinate-mapping features driven by local flux geometry rather than actual physical collapses of turbulent mixing.
* **Guidance for Model Developers:** Citing Nieuwstadt’s local scaling allows modelers to distinguish between failures in local empirical stability functions (\\(\phi_m, \phi_h\\)) and failures in vertical transport parameterizations. If an SCM's coordinate curvature diverges from observations, the model's vertical flux-divergence profile is over-smoothed, signaling that developers must adjust mixing lengths or sub-grid transport closures rather than "tuning" local Monin-Obukhov parameters.

# Coordinate Curvature Under Nieuwstadt Local Scaling: A Formal Treatment

## 1. Setup and Definitions

Let $z \in I = (0, z_{\text{top}})$ denote height above the surface within the stable boundary layer (SBL). Let $\tau(z) > 0$ denote the local vertical shear stress (momentum flux) and $H(z) \equiv \overline{w'\theta_v'}(z)$ denote the local kinematic virtual potential temperature flux, with $H(z) < 0$ under stable stratification. Let $\kappa$ be the von Kármán constant and $g/\theta_0$ the buoyancy parameter.

**Definition 1 (Local Obukhov length and its inverse).**
$$
L(z) \;=\; \frac{-\tau(z)^{3/2}}{\kappa \dfrac{g}{\theta_0}\,H(z)}, \qquad
\chi(z) \;\equiv\; \frac{1}{L(z)} \;=\; -\,\kappa\,\frac{g}{\theta_0}\,\frac{H(z)}{\tau(z)^{3/2}}.
$$

**Definition 2 (Local similarity coordinate).**
$$
\zeta(z) \;=\; \frac{z}{L(z)} \;=\; z\,\chi(z).
$$

**Standing assumption (A1).** On any subinterval of interest, $\tau(z) > 0$ (turbulence is locally active) and $\chi \in C^2$, except possibly at isolated points explicitly treated in Lemma 4.

---

## 2. Coordinate Derivatives

**Proposition 1 (Coordinate derivatives in inverse-profile form).**
Under (A1), for all $z \in I$,
$$
\zeta_z(z) = \chi(z) + z\,\chi'(z), \tag{1}
$$
$$
\zeta_{zz}(z) = 2\chi'(z) + z\,\chi''(z). \tag{2}
$$

_Proof._ Immediate from Definition 2 by the product rule:
$$
\zeta_z = \frac{d}{dz}\big(z\chi\big) = \chi + z\chi',
$$
$$
\zeta_{zz} = \frac{d}{dz}\big(\chi + z\chi'\big) = \chi' + \big(\chi' + z\chi''\big) = 2\chi' + z\chi''. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

**Remark.** Equations (1)–(2) are algebraically equivalent to the $L(z)$-based forms $\zeta_z = (1-\zeta L')/L$ and $\zeta_{zz} = -(L''/L)\zeta - 2(L'/L)\zeta_z$, but the $\chi$-parametrization removes the removable singularity at $L\to\infty$ (Lemma 4 below), which is why it is preferred for asymptotic analysis near neutral or turbulence-collapse conditions.

---

## 3. Exclusion of Coordinate Folds from Constant-Flux Regions

Define a **coordinate fold point** $z_* \in I$ as a point where $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$; it is **nondegenerate** if additionally $\zeta_{zz}(z_*) \neq 0$.

**Proposition 2 (Structural exclusion of folds under constant flux).**
Let $J \subset I$ be a subinterval on which $\chi$ is constant, $\chi \equiv \chi_0 \neq 0$ (equivalently: $\tau^{3/2}/H$ is fixed, i.e. $J$ is a classical constant-flux surface layer, and stratification is non-neutral so $\chi_0 \neq 0$). Then $\zeta_z(z) \neq 0$ for every $z \in J$; hence $J$ contains no coordinate fold point.

_Proof._ On $J$, $\chi' \equiv 0$, so by (1), $\zeta_z(z) = \chi_0$ for all $z \in J$. Since $\chi_0 \neq 0$ by hypothesis, $\zeta_z$ never vanishes on $J$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 2.1 (Folds require active flux curvature).**
Any coordinate fold observed in a profile ($\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$ for some $z_*$) must lie outside every constant-flux subinterval; equivalently, its existence certifies $\chi'(z_*) \neq 0$ somewhere in a neighborhood of $z_*$, i.e. genuine vertical flux divergence.

**Corollary 2.2 (Nondegeneracy condition at a fold).**
Let $z_*$ satisfy $\zeta_z(z_*) = 0$, so from (1),
$$
\chi(z_*) = -z_*\,\chi'(z_*). \tag{3}
$$
Evaluating (2) at $z_*$ gives, without further simplification (the two equations share no common factor to cancel),
$$
\zeta_{zz}(z_*) = 2\chi'(z_*) + z_*\chi''(z_*). \tag{4}
$$
If $\chi'(z_*) \neq 0$, nondegeneracy ($\zeta_{zz}(z_*)\neq 0$) holds generically. If $\chi'(z_*) = 0$, then (3) forces $\chi(z_*) = 0$ as well (a simultaneous coordinate fold _and_ a locally neutral point), and nondegeneracy reduces to the single condition $\chi''(z_*) \neq 0$.

_(This corrects a common shorthand in which (3) is described as being "substituted into" (4); the two expressions do not algebraically interact — (4) is simply (2) evaluated at the point identified by (3). The physical content is entirely in Proposition 2 and Corollary 2.1: folds cannot occur where the flux ratio is constant.)_

## 4. The Central Result: Curvature Decomposition of the Diagnostic Profile

This section makes explicit the mechanism referenced informally as "GSPT curvature decomposition" — the separation of an observed physical-space inflection into a genuine closure-curvature term and a coordinate-mapping term.

Let $R(\cdot)$ be the local similarity function such that $Ri_g(z) = R(\zeta(z))$, with $R \in C^2$.

**Proposition 3 (Curvature decomposition).**
$$
\frac{d\,Ri_g}{dz} = R'(\zeta)\,\zeta_z, \tag{5}
$$
$$
\frac{d^2 Ri_g}{dz^2} = \underbrace{R''(\zeta)\,\zeta_z^{\,2}}_{\text{intrinsic closure curvature}} \;+\; \underbrace{R'(\zeta)\,\zeta_{zz}}_{\text{coordinate-mapping curvature}}. \tag{6}
$$

_Proof._ Direct chain rule applied twice to the composition $Ri_g(z) = R(\zeta(z))$:
$$
\frac{d}{dz}R(\zeta(z)) = R'(\zeta)\zeta_z,
$$
$$
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}R(\zeta(z)) = \frac{d}{dz}\big[R'(\zeta)\zeta_z\big] = R''(\zeta)\zeta_z\cdot\zeta_z + R'(\zeta)\zeta_{zz} = R''(\zeta)\zeta_z^2 + R'(\zeta)\zeta_{zz}. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

**Corollary 3.1 (Diagnostic criterion: physical vs. coordinate "knee").**
An observed physical-space inflection ($d^2Ri_g/dz^2$ large in magnitude at some $z_0$) is attributable predominantly to:

* **a genuine closure failure** if $R''(\zeta(z_0))$ is large relative to $R'(\zeta(z_0))\zeta_{zz}(z_0)$ — i.e. the stability function $\phi_m,\phi_h$ (via $R$) itself has strong curvature in $\zeta$-space;
* **a coordinate-mapping artifact** if instead $R'(\zeta(z_0))\zeta_{zz}(z_0)$ dominates while $R''(\zeta(z_0))$ is modest — i.e. the apparent inflection is manufactured by the nonlinearity of $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ itself, per Corollary 2.1 traceable to vertical flux divergence rather than to $\phi_m,\phi_h$.

This gives model developers an operational test: compute $\zeta_z,\zeta_{zz}$ from the model's own flux-divergence profile via Proposition 1, then check which term in (6) dominates at the location of an observed SCM/observation mismatch, before retuning $\phi_m,\phi_h$.

## 5. Regularity at a Turbulence-Collapse Height

Proposition 2's fold-exclusion argument and the $\chi$-parametrization of Proposition 1 remove the singularity associated with the _pure neutral limit at fixed nonzero $\tau$_ ($H\to 0$, $\tau$ finite $\Rightarrow \chi\to 0$ smoothly, provided $H$ vanishes at a suitable rate — see below). They do **not** automatically resolve the distinct case of a **turbulence-collapse height** $z_c$, at which _both_ fluxes vanish together, $\tau(z)\to 0$ and $H(z)\to 0$ as $z \to z_c^-$. Here $\chi$ is a $0/0$ form, and regularity is not automatic.

**Lemma 4 (Sufficient condition for $C^2$ regularity of $\chi$ at $z_c$).**
Suppose, as $z \to z_c^-$,
$$
\tau(z) \sim A\,(z_c - z)^a, \qquad H(z) \sim -B\,(z_c-z)^b, \qquad A,B>0,\ a,b>0,
$$
(the standing power-law asymptotic assumption on the flux profiles near collapse). Then
$$
\chi(z) \sim C\,(z_c-z)^{p}, \qquad C = \kappa\frac{g}{\theta_0}\frac{B}{A^{3/2}} > 0, \qquad p \equiv b - \tfrac{3}{2}a.
$$
Consequently:

* $\chi(z_c) = 0$ (finite limit) $\iff p > 0$, i.e. $b > \tfrac{3}{2}a$;
* $\chi'(z_c) = 0$ requires $p \geq 1$;
* $\chi(z), \chi'(z), \chi''(z)$ all remain bounded and $\chi,\chi'\to 0$ at $z_c$ (full $C^2$ regularity with vanishing first two derivatives) requires

$$
p = b - \tfrac{3}{2}a \;\geq\; 2. \tag{7}
$$

_Proof sketch._ Differentiate the asymptotic form termwise: $\chi' \sim -Cp(z_c-z)^{p-1}$, $\chi'' \sim Cp(p-1)(z_c-z)^{p-2}$. Each remains bounded (and $\to 0$ for the first two) as $z\to z_c^-$ iff its exponent is $\geq 0$ (resp. $>0$), giving $p-1\geq 1 \Leftrightarrow p\geq2$ for $\chi''$ to stay bounded, which is the binding constraint. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (implication for the pure near-neutral sub-case).** Setting $a=0$ (momentum flux held finite, only buoyancy flux vanishing) reduces condition (7) to $b \geq 2$: it is _not sufficient_ for $H(z)\to 0$ merely as $z\to z_c$; $H$ must vanish at least **quadratically** in $(z_c - z)$ for the coordinate mapping to remain fully $C^2$-regular there. Taking the supremum over all nodes yields the global convergence limit:
\begin{equation}
    \max_{1 \le j \le N_z} \left| z_j - z(y_j) \right| \le \frac{M}{2 (N_z - 1)^2} = \mathcal{O}\left( N_z^{-2} \right). \label{eq:convergence_proof_complete}
\end{equation}
This completes the proof. Under smooth local fluxes, the discrete physical grid converges quadratically to the continuous coordinate geometry as vertical resolution increases \cite{CoreMathRevisions}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fenichel Invariant Manifold Slaving and Stiffness Regularization}

When NWP models integrate the prognostic TKE equation near the critical manifold boundary $\mathcal{C}_0$, the fast turbulent eigenvalue $\lambda_f \equiv \frac{\partial f}{\partial E}$ approaches zero \cite{BelowFastSlow, 186}. In this regime, low-order Galerkin modal projections undergo a spurious, nonphysical modal buildup \cite{BelowFastSlow}.

Consider a two-mode spectral discretization where the primary mode $u_1$ represents geostrophically balanced slow shear, and the secondary mode $u_2$ represents high-frequency fast TKE \cite{BelowFastSlow, 112}. The unslaved fast-slow modal projection satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{u}_2 = \lambda_2 u_2 - \gamma u_1^2, \qquad \lambda_2 < 0, \ \gamma > 0. \label{eq:unslaved_fast_ode}
\end{equation}
If the secondary mode is integrated explicitly, the algebraic feedback loop converts the quadratic coupling into an unphysical cubic production drive ($\dot{u}_1 \ge \sigma u_1^3 - C u_1$), triggering a spurious superlinear finite-time blowup at $t_{\text{blowup}} \le \frac{1}{2 \sigma u_1(0)^2}$ \cite{BelowFastSlow, 112}.

To eliminate this computational singularity, we apply \textit{Fenichel Invariant Manifold Slaving} \cite{fenichel1979, BelowFastSlow}. We replace the prognostic ODE \eqref{eq:unslaved_fast_ode} with the algebraic manifold parameterization \cite{BelowFastSlow}:
\begin{equation}
    u_2 = \Phi(u_1, S) \equiv -\frac{c_{\text{cross}} u_1^2}{2 \left| \lambda_f(S) \right| + \delta_m}, \label{eq:fenichel_slaving_formulation}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{cross}} > 0$ is the spectral cross-coupling coefficient and $\delta_m \sim 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$ is a hyperbolic regularization parameter that bounds the manifold solver near the fold locus where $\lambda_f \to 0$ \cite{BelowFastSlow}.

\begin{theorem}[Global Boundedness of Slaved Galerkin Trajectories]
The algebraic substitution of the regularized Fenichel slaving manifold \eqref{eq:fenichel_slaving_formulation} into the primary mode equation eliminates the spurious cubic production feedback term $\sigma u_1^3$ entirely, establishing a globally bounded, stable attractor on the slow manifold $\mathcal{S}_\epsilon$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let the prognostic equation for the primary slow shear mode $u_1$ be governed by \cite{BelowFastSlow}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{u}_1 = G - c_{\text{shear}} u_1 - \beta u_1 u_2 - K_d u_1^3, \label{eq:u1_governing}
\end{equation}
where $G > 0$ represents external geostrophic forcing, $c_{\text{shear}} > 0$ represents linear damping, $\beta > 0$ represents modal cross-coupling, and $K_d > 0$ represents physical spectral dissipation \cite{BelowFastSlow}.

Substituting the regularized Fenichel slaving relation \eqref{eq:fenichel_slaving_formulation} into \eqref{eq:u1_governing} yields the closed, single-mode dynamical system:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{u}_1 = G - c_{\text{shear}} u_1 - \beta u_1 \left( -\frac{c_{\text{cross}} u_1^2}{2 \left| \lambda_f(S) \right| + \delta_m} \right) - K_d u_1^3. \label{eq:u1_closed_system}
\end{equation}
Collecting terms of equivalent powers of $u_1$ simplifies the closed system to:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{u}_1 = G - c_{\text{shear}} u_1 + \left( \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{2 \left| \lambda_f(S) \right| + \delta_m} - K_d \right) u_1^3. \label{eq:u1_grouped}
\end{equation}

To evaluate the stability of \eqref{eq:u1_grouped} in the large-amplitude limit, we analyze the sign of the cubic coefficient \cite{BelowFastSlow}. Let $\mu(S) \equiv \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{2 \left| \lambda_f(S) \right| + \delta_m}$ represent the regularized production feedback rate \cite{BelowFastSlow}. Because the denominator is strictly regularized by the positive threshold $\delta_m > 0$, the feedback rate is globally bounded from above \cite{BelowFastSlow}:
\begin{equation}
    \mu(S) \le \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{\delta_m} < \infty \qquad \forall S \in \mathbb{R}. \label{eq:mu_upper_bound}
\end{equation}

By choosing a physical spectral dissipation parameter $K_d$ that satisfies the sub-critical dissipation constraint:
\begin{equation}
    K_d > \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{\delta_m}, \label{eq:subcritical_dissipation}
\end{equation}
the effective cubic coefficient remains strictly negative for all shear states \cite{BelowFastSlow}:
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda(S) \equiv \mu(S) - K_d \le \frac{\beta c_{\text{cross}}}{\delta_m} - K_d \equiv -\Lambda_0 < 0. \label{eq:lambda_strictly_negative}
\end{equation}

Substituting \eqref{eq:lambda_strictly_negative} back into the dynamical equation \eqref{eq:u1_grouped} yields the differential inequality:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{u}_1 \le G - c_{\text{shear}} u_1 - \Lambda_0 u_1^3. \label{eq:differential_inequality_complete}
\end{equation}
For any initial state $u_1(0) > 0$, the negative cubic term $-\Lambda_0 u_1^3$ dominates the geostrophic forcing $G$ for sufficiently large $u_1$. Defining the compact, invariant region:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K} = \left\{ u_1 \in \mathbb{R} : 0 \le u_1 \le \sqrt{\frac{G}{\Lambda_0}} + 1 \right\}, \label{eq:invariant_region_definition}
\end{equation}
any trajectory starting outside $\mathcal{K}$ has a strictly negative derivative ($\dot{u}_1 < 0$) and enters $\mathcal{K}$ in finite time. Once inside $\mathcal{K}$, the trajectory remains trapped for all future times ($t > 0$). This proves that the regularized Fenichel slaving solver eliminates the spurious cubic blowup, regularizing solver stiffness and establishing a globally bounded, stable attractor on the slow manifold \cite{BelowFastSlow}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coupled Solver Stability and CFL Guarantees}

In production-scale NWP systems, the maximum allowable time step $\Delta t$ is governed by the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) stability criterion of the coupled vertical transport solver \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}. Under standard SCM architectures, as the stable boundary layer collapses and vertical mixing vanishes, the grid spacing must contract to resolve sharp shear lines \cite{BelowFastSlow}.

By utilizing the GSPT bounded grid-stretching formulation and Fenichel slaving solver, we can guarantee that the coupled solver remains stable under a non-vanishing time-step constraint \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}:

\begin{proposition}[SCM Global CFL Stability Guarantee]
The maximum time step $\Delta t$ required to maintain numerical stability in the coupled TKE-shear solver remains strictly bounded from below by a non-zero physical limit, independent of the singular perturbation limit $\epsilon \to 0$ or the zero-shear limit $U_z \to 0$ \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t \ge \min\left( \Delta t_{\text{base}}, \; \frac{\Delta z_{\text{min}}}{2 \max(K_m, K_h)} \right) > 0, \label{eq:global_cfl_claim}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\text{base}}$ is the baseline atmospheric model step and $K_m$ and $K_h$ are the vertical turbulent exchange coefficients \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a vertical diffusion equation solved on a vertical grid mesh of spacing $\Delta z_j$ with a vertical exchange coefficient $K(z)$, the absolute numerical stability requirement of an explicit integration scheme is \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_j \le \frac{(\Delta z_j)^2}{2 K_j} \qquad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, N_z\}. \label{eq:explicit_stability_limit}
\end{equation}

Under the bounded grid-stretching formulation proved in Theorem 1, the vertical grid step $\Delta z_j$ is strictly bounded from below by $\Delta z_{\text{min}} > 0$, preventing local node collapse \cite{CoreMathRevisions}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta z_j \ge \Delta z_{\text{min}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (\Delta z_j)^2 \ge (\Delta z_{\text{min}})^2. \label{eq:grid_lower_bound}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, because the Fenichel slaving solver locks the high-frequency modal energy $u_2$ to the slow manifold, the maximum turbulent kinetic energy is globally bounded ($E \le E_{\text{max}} < \infty$). Since the turbulent exchange coefficients scale as $K_m = l_m \sqrt{E + \delta}$, the vertical exchange coefficients are globally bounded from above \cite{14, CoreMathRevisions}:
\begin{equation}
    \max(K_m, K_h) \le K_{\text{max}} < \infty. \label{eq:exchange_coeff_upper_bound}
\end{equation}

Substituting \eqref{eq:grid_lower_bound} and \eqref{eq:exchange_coeff_upper_bound} into \eqref{eq:explicit_stability_limit} yields the global minimum time-step limit:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_j \ge \frac{(\Delta z_{\text{min}})^2}{2 K_{\text{max}}} \equiv \Delta t_{\text{CFL}} > 0. \label{eq:cfl_lower_bound_proven}
\end{equation}
Because the denominator $K_{\text{max}}$ remains finite and the minimum vertical spacing $\Delta z_{\text{min}}$ is clamped, the CFL limit remains strictly bounded away from zero. This completes the proof. The coupled GSPT solver guarantees global numerical stability without requiring unphysically small time steps near turning points or SBL transition layers \cite{CoreMathRevisions, 132}.
\end{proof}
