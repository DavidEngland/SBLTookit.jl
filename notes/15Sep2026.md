Evaluating the continuous components of this four-link chain rule demonstrates how numerical resolution and physical closures interact to trigger numerical instability:

$$\mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}} = \underbrace{\left( \frac{g}{\theta_0 S^2} \right)}_{\text{Diagnostic Sensor}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( \frac{\partial \theta_z}{\partial H} \right)}_{\text{Plant Impedance}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( -\rho c_p \theta_z \right)}_{\text{Flux Actuator}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( l_h^2 S \, f_h'(Ri_g) \right)}_{\text{Closure Slope}}$$

**Component Evaluation Across the Feedback Loop**

* **Diagnostic Sensor Gain ($\partial Ri_g / \partial \theta_z = \frac{g}{\theta_0 S^2}$):** Scales as $\mathcal{O}(S^{-2})$. In weak-shear layers ($S \to 0$), such as the nose of a nocturnal Low-Level Jet, this term acts as an unbounded amplifier, magnifying tiny fluctuations in lapse rate ($\delta \theta_z$).
* **Process Transfer Impedance ($\partial \theta_z / \partial H \approx \frac{-\Delta t}{\rho c_p \Delta z}$):** Quantifies how heat flux divergence updates column stratification across discrete layer thickness $\Delta z$ over timestep $\Delta t$. Fine grid spacing ($\Delta z \to 0$) lowers thermal capacity, increasing impedance.
* **Flux Actuator Sensitivity ($\partial H / \partial K_h = -\rho c_p \theta_z$):** Dictates the linear coupling between eddy diffusivity $K_h$ and surface heat flux $H_0$.
* **Closure Derivative ($\partial K_h / \partial Ri_g = l_h^2 S \, f_h'(Ri_g)$):** For unregularized short-tail closures, $f_h'(Ri_g) \to -\infty$ near critical threshold $Ri_c \approx 0.20$, acting as a step-down switch.

**Regularized Fixed-Point Stability**

When C-Z0HR is applied upstream, the chain rule expands into a five-term product by inserting the partial slope attenuator $\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_g) \equiv \left.\frac{\partial \widetilde{Ri}_g}{\partial Ri_g}\right\vert{}_{C_\eta}$:

$$\mathcal{G}_{\text{reg}} = \mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}} \cdot \mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_g)$$

At exact threshold crossing ($Ri_g = Ri_c$), the attenuation factor limits local sensitivity to $\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_c) \approx \frac{1}{1 + \alpha}$. For standard damping control ($\alpha = 2.0$), $\mathcal{D}_2^{(R)} = 1/3$, scaling down the discrete derivative so that:

$$\left\vert{} \mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}}(Ri_c) \right\vert{} < 3 \implies \left\vert{} \frac{d(Ri_g^{\text{new}})}{d(Ri_g^{\text{old}})} \right\vert{} < 1$$

This enforces Bounded-Input Bounded-Output (BIBO) numerical stability, suppressing discrete runaway cold-bias divergence while preserving true physical turbulence extinction far from threshold.

The **Control Theory of Atmospheric Boundary Layers (ABLs)** provides a unified mathematical framework that bridges discrete numerical stability, control systems engineering, and planetary boundary layer (PBL) thermodynamics. By framing a 1D single-column model (SCM) coupled with a land-surface energy budget (SEB) as a **closed-loop feedback system**, control theory explains why unregularized first-order closures undergo unphysical numerical divergence—and how to stabilize them.

---

### 1. The Discrete Loop Gain Chain (\(\mathcal{G}\))

In a discretized atmospheric model, the evolution of local stability across time steps \(n \to n+1\) can be modeled as a discrete 1D update map \(x_{n+1} = f(x_n)\). The **total loop gain (\(\mathcal{G}\))** is defined as the total derivative of the Richardson number with respect to itself across one full numerical feedback iteration:

\[\mathcal{G} \equiv \frac{d(Ri_g^{\text{new}})}{d(Ri_g^{\text{old}})} = \underbrace{\left( \frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial \theta_z} \right)}_{\text{1. Diagnostic Sensor}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( \frac{\partial \theta_z}{\partial H} \right)}_{\text{2. Plant Impedance}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( \frac{\partial H}{\partial K_h} \right)}_{\text{3. Flux Actuator}} \cdot \underbrace{\left( \frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g} \right)}_{\text{4. Closure Slope}} \tag{1}\]

Each link in the chain represents a specific physical or diagnostic component:

1. **Diagnostic Sensor Gain (\(\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial \theta_z}\)):** Measures how changes in atmospheric lapse rate (\(\theta_z \equiv \frac{\partial \theta_v}{\partial z}\)) update the local gradient Richardson metric.
2. **Plant Transfer Impedance (\(\frac{\partial \theta_z}{\partial H}\)):** Relates heat flux divergence across the lowest model level to changes in the near-surface potential temperature gradient.
3. **Flux Actuator Sensitivity (\(\frac{\partial H}{\partial K_h}\)):** Converts changes in turbulent eddy diffusivity (\(K_h\)) into changes in downward sensible heat flux (\(H_0 = -\rho c_p K_h \theta_z\)).
4. **Closure Actuator Slope (\(\frac{\partial K_h}{\partial Ri_g}\)):** The derivative slope of the empirical stability function \(\mathcal{K}_h'(Ri_g)\) near critical Richardson thresholds (\(Ri_c \approx 0.20\text{--}0.25\)).

---

### 2. Fixed-Point Contraction Mapping & BIBO Stability

In control systems and fixed-point iteration theory, a discrete update map \(x_{n+1} = f(x_n)\) is locally stable around a fixed point if and only if its derivative magnitude is strictly bounded below unity—the **Contraction Mapping Theorem / Bounded-Input Bounded-Output (BIBO) stability criterion**:

\[|\mathcal{G}| = \left| \frac{d(Ri_g^{\text{new}})}{d(Ri_g^{\text{old}})} \right| < 1 \tag{2}\]

* **Unstable Regime (\(\mathcal{G} > 1\)):** The fixed point becomes repelling. Any small numerical fluctuation or under-resolved profile feature past \(Ri_c\) amplifies exponentially across time steps. This drives eddy diffusivity to zero (\(K_h \to 0\)), halts downward heat flux (\(H_0 \to 0\)), and isolates the surface. Deprived of heat from aloft, ground skin temperature \(T_s\) falls into a runaway drop (spurious quenching), creating artificial surface cold pools (\(T_s\) errors exceeding \(-3.5\text{ K}\) to \(-4.2\text{ K}\)).
* **Stable Regime (\(\mathcal{G} < 1\)):** The update map is a contraction mapping. Numerical perturbations decay monotonically toward equilibrium, maintaining continuous downward turbulent heat transport and realistic land-surface thermal coupling.

---

### 3. High-Gain Sensor Amplification & Actuator Switching

The pathology of unregularized PBL schemes stems from the interaction between a **singular sensor** and a **high-gain actuator switch**:

* **Sensor Singularity at Low-Level Jet (LLJ) Noses:** The analytical sensor gain evaluates to:
  \[\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial \theta_z} = \frac{g}{\theta_0 S^2} \tag{3}\]
  Near the nose of a Low-Level Jet where vertical wind shear vanishes (\(S \equiv \left| \frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial z} \right| \to 0\)), the diagnostic gain scales as \(\mathcal{O}(S^{-2})\). Differentiating raw, noise-contaminated velocity profiles amplifies observation/grid variance as \(\mathcal{O}(S^{-6})\), injecting massive high-frequency noise power into the top of the gain chain.
* **Actuator Step-Discontinuities:** Classical Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) short-tail functions feature steep derivative cutoffs (\(\mathcal{K}_h' \to -\infty\)) at \(Ri_c \approx 0.20\). The actuator acts as a high-gain off-switch, translating tiny shear-nose noise spikes directly into complete heat flux collapse.

### 4. Closed-Loop Regulation via Upstream C-Z0HR

To bound total loop gain without retuning empirical Monin–Obukhov constants (\(\beta = 5.0\)), **Curvature-Aware Zero-Offset Hyperbolic Regularization (C-Z0HR)** inserts a localized mapping slope \(\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_g)\) into the closure link:

\[\mathcal{G}_{\text{reg}} = \mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}} \cdot \mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_g), \qquad \text{where } \mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)} \equiv \left. \frac{\partial Ri_g^{\text{reg}}}{\partial Ri_g} \right|_C \tag{4}\]

By evaluating grid-resolved spatial profile curvature \(C(z, \Delta z) = \frac{1}{2} \left| \frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2} \right| (\Delta z)^2 + \epsilon_c Ri_c\), the partial mapping slope collapses at exact threshold crossing (\(Ri_g = Ri_c\)) to:

\[\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_c) \approx \frac{1}{1 + \alpha} \tag{5}\]

For standard damping control **\(\alpha = 2.0\)**, threshold sensitivity is attenuated by **\(66.7\%\)** (\(\mathcal{D}_2^{(R)} = 1/3\)). This establishes the conditional stability criterion:

\[\left|\mathcal{G}_{\text{physical}}(Ri_c)\right| < 1 + \alpha \implies \left|\mathcal{G}_{\text{reg}}(Ri_c)\right| < 1 \tag{6}\]

Because \(\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)} \to 1.0\) as distance from threshold dominates spatial curvature (\(|Ri_g - Ri_c| \gg C\)), C-Z0HR acts as an **equilibrium-compatible attenuator**—it enforces discrete numerical stability at the threshold boundary while recovering exact, unregularized physical turbulence physics under laminar conditions aloft.

### 5. Fast–Slow State-Space Representation (GSPT Integration)

In Geometric Surface Pattern Theory (GSPT), the control-theoretic SCM column is mapped onto a **singularly perturbed fast–slow state space** (\(\epsilon \ll 1\)):

* **Fast Variable:** Microscale Turbulent Kinetic Energy (\(e\), relaxation timescale \(\epsilon \ll 1\)):
  \[\epsilon \frac{de}{dt} = \mathcal{P}(e, S) - \mathcal{B}(e) - \varepsilon_{\text{diss}}(e) \tag{7}\]
* **Slow Variable:** Mesoscale Wind Shear (\(S\), geostrophic forcing timescale):
  \[\frac{dS}{dt} = G_0 - \gamma_s e S - r_s S \tag{8}\]

The fast nullcline \(F(e, S) = 0\) defines the critical manifold \(\mathcal{C}_0\) where turbulence "rides" slow geostrophic forcing. Transverse stability along \(\mathcal{C}_0\) is governed by the fast eigenvalue:

\[\lambda_f(e, S) = \frac{1}{\epsilon} \frac{\partial F}{\partial e} \tag{9}\]

The manifold is normally hyperbolic and attracting wherever \(\lambda_f < 0\). A true physical state-space transition occurs only when \(\lambda_f = 0\) (saddle-node bifurcation).

**The Decoupling Principle:** GSPT proves that spatial coordinate profile turning points (\(\zeta_z = 0\), "Fold Illusions" caused by LLJ coordinate compression) are logically independent of fast eigenvalue zero-crossings (\(\zeta_z = 0\; \not\!\!\!\!\!\iff \lambda_f = 0\)). Gating relaminarization triggers in NWP closures by \(\lambda_f \ge 0\) prevents SCMs from treating pure coordinate-stretching geometry as physical turbulence collapse, eliminating false bifurcation triggers and solving surface cold-bias divergence.

### 5. Vertical Curvature Kinematics: $Ri_{zz}$ for $Ri = \mathcal{R}(\zeta(z))$

Expanding the gradient Richardson number as a composite function of the Monin–Obukhov similarity variable $\zeta(z) \equiv z / L(z)$, where $L$ is the local Obukhov length:

$$Ri(z) = \mathcal{R}(\zeta(z))$$

Applying the chain rule yields the first and second vertical spatial derivatives:

$$\frac{\partial Ri}{\partial z} = \mathcal{R}'(\zeta) \zeta_z$$

$$Ri_{zz} \equiv \frac{\partial^2 Ri}{\partial z^2} = \mathcal{R}''(\zeta) (\zeta_z)^2 + \mathcal{R}'(\zeta) \zeta_{zz} \tag{7}$$

In the constant-flux surface layer where vertical gradients in Obukhov length are negligible ($\zeta_z = 1/L$ and $\zeta_{zz} = 0$), equation (7) simplifies to the continuous spatial curvature mapping:

$$Ri_{zz} = \frac{1}{L^2} \mathcal{R}''(\zeta) \tag{8}$$

---

### 6. Analytical Behavior of $\mathcal{R}''(\zeta)$ across Turbulence Regimes

The curvature spectrum $Ri_{zz}$ dictates how analytical stability functions map onto discrete computational grids:

* **Classic MOST Monotonicity:** For standard stability functions $\phi_m(\zeta) = \phi_h(\zeta) = 1 + \beta \zeta$ (with $\beta \approx 5.0$), the function reduces to $\mathcal{R}(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta \zeta}$. The second derivative is strictly negative across all stable states ($\zeta > 0$):

$$\mathcal{R}''(\zeta) = -\frac{2\beta}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^3} \implies Ri_{zz} = -\frac{2\beta}{L^2 (1 + \beta \zeta)^3} \tag{9}$$

As $\zeta \to \infty$, physical curvature vanishes ($Ri_{zz} \to 0$), reflecting smooth asymptotic decay of turbulent shear capacity under strongly stable stratification.

* **Non-Linear Functions & Critical Inflection:** In non-linear or short-tail closures, $\mathcal{R}(\zeta)$ exhibits localized inflection points ($\mathcal{R}''(\zeta_{\text{inf}}) = 0$) near critical stability $Ri_c$. Surrounding these points, localized derivative spikes produce large absolute spatial curvature ($\vert{}Ri_{zz}\vert{} \gg 0$).

---

### 7. Coupling $Ri_{zz}$ to Grid-Scale C-Z0HR Attenuation

Substituting the dimensionless grid resolution parameter $\hat{\Delta z} \equiv \frac{\Delta z}{L}$ into the profile curvature metric $C(z, \Delta z)$ connects continuous physical scaling directly to discrete numerical regularization:

$$C(\zeta, \hat{\Delta z}) = \frac{1}{2} \left\vert{} \mathcal{R}''(\zeta) \hat{\Delta z}^2 + \mathcal{R}'(\zeta) L \zeta_{zz} \Delta z^2 \right\vert{} + \epsilon_c Ri_c \tag{10}$$

Under spatial discretization, $C(\zeta, \hat{\Delta z})$ acts as an adaptive filter:

1. **Under-Resolved Grids / High Physical Curvature ($\hat{\Delta z} \gg 1$ or $\vert{}Ri_{zz}\vert{} \gg 0$):** The spatial metric expands, forcing the local mapping slope $\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)}(Ri_c) \to \frac{1}{1 + \alpha}$. This attenuates the discrete loop gain $\mathcal{G}_{\text{reg}}$ to guarantee contraction mapping stability ($\vert{}\mathcal{G}_{\text{reg}}\vert{} < 1$).
2. **Asymptotic Convergence Limit ($\hat{\Delta z} \to 0$):** Grid-resolved curvature vanishes ($C \to \epsilon_c Ri_c$), recovering unregularized MOST physics ($\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(R)} \to 1.0$) with zero residual numerical bias.
