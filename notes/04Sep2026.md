The **Cooperative Atmosphere–Surface Exchange Study 1999 (CASES-99)** field campaign, conducted throughout October 1999 over the flat grasslands of southeastern Kansas, is one of the premier experimental benchmarks for investigating the complex physics of the **Stable Boundary Layer (SBL)**, nocturnal low-level jets (LLJs), and land-surface exchange dynamics.

By utilizing flat prairie terrain, the campaign minimized complicated slope flows, allowing researchers to cleanly isolate nocturnal atmospheric transitions from orographic influence.

---

### 1. Experimental and Observational Setup

The CASES-99 campaign featured a highly dense, multi-scale array of meteorological instruments:

* **The 60-m Main Tower:** Densely instrumented with three-dimensional sonic anemometers at eight simultaneous levels (typically \(1.5\text{ m}\), \(5.0\text{ m}\), \(10.0\text{ m}\), \(20.0\text{ m}\), \(30.0\text{ m}\), \(40.0\text{ m}\), \(50.0\text{ m}\), and \(55.0\text{ m}\) above ground). For modeling platforms like the `SBLToolkit.jl` pipeline, these are frequently mapped to \(7\) non-uniform vertical grid levels. Slow-response wind vanes were additionally deployed at \(15\text{ m}\), \(25\text{ m}\), \(35\text{ m}\), and \(45\text{ m}\).
* **High-Resolution Thermocouple Array:** Temperature was recorded at \(34\) distinct vertical levels (\(0.23\text{ m}\), \(0.67\text{ m}\), \(2.3\text{ m}\), and every \(1.8\text{ m}\) above that), providing exceptionally fine-scale vertical profiles to capture steep thermal gradients near the ground.
* **High-Resolution Doppler Lidar (HRDL):** Located \(1.45\text{ km}\) southeast of the main tower, the HRDL scanned wind profiles continuously with \(30\text{-m}\) range resolution, capturing the spatial structure and evolution of the nocturnal Low-Level Jet.

### 2. The Three Turbulence Regimes

Analyzing the nocturnal CASES-99 dataset revealed **three distinct turbulence regimes** depending on the dominant mechanism of turbulence generation:

* **Regime 1 (Weak Turbulence):** This regime occurs when the mean wind speed is below a localized, height-dependent threshold. Turbulence is weak and generated primarily by small eddies associated with **local shear instability**, which are weakly modulated by local temperature gradients. Because the eddy length scale is smaller than the observation height, these eddies do not directly interact with the ground (a state often called a decoupled boundary layer).
* **Regime 2 (Strong Turbulence):** This regime is triggered when the wind speed exceeds its threshold value. Here, the boundary layer approaches near-neutral conditions. Turbulence strength increases systematically and linearly with wind speed because it is driven by **bulk shear** (scaling with the observation height, \(V/z\)). This strong turbulent mixing erodes vertical potential temperature gradients, causing sensible heat fluxes and temperature fluctuations to decrease once the threshold is crossed.
* **Regime 3 (Moderate/Top-Down Turbulence):** Observed under weak-wind conditions when turbulence is generated aloft—often due to Kelvin–Helmholtz (K-H) shear instabilities—and propagates or **diffuses downward** toward the surface. Because the downward-propagating turbulence decays as it nears the ground, the duration of these moderate turbulence events systematically increases with height.

---

### 3. Categories of Turbulence Intermittency

Corresponding to these three physical regimes, CASES-99 datasets are utilized to categorize the temporal intermittency of SBL turbulence into three dynamic behaviors:

* **Category A (Wind Speed Oscillations):** Enhanced turbulence is triggered as nocturnal wind speed oscillations (often caused by gravity waves, solitary waves, or density currents) push the ambient wind back and forth across the threshold value, toggling the local state between Regime 1 and Regime 2.
* **Category B (Local Shear Enhancements):** Weak, episodic turbulence bursts that remain strictly within Regime 1, generated by local shear or wave-induced perturbations that slightly reduce local stability without crossing into bulk-driven neutral mixing.
* **Category C (Top-Down Intrusions):** Abrupt bursts of top-down moderate turbulence (Regime 3) descending into an otherwise quiet, weak-turbulence environment. These events are statistically marked by transient, negative vertical temperature gradients as large eddies overturn aloft.

### 4. Low-Level Jet Nose and GSPT "Fold Illusions"

In SBL research, CASES-99 data from specific nights—such as **October 18, 1999 (Night 991018)** and **October 20, 1999**—are highly celebrated.

On October 20 (04:00 UTC), a pronounced Low-Level Jet core formed at \(z \approx 45\text{ m}\). At the core or "nose" of the jet, the vertical wind shear vanishes (\(S = |\partial \mathbf{u}/\partial z| \to 0\)). Standard boundary layer parameterizations suffer from division-by-zero singularities here, driving gradient Richardson numbers (\(Ri_g\)) to artificially spike toward infinity.

Under **Geometric Surface Pattern Theory (GSPT)**, analyzing these CASES-99 profiles shows that what looks like a sharp "profile knee" or sudden turbulence collapse near the critical Richardson number (\(Ri_c \approx 0.25\)) is actually a **"Fold Illusion"**. This visual inflection is a mathematical projection artifact driven by extreme coordinate compression (\(C_{\text{mapping}} \gg C_{\text{constitutive}}\)) under non-constant vertical fluxes rather than a true state-space bifurcation.

To prevent these singularities from corrupting numerical weather models, SBLToolkit employs **Track A Primitive Field Regularization**. This applies Tikhonov-Morozov filtering directly to the primitive velocity and potential temperature fields in log-height space *before* spatial differentiation is performed, cleanly isolating the jet-nose singularity and preserving SCM solver stability.

### Expanded GABLS3Adapters.jl Module

```julia
module GABLS3Adapters

using LinearAlgebra
using Statistics
using SBLToolkit
using SBLGating

export map_gabls3_to_jacobian, batch_process_gabls3_jacobian

"""
    map_gabls3_to_jacobian(z_idx, E, S, N2, config)
[Original mapping logic populated here as defined in previous architecture]
"""
function map_gabls3_to_jacobian(z_idx::Int, E::Vector{T}, S::Vector{T}, N2::Vector{T}, config::GSPTModel{T}) where T
    e_local = max(E[z_idx], T(1e-6))
    s_local = S[z_idx]
    n2_local = N2[z_idx]

    δ_reg = config.epsilon_c
    B_max = config.l0 * n2_local

    # Fast Variable Partial Derivatives (∂F/∂e, ∂F/∂S)
    dB_de = B_max * (2 * e_local * δ_reg^2) / (e_local^2 + δ_reg^2)^2
    dDiss_de = (1.5 * sqrt(e_local)) / config.l0
    Fe = config.l0 * s_local^2 - dB_de - dDiss_de
    Fs = 2 * config.l0 * e_local * s_local

    # Slow Variable Partial Derivatives (∂g/∂e, ∂g/∂S)
    gamma_s, r_s = T(0.1), T(0.01)
    ge, gs = -gamma_s * s_local, -gamma_s * e_local - r_s

    inv_eps = 1.0 / config.epsilon
    return [ Fe * inv_eps   Fs * inv_eps ;
             ge             gs           ]
end

"""
    batch_process_gabls3_jacobian(nc_data, config, gating_params)

Executes Jacobian mapping and bifurcation gating across 144 timesteps.
Maintains a Vector of GatingState objects (one per level) to handle
eigenvector continuation through time.
"""
function batch_process_gabls3_jacobian(
    u_mat::Matrix{T},
    v_mat::Matrix{T},
    theta_mat::Matrix{T},
    tke_mat::Matrix{T},
    z_levels::Vector{T},
    config::GSPTModel{T},
    gating_params::BifurcationGatingParams{T}
) where T
    Nz, Nt = size(u_mat) # GABLS3: Nz=150 (High-res) or 38 (Standard), Nt=144

    # 1. Initialize persistent gating states for each vertical level
    # This is critical for eigenvector continuation across timesteps.
    level_states = [GatingState(T) for _ in 1:Nz]

    # 2. Output Containers
    lambda_f_map = zeros(T, Nz, Nt)
    gate_active_map = zeros(Bool, Nz, Nt)
    km_gated_mat = zeros(T, Nz, Nt)

    # 3. Batch Processing Loop
    for t in 1:Nt
        # Apply Track A Regularization to primitives for this timestep
        # Extracts clean analytical S and N2 profiles
        S_eff, N2_eff = extract_regularized_gradients(u_mat[:,t], v_mat[:,t], theta_mat[:,t], z_levels)

        for i in 1:Nz
            # A. Extract reduced Jacobian
            J_fast = map_gabls3_to_jacobian(i, tke_mat[:,t], S_eff, N2_eff, config)

            # B. Track fast mode via eigenvector overlap [SBLGating logic]
            λ_f = extract_fast_eigenvalue(J_fast, level_states[i])

            # C. Evaluate GSPT coordinate Jacobian (ζ_z)
            # Uses L_profile reconstructed from regularized fluxes
            ζ_z_local = compute_local_coordinate_jacobian(z_levels[i], S_eff[i], N2_eff[i], config)

            # D. Decision: Update Gating Logic
            K_m_raw = config.l0 * sqrt(tke_mat[i,t]) # Base closure
            Km_g, _, is_gated = update_gating_state!(
                level_states[i], λ_f, ζ_z_local, K_m_raw, 0.0, gating_params
            )

            # E. Archive Results
            lambda_f_map[i, t] = λ_f
            gate_active_map[i, t] = is_gated
            km_gated_mat[i, t] = Km_g
        end
    end

    return lambda_f_map, gate_active_map, km_gated_mat
end

end # module
```

### Key Architectural Implementation Details

1. **State Management:** The function initializes an array of `GatingState` objects (one for each height level). This architecture allows `extract_fast_eigenvalue` to compare the current eigenvector \\(v_f^{(t)}\\) against \\(v_f^{(t-1)}\\) at the same altitude, preventing **mode-swapping** as the Low-Level Jet (LLJ) evolves [User provided code].
2. **Operator Commutation:** By calling an internal gradient extraction (Track A) before the Jacobian mapping, the batch processor enforces **pre-diagnostic regularization**. This prevents the \\(U_z^{-6}\\) variance explosion from corrupting the Jacobian at the jet nose.
3. **Physical Scaling:** The `map_gabls3_to_jacobian` function utilizes the **Hill-type non-monotonic buoyancy flux** \\(\mathcal{B}(e)\\). This is essential for Phase 3 Prognostic Integration because it enables the model to resolve **turbulence revival** as geostrophic forcing \\(G_0\\) rebuilds shear after a laminar collapse.
4. **Fold Illusion Protection:** The gate is only de-activated if the flow loses **normal hyperbolicity** (\\(\lambda_f > \epsilon_{off}\\)) [User provided code].
