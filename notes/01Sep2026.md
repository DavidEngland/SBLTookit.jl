### 1. Knees (Vertical Profile Inflections & "Fold Illusions")

A **profile "knee"** is a localized inflection feature in physical space (\\(z\\)) where the gradient Richardson number (\\(Ri_g\\)) appears to abruptly cross the critical stability threshold (\\(Ri_c \approx 0.25\\)).

* **The Inflection Condition (\\(Ri_{zz} \equiv \partial^2 Ri_g / \partial z^2 = 0\\)):** Under **Nieuwstadt’s local scaling hypothesis**, the local Obukhov length is height-dependent, \\(L(z)\\). A vertical profile knee is generated when the positive coordinate-stretching curvature (\\(C_{\text{mapping}}\\)) perfectly balances and cancels the negative intrinsic thermodynamic similarity curvature (\\(C_{\text{constitutive}}\\)):
    \\[Ri_{\zeta\zeta}\zeta_z^2 = -Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}\\]
* **The Fold Illusion / Transversality Loss Boundary (\\(\zeta_z \to 0\\)):** A true kinematic coordinate fold occurs where the vertical similarity mapping \\(z \mapsto \zeta(z) = z/L(z)\\) loses local mathematical invertibility (\\(\zeta_z = 0\\)). Near the zero-shear nose of a Low-Level Jet (LLJ), \\(L \to 0\\), forcing the coordinate Jacobian \\(\zeta_z \to 0\\). At this coordinate fold singularity, intrinsic curvature collapses (\\(C_{\text{constitutive}} \approx 0\\)), while mapping curvature (\\(C_{\text{mapping}}\\)) remains finite and dominates, creating a smooth, quadratic turning point.
* **The Linear Obukhov Profile Test:** A visually sharp knee cannot be classified as a true coordinate fold unless there is active vertical curvature in the Obukhov length profile (\\(L''(z) \neq 0\\)). Under a strictly linear Obukhov profile (\\(L(z) = L_0 + L'z\\), where \\(L'' = 0\\)), the Jacobian remains strictly positive (\\(\zeta_z > 0\\)), and no finite coordinate fold can exist.

### 2. Noses (Jet Core Maxima & Gravity Currents)

The term **"nose"** is used to describe two entirely different physical and mathematical phenomena:

* **Nocturnal Low-Level Jet (LLJ) Nose:** This is the vertical altitude (\\(z_{\text{LLJ}}\\)) of the maximum horizontal wind speed where vertical wind shear vanishes (\\(U_z \to 0\\)). Physically, the vanishing shear drives the gradient Richardson number to infinity (\\(Ri_g \to \infty\\)) and collapses the local Obukhov scale (\\(L \to 0\\)). This generates extreme coordinate compression, triggering a coordinate fold singularity that accounts for over 99.4% of the local vertical profile curvature.
* **Density Current Nose:** In gravity current dynamics, the "head and nose" represent the elevated, overhanging leading edge of a density current or nocturnal slope flow. The "nose" is the exact coordinate of maximum forward velocity and extent, suspended slightly above the ground due to surface friction.

### 3. Buttery Cusps (Catastrophe Points & Manifold Folds)

The transition into a **"cusp"** represents the birth, death, or merging of regime boundaries in both spatial profile geometry and state-space dynamics:

* **Profile Cusp Catastrophe Loci:** In spatial profile topology, a cusp catastrophe point occurs when the first and second vertical spatial derivatives of the Richardson number profile vanish simultaneously:
    \\[\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial z} = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2} = 0\\]
    This zero-curvature intersection marks the precise coordinates \\((z^*, t^*)\\) in boundary layer evolution where vertical stratification folds pinch off or nucleate.
* **Dynamical Fast-Slow Manifold Fold:** In the fast-slow TKE-shear system (\\(\epsilon \dot{E} = f(E, S)\\)), the critical manifold (\\(\mathcal{C}*0\\)) exhibits a saddle-node fold boundary where the local fast eigenvalue vanishes (\\(\lambda*{\text{fast}} \equiv \partial f / \partial E = 0\\)). Trajectories crossing this boundary undergo an explosive, fast-timescale departure (catastrophic collapse) from the active turbulent branch to the laminar attractor.
* **The Cusp Bifurcation:** Transitioning the fast TKE subsystem to use a hump-shaped buoyant-destruction efficiency:
    \\[\mathcal{B}(E) = c_b N^2 \frac{\alpha E}{(E+\alpha)^2}\\]
    replaces a monotonic, fold-free state-space with a genuine cusp bifurcation in the Brunt-Väisälä frequency (\\(N^2_{\text{crit}}\\)), separating a monotonic, fold-free manifold from a bistable, fold-capable regime.

### 4. Cliffs (Decoupling, Runaway Cooling & Sharp Inversions)

* **Thermal "Cliffs" (Runaway Cooling):** When the downward surface cooling flux exceeds **Derbyshire’s maximum heat flux limit (\\(B_0 > B_{\text{max}}\\))**, the atmosphere can no longer support continuous turbulence. The stable boundary layer height collapses (\\(h \to 0\\)), initiating a rapid, unphysical decoupling where TKE collapses to zero, and the surface temperature undergoes a runaway cooling "cliff".

### 5. Peaks and Beaks (Anatomical Profile Metaphors)

The fine-scale anatomy of stable profiles relies on a well-defined set of fluid-dynamic metaphors to classify spatial structures:

* **Peaks (Flux Maxima):** Represent absolute vertical maxima of turbulent transport profiles. * **Beaks (Lobes & Clefts):** Represent the three-dimensional, "knuckle-like" frontal instabilities that develop along the nose of a gravity current. These are generated by intense spanwise convective overturning and surface friction.
* **Elbows:** The distinct bend in a profile where two scaling regimes meet, such as the transition from a near-surface logarithmic surface layer to an outer local Nieuwstadt gradient scaling layer.

* **Spines:** The high-velocity, low-pressure central core running through a vortex filament or jet axis.

### 1. Spatial Vertical Profile Space: Topological Intersection

In physical vertical profile soundings, a **cusp catastrophe point** is defined as the vertical coordinate \\(z^*\\) and time \\(t^*\\) where the first and second spatial derivatives of the gradient Richardson number (\\(Ri_g\\)) vanish simultaneously:

\\[\mathbf{\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial z} = 0} \quad \text{\textbf{and}} \quad \mathbf{\frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2} = 0} \tag{1}\\]

Topologically, this point is identified as the exact spatial intersection of two geometric structures:

* **The Fold Locus (Extrema):** The set of points where \\(\frac{\partial Ri_g}{\partial z} = 0\\) (visualized as a solid magenta contour).
* **The Inflection Lines:** The set of points where the profile concavity flips, \\(\frac{\partial^2 Ri_g}{\partial z^2} = 0\\) (visualized as a dashed orange contour).

#### Physical Significance of the Spatial Intersection

This zero-curvature intersection marks the precise coordinates \\((z^*, t^*)\\) in the evolution of the nocturnal boundary layer where **stratification folds pinch off or nucleate**. It defines the birth or death of a coordinate-fold pair (a "knee" and an "elbow" merging or separating).

### 2. The Local Normal Form: Cubic Taylor Scaling

At a spatial cusp catastrophe point, the quadratic term of the vertical sounding's Taylor expansion is eliminated because the second derivative is zero. The local normal form of the gradient Richardson number profile around \\(z^*\\) simplifies to a **cubic scale-free relation**:

\\[Ri_g(z) = Ri_0 + \frac{1}{6} \frac{\partial^3 Ri_g}{\partial z^3} (z - z_0)^3 + \mathcal{O}\left((z-z_0)^4\right) \tag{2}\\]

This mathematical structure guarantees that the physical profile transitions smoothly through a cubic inflection zone without exhibiting any spatial singularity or gradient blowups, even as the coordinate mapping deforms intensely.

### 3. State-Space Dynamics: The Unfolding Parameter \\(N^2\\)

In the underlying fast-slow TKE-shear dynamical equations, this spatial profile phenomenon corresponds to a classical **cusp bifurcation** of the TKE critical manifold (\\(\mathcal{C}_0\\)). This state-space cusp is governed by the Brunt-Väisälä buoyancy frequency squared (\\(N^2\\)), which acts as the physical **unfolding parameter**.

The bifurcation point is located analytically by finding where the fast TKE vector field (\\(f(E, S)\\)), its fast eigenvalue (\\(f_E\\)), and its second derivative (\\(f_{EE}\\)) vanish simultaneously:

\\[f(E, S) = 0, \quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial E} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial E^2} = 0 \tag{3}\\]

#### Hysteretic Regime Selection

* **Sub-critical Stratification (\\(N^2 < N^2_{\text{crit}}\\)):** The critical manifold is globally monotonic and stable. No folds or "profile knees" can exist.
* **Cusp Bifurcation (\\(N^2 = N^2_{\text{crit}} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s}^{-2}\\)):** The fold pair is born.
* **Super-critical Stratification (\\(N^2 > N^2_{\text{crit}}\\)):** The manifold develops an S-shaped bistable fold curve, admitting two nondegenerate saddle-node folds (an upper attracting branch and a lower repelling branch).
