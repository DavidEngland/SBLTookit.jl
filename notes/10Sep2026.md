The **fast subsystem Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE) equation** models microscale turbulence as a singularly perturbed fast variable ($e$ or $E$) that relaxes rapidly toward a critical manifold on a small timescale ($\epsilon \ll 1$) relative to the slow evolution of mesoscale wind shear ($S$).

Analyzing the fast TKE subsystem involves examining its mathematical formulation, its physical regularizations, its state-space manifold geometry, and its fast-eigenvalue stability.

---

**Mathematical Formulation & Governing Equations**

The non-dimensional fast TKE budget represents a balance among mechanical shear production ($\mathcal{P}$), buoyant destruction/loss ($\mathcal{B}$), and viscous dissipation ($\varepsilon$):

$$\epsilon \frac{\mathrm{d}e}{\mathrm{d}t} = f(e, S; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = \mathcal{P}(e,S) - \mathcal{B}(e) - \varepsilon(e) \tag{1}$$

Depending on the specific closure variant within the codebase, the budget components are structured as follows:

* **Mechanical Shear Production ($\mathcal{P}$):** Formulated as $\mathcal{P}(e,S) = l_0 \sqrt{e + \delta} \, S^2$, where $l_0$ is the turbulent mixing length.
* **Buoyant Loss ($\mathcal{B}$):** Parameterized either via a **Hill-type saturating rational function** or a **non-monotonic heat flux closure**:

$$\mathcal{B}(e) = B_{0,\max} \left(\frac{e^2}{e^2 + \delta_{\text{reg}}^2}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad B_{\text{mod}}(e) = l_0 \sqrt{e + \delta} \cdot \frac{c_b N^2 \alpha e}{(e + \alpha)^2} \tag{2}$$

* **Viscous Dissipation ($\varepsilon$):** Closed using a 1.5-order Kolmogorov-type dissipation rate:

$$\varepsilon(e) = \frac{e^{3/2}}{l_0} \tag{3}$$

---

**Specialized Regularizations & Singularity Mitigations**

Standard 1.5-order closures suffer from severe numerical singularities near the laminar limit ($e \to 0$). The fast subsystem incorporates two crucial mathematical regularizations to restore physical and numerical stability:

**A. Velocity-Scale Regularization Floor ($\delta = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$)**

In classical unregularized formulations ($\delta = 0$), production contains $\sqrt{e}$, causing its derivative to blow up at zero energy:

$$\lim_{e \to 0^+} \frac{\partial}{\partial e}\left(l_0 \sqrt{e} S^2\right) = +\infty \tag{4}$$

Adding a small velocity-scale floor ($\delta = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$) inside $\sqrt{e + \delta}$ restores $C^1$-continuity of the fast vector field across $e = 0$, eliminating singular Jacobian derivatives and stabilizing linear stability analysis near the laminar boundary.

**B. Non-Monotonic Buoyant Destruction & Shear-Driven Reignition**

* **Manifold Folding:** Standard monotonic stability closures (like Businger–Dyer or Louis functions) cannot generate an equilibrium fold. The non-monotonic closure $B_{\text{mod}}(e)$ requires the full product-rule derivative, since $l_0\sqrt{e+\delta}$ is itself $e$-dependent — an earlier pass omitted this prefactor term:

$$\frac{\partial B_{\text{mod}}}{\partial e} = \frac{l_0 c_b N^2 \alpha e}{2\sqrt{e+\delta}(e+\alpha)^2} + l_0 \sqrt{e+\delta} \cdot \frac{c_b N^2 \alpha (\alpha - e)}{(e+\alpha)^3}$$

The second term's sign flip at $e = \alpha$ is what creates the non-monotonicity in $f(e,S)$ needed to fold the critical manifold into an S-shaped curve in phase space.

* **Shear-Driven Reignition:** Evaluating the complete derivative above at $e = 0$ gives $\left.\frac{\partial B_{\text{mod}}}{\partial e}\right\vert{}_{e=0} = \frac{l_0 \sqrt{\delta}\, c_b N^2}{\alpha}$ (the first term vanishes since it is $\propto e$). Substituting into the linearized fast subsystem at $e=0$:

$$\left.\frac{\partial \dot{e}}{\partial e}\right\vert{}_{e=0} = \frac{l_0}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{\delta}} - \frac{\sqrt{\delta}\, c_b N^2}{\alpha}\right)$$

Reignition is therefore **conditional, not unconditional**: it requires shear to clear a stratification-dependent threshold, $S^2 > \dfrac{2\delta c_b N^2}{\alpha}$. Because the growth rate scales as $\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-1/2})$, treating $\delta$ as an arbitrary numerical floor ($10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{s}^{-2}$) makes the reignition rate a regularization artifact rather than a physical quantity. To carry physical meaning, $\delta$ should instead be anchored to a physical microscale, e.g. the Kolmogorov velocity scale $v_\eta^2 = (\nu\varepsilon)^{1/2}$.

---

**Critical Manifold ($\mathcal{C}_0$) and Fast Stability Eigenvalue ($\lambda_f$)**

In the singular limit $\epsilon \to 0$, fast TKE dynamics collapse onto the **critical manifold ($\mathcal{C}_0$)**, defined by the algebraic zero-locus of the fast vector field:

$$\mathcal{C}_0 = \left\{ (e, S) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^2 \;\middle\vert{}\; f(e, S; \boldsymbol{\mu}) = 0 \right\} \tag{5}$$

The transverse stability and normal hyperbolicity of $\mathcal{C}_0$ are governed by the **fast stability eigenvalue ($\lambda_f$)**, defined as the scalar partial derivative of $f$ with respect to $e$:

$$\lambda_f(e, S) \equiv \frac{\partial f}{\partial e} = \frac{l_0 S^2}{2\sqrt{e+\delta}} - \frac{\partial \mathcal{B}}{\partial e} - \frac{3\sqrt{e}}{2 l_0} \tag{6}$$

* **Normally Attracting Sheet ($\lambda_f < -\epsilon_\lambda$):** Under Fenichel's Theorem, if $\lambda_f < 0$, the manifold branch is normally attracting, and trajectories relax exponentially onto the invariant slow manifold sheet ($\mathcal{C}_0^+$) on a fast timescale $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon / \vert{}\lambda_f\vert{})$.
* **Loss of Normal Hyperbolicity ($\lambda_f = 0$):** At the fold boundary of $\mathcal{C}_0$, where $\frac{\partial f}{\partial e} = 0$, normal hyperbolicity is lost. The Fenichel invariance breaks down, and the trajectory undergoes an irreversible fast-timescale jump (laminar collapse or runaway relaminarization) toward the zero-TKE attractor.

**Decoupling Fast TKE Dynamics from Spatial Coordinate Geometry**

A central finding of GSPT is the **Geometric–Dynamical Decoupling Principle**, which proves that an observational profile turning feature in physical space is independent of fast subsystem stability. Chaining a triple $\not\!\!\!\Longleftrightarrow$ across three unrelated terms is logically sloppy; the correct statement is two separate pairwise non-implications:

$$\left(\zeta_z(z^*, t^*) = 0\right) \not\!\!\!\implies \left(\lambda_f(e^*, S^*) = 0\right) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left(\lambda_f(e^*, S^*) = 0\right) \not\!\!\!\implies \left(e \to 0\right) \tag{7}$$

**Scope Caveat (0-D Assumption)**

This entire fast-subsystem analysis is a **local, 0-D phase-space projection**: it omits the spatial turbulent-transport divergence term $\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left[K_e \frac{\partial e}{\partial z}\right]$ that would appear in the full prognostic TKE equation. Vertical turbulent transport can itself damp or trigger manifold folds, so treating $\mathcal{C}_0$ and $\lambda_f$ as local invariant-manifold diagnostics — rather than a full spatial stability statement — is an explicit underlying assumption of this section.
