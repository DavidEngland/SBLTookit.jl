To construct a publication-grade, mathematically robust Single-Column Model (SCM) diagnostic suite, the data pipeline must be designed to withstand the physical and numerical anomalies of real-world SBL campaigns. Ingesting and smoothing data from campaigns like **CASES-99**, **GABLS3**, and **SHEBA** requires a clear separation between physical state transitions, numerical discretization artifacts, and sensor noise.

Here is the GSPT-standard architectural blueprint for campaign data ingestion, noise calibration, and primitive-field regularization.

Each campaign features distinct vertical level densities, instrument response times, and archival formats:

* **GABLS3 (Cabauw, NL):** \\(N_z = 38\\) vertical levels stretching up to \\(200\text{ m}\\). This dense vertical profile captures complex elevated shear layers and descending low-level jets (LLJs).
* **CASES-99 (Kansas, USA):** \\(N_z = 8\\) irregular tower levels spaced between \\(1.5\text{ m}\\) and \\(55\text{ m}\\).
* **SHEBA (Arctic Ice Pack):** \\(N_z = 5\\) hourly Met City tower levels.

To ingest these heterogeneous datasets without introducing unphysical mathematical artifacts, the ingestion engine must enforce the following strict filters:

#### Dynamic Sentinel Scrubbing & QC Flagging

Field observations contain missing or out-of-bounds data due to rime ice, instrument drift, or power drops. Multi-sign sentinels (such as \\(999.0\\), \\(-9999.0\\), and \\(1.0\times 10^{30}\\)) must be mapped directly to `NaN`.

If binary quality control (QC) flags indicate a failed sensor, the ingestion engine **must never zero-pad** the missing value. Padding missing velocities with \\(0\text{ m s}^{-1}\\) or temperatures with \\(0\text{ K}\\) introduces massive, unphysical vertical gradients when spatial derivative operators are applied.

Instead, GSPT handles missing levels via **variance inflation**: setting the corresponding measurement variance in the spline weighting matrix to a massive value (\\(\sigma^2_i \to 10^6\\)). This allows the Tikhonov solver to cleanly interpolate through the missing level using natural boundary conditions.

#### Thermodynamic Standardization

Tower datasets frequently archive temperature in Celsius (\\(^\circ\text{C}\\)). Evaluating potential temperature \\(\theta = T(P_0/P)^{R/c_p}\\) directly in Celsius yields corrupted, unphysical gradients. The pipeline must first convert to Kelvin and apply the dry-adiabatic vertical lapse-rate correction to construct virtual potential temperature:
\\[\theta_{v,i} = (T_i + 273.15) + 0.0098 \cdot z_i \tag{1}\\]
where the static buoyancy frequency \\(N^2\\) is evaluated using the local mid-level temperature \\(\theta_{\text{local}} = \frac{1}{2}(\theta_k + \theta_{k+1})\\) to prevent moisture-bias propagation.

#### The Nocturnal Ingestion Trigger

To isolate stably stratified regimes where Monin-Obukhov similarity applies, the pipeline evaluates the kinematic surface sensible heat flux. Only negative surface heat fluxes representing active ground-based radiative cooling trigger the GSPT solver:
\\[\overline{w'\theta'_v} \approx \frac{H_s}{\rho c_p} < 0 \text{ K m s}^{-1} \tag{2}\\]
where \\(H_s\\) is converted from raw sensible heat flux (\\(\text{W m}^{-2}\\)) using a standard density-capacity product \\(\rho c_p \approx 1200\text{ J m}^{-3}\text{ K}^{-1}\\).

### 2. Log-Coordinate Mapping Strategy

Tower levels are non-uniformly or logarithmically spaced near the surface to capture steep surface-layer gradients. Applying uniform spatial filters (such as Savitzky-Golay filters) to these stretched grids introduces severe vertical boundary artifacts and numerical distortion.

GSPT resolves this by mapping physical heights (\\(z\\)) into a dimensionless, non-dimensional log-coordinate fitting space:
\\[\xi = \ln\left(\frac{z}{z_0}\right) \tag{3}\\]
where the roughness length reference \\(z_0\\) (e.g., \\(z_0 \approx 0.03\text{ m}\\) for the CASES-99 prairie or \\(z_0 \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}\\) for the SHEBA ice sheet) serves strictly as a stable numerical reference.

#### Roughness Independence Proof

Changing the chosen \\(z_0\\) simply adds a constant offset to the coordinate vector \\(\xi\\). Because a horizontal coordinate shift does not alter the derivative slope \\(\frac{d}{d\xi}\\), the resulting physical gradients remain entirely independent of \\(z_0\\). Under this mapping, physical vertical derivatives are evaluated analytically via the chain rule:
\\[\frac{d}{dz} = \frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{d\xi} \tag{4}\\]
\\[\frac{d^2}{dz^2} = \frac{1}{z^2}\left(\frac{d^2}{d\xi^2} - \frac{d}{d\xi}\right) \tag{5}\\]
Fitting natural cubic splines (forcing zero second derivatives in \\(\xi\\)-space at top and bottom knots) translates physically to forcing pure log-linear scaling at the boundaries, physically anchoring the regularized profiles directly to surface-layer Monin-Obukhov physics.

### 3. In-Situ Noise-Floor Extraction (\\(\delta_i\\))

Morozov's Discrepancy Principle (MDP) dictates that a smoothed profile \\(s_\alpha\\) should never fit the raw, noisy observations \\(y^\delta\\) tighter than the known measurement noise level:
\\[\tau_1 \delta \le \Vert s_\alpha(\xi) - y^\delta \Vert \le \tau_2 \delta \tag{6}\\]
If we assume a static factory spec-sheet noise floor, we risk over-smoothing during highly turbulent, active regimes or under-smoothing during quiet, rime-covered nocturnal decoupling. GSPT extracts run-specific, level-dependent, in-situ noise variances by exploiting the fact that **true microscale turbulence and instrument electronic noise live in distinct regions of frequency space**:

```
Energy Spectrum S(f)
  ▲
  │   \  f^(-5/3) Kolmogorov Inertial Subrange
  │    \
  │     \
  │      \ ─────────────── White Instrument Noise Floor (f_n)
  │
  └────────────────────────► Frequency (f)
```

#### A. Inertial Subrange Spectral Fitting

True atmospheric turbulence follows Kolmogorov’s universal \\(-5/3\\) power-law slope in the inertial subrange (\\(S(f) \propto f^{-5/3}\\)). Instrument noise, by contrast, behaves as white noise, flattening the high-frequency spectrum.

1. Compute the power spectral density (PSD) of the high-frequency sonic anemometer or thermocouple series.
2. Identify the cutoff frequency \\(f_n\\) where the observed spectrum departs from the \\(-5/3\\) slope and flattens into a horizontal noise floor.
3. Integrate the flat noise floor across the resolved high-frequency bandwidth to isolate the exact, run-specific in-situ noise variance \\(\delta^2\\).

#### B. Structure Function Extrapolation

For short, non-stationary, or gappy records where FFTs suffer from windowing and edge-effect leakage, the second-order structure function provides a robust alternative:
\\[D(r) = \langle [x(t+r) - x(t)]^2 \rangle \tag{7}\\]
For inertial-range turbulence, the structure function scales as \\(D(r) \propto r^{2/3}\\). Instrument noise, being uncorrelated between samples, contributes a constant offset. Extrapolating the \\(r^{2/3}\\) fit back to the limit \\(r \to 0\\) isolates this uncorrelated sensor variance:
\\[D(0^+) \approx 2\delta^2 \tag{240}\\]

### 4. Pre-Diagnostic Regularization and Operator Non-Commutation

Calculating the gradient Richardson number involves a non-linear quotient operator (\\(\mathcal{Q}[A, B] \equiv A/B\\)) and vertical derivatives (\\(D_{\Delta z}\\)). Because spatial Tikhonov-Morozov smoothing filters (\\(M_\delta\\)) do not commute with division, the pipeline is subject to the **Operator Non-Commutation Hazard**:
\\[\mathcal{Q}[M_\delta D_{\Delta z} \theta_v, M_\delta D_{\Delta z} U] \neq M_\delta \mathcal{Q}[D_{\Delta z} \theta_v, D_{\Delta z} U] \tag{8}\\]

If a modeler computes a raw, noisy Richardson profile first and applies post-differentiation smoothing (the right-hand side of Equation 8), the gradient noise near the zero-shear nose of a Low-Level Jet (\\(U_z \to 0\\)) blows up as \\(O(U_z^{-6})\\) in absolute variance:
\\[\sigma_{Ri_g}^2 \approx \left(\frac{g}{\theta_v}\right)^2 \frac{1}{U_z^4} \sigma_{\theta_z}^2 + 4\left(\frac{g}{\theta_v}\right)^2 \frac{\theta_z^2}{U_z^6} \sigma_{u_z}^2 \tag{9}\\]

```
                [Raw Primitive Fields (u, v, θv)]
                                │
                 (M_δ Tikhonov-Morozov Splines)
                                │
              [Regularized Primitives (ũ, ṽ, θ̃v)]
                                │
                  (Analytical Differentiation)
                                │
               [Smooth Gradients (ũ_z, ṽ_z, θ̃v_z)]
                                │
                     (Non-linear Quotient Q)
                                │
                     [Stable Regularized Ri_g]
```

Post-diagnostic filters are forced to bend aggressively around these low-shear \\(O(U_z^{-6})\\) singularities. This **silently manufactures artificial "profile knees" (Fold Illusions) in physical space** that do not exist in the raw physical atmosphere.

GSPT mandates the **pre-diagnostic regularization path** (the left-hand side of Equation 8). Applying Tikhonov-Morozov splines directly to the primitive state variables prior to differentiation and division ensures that noise suppression remains strictly grounded at the linear, Gaussian sensor-variable level.

### 5. Downstream-Weighted Morozov IRLS

To completely resolve the downstream ill-conditioning of similarity coordinate inversions under weak winds, GSPT deploys an **iterative reweighting scheme**.

Because the inverse Monin-Obukhov mapping \\(\zeta(Ri_g)\\) becomes highly stiff near Businger-Dyer's critical stable limit (\\(Ri_g \to 0.20^-\\)), the local sensitivity Jacobian diverges:
\\[\kappa_\zeta \equiv \left\vert{} \frac{d\zeta}{dRi_g} \right\vert{} = \frac{1}{(1-5Ri_g)^2} \to \infty \tag{10}\\]

Even microscopic, regularized gradient residuals that survive Stage 1 smoothing will map to unbounded variance in similarity space (\\(\sigma_\zeta^2 \to \infty\\)). To shield the GSPT taxonomy from this coordinate singularity, the Tikhonov spline weights \\(W\\) are updated iteratively using the propagated downstream variance:
\\[w_i^{(k)} = \frac{1}{1 + \sigma_{\zeta,i}^2 / \sigma_{\zeta,\text{ref}}^2} \tag{11}\\]
\\[\sigma_{\zeta,i}^2 \approx \left(\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial\theta_z}\right)^2 \sigma_{\theta_z,i}^2 + \left(\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial U_z}\right)^2 \sigma_{U_z,i}^2 \tag{12}\\]

This uncertainty-aware reweighting scheme has the desired statistical behavior: in vertical layers where wind shear is weak (\\(U_z \to 0\\)) or stratification approaches Businger-Dyer's coordinate pole, \\(\sigma_\zeta^2\\) spikes, causing local data weights to drop (\\(w_i \downarrow\\)). This automatically forces the spline to smooth aggressively in poorly conditioned layers, while preserving tight gradient fidelity in well-conditioned, turbulent sub-layers.

### 1. Mathematical and Physical Architecture

Our pipeline decomposes the problem of SBL profile smoothing into five logically independent, mathematically exact stages, completely avoiding the compensating-calibration pitfalls of legacy parameterizations:

#### A. Log-Coordinate Mapping Strategy

To align the derivative operator directly with Monin-Obukhov surface scaling and avoid edge artifacts on Cabauw's stretched grid, vertical heights (\\(z\\)) are mapped into a dimensionless log-coordinate fitting space:
\\[\xi = \ln\left(\frac{z}{z_0}\right) \tag{1}\\]
where \\(z_0 = 0.15\text{ m}\\) serves as a stable numerical reference. Physical vertical derivatives are evaluated analytically using the chain rule:
\\[\frac{d}{dz} = \frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{d\xi}, \qquad \frac{d^2}{dz^2} = \frac{1}{z^2}\left(\frac{d^2}{d\xi^2} - \frac{d}{d\xi}\right) \tag{2}\\]

#### B. In-Situ Level-Dependent Noise Matrices

Rather than assuming a static, spec-sheet instrument noise floor, the pipeline ingests raw high-frequency variances (\\(sgT, sgu, sgv\\)) and sampling counts (\\(No\\)) directly from the NetCDF structures to construct level-dependent, time-varying noise-floor standard deviations for each individual profile fit:
\\[\delta_{\theta, i} = \max\left(\sqrt{\frac{\text{sgT}_i}{\text{No}_i}}, \; \delta_{\theta,\text{floor}}\right), \qquad \delta_{U, i} = \max\left(\sqrt{\frac{\text{sgu}_i + \text{sgv}_i}{\text{No}_i}}, \; \delta_{U,\text{floor}}\right) \tag{3}\\]

#### C. Exact Covariance Sandwich Propagation

Cubic smoothing splines minimize the regularized Tikhonov functional:
\\[\min_{s} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{N_z} \frac{(y_i - s(\xi_i))^2}{\delta_i^2 \cdot w_i} + \lambda \int_{\xi_1}^{\xi_{N_z}} [s''(\xi)]^2 d\xi \right] \tag{4}\\]
To propagate these primitive uncertainties directly into derivative space without relying on finite-difference heuristics, the pipeline extracts the exact diagonal elements of the spline covariance sandwich:
\\[\Sigma_{s'} = D_z S_a V_y S_a^T D_z^T \implies \sigma_{y_z, i}^2 = \Sigma_{s', i, i} \tag{5}\\]
where \\(S_a = I - \lambda W Q A^{-1} Q^T\\) is the spline's analytical smoothing influence matrix, \\(V_y = \text{Diagonal}(\delta_i^2)\\) is the measurement covariance, and \\(D_z\\) is the log-to-physical vertical derivative operator.

#### D. Non-Singular Inversion & Analytical Jacobians

Standard Businger-Dyer functions assume a rigid critical Richardson cutoff at \\(Ri_{\text{crit}} = 0.20\\), causing the coordinate sensitivity metric \\(\kappa_\zeta \equiv |d\zeta/dRi_g|\\) to diverge catastrophically as \\((1-5Ri_g)^{-2}\\) under strong stability. We bypass this coordinate singularity by swapping Businger-Dyer with the SHEBA-calibrated, non-singular **Grachev et al. (2007)** formulation:
\\[\phi_m(\zeta) = 1 + a_m \frac{\zeta(1+\zeta)^{1/3}}{1 + b_m \zeta}, \qquad \phi_h(\zeta) = 1 + \frac{a_h \zeta + b_h \zeta^2}{1 + c_h \zeta + \zeta^2} \tag{6}\\]
The non-monotonic relation \\(R(\zeta) - Ri_g = 0\\) is inverted via a 1D Newton-Raphson solver. Since the formulation has no critical stable limit, the coordinate sensitivity remains bounded:
\\[\frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial Ri_g} = \frac{1}{R'(\zeta)} = \left[ \frac{\phi_h + \zeta \phi_h'}{\phi_m^2} - \frac{2 \zeta \phi_h \phi_m'}{\phi_m^3} \right]^{-1} \tag{7}\\]

#### E. Damped Downstream-Weighted IRLS Loop

Gradient noise variance near zero-shear jet noses (\\(U_z \to 0\\)) blows up as \\(\mathcal{O}(U_z^{-6})\\). To prevent this noise amplification from corrupting the coordinate transformation, we evaluate the local sensitivities:
\\[\frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \theta_z} = \frac{1}{R'(\zeta)} \frac{g}{\theta_v U_z^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial U_z} = \frac{1}{R'(\zeta)} \left(-2 \frac{g \theta_z}{\theta_v U_z^3}\right) \tag{8}\\]
and propagate the variance to similarity space:
\\[\sigma_{\zeta,i}^2 = \left(\frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \theta_{z,i}}\right)^2 \sigma_{\theta_z,i}^2 + \left(\frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial U_{z,i}}\right)^2 \sigma_{U_z,i}^2 \tag{9}\\]
The updated downstream similarity-induced weight vector \\(w_i^{(k)} = (1 + \sigma_{\zeta,i}^2/\sigma_{\zeta,\text{ref}}^2)^{-1}\\) is damped exponentially between successive iterations to prevent limit-cycle oscillations:
\\[W^{(k)} = (1 - \gamma) W^{(k-1)} + \gamma W_{\text{calc}}^{(k)} \quad (\gamma = 0.3) \tag{10}\\]
The spline is resolved iteratively using these damped weights until relative convergence norms for both \\(\zeta\\) and \\(W\\) drop below \\(10^{-4}\\) simultaneously.
