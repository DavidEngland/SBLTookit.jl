Part 2: Answer Key

1. Embedding \delta allows for the derivation of an exact C^1 Jacobian derivative and ensures that all fast budget terms maintain consistent TKE dissipation units (m^2 s^{-3}). This integration provides a definitive resolution to previous mathematical and dimensional gaps.
2. The fast subsystem, represented by E, governs the evolution of Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE) through factors like length scale and buoyancy. The slow subsystem, represented by S, dictates the evolution of mean shear driven by external geostrophic forcing and moderated by turbulent feedback.
3. The eigenvalue \lambda_f is defined as the derivative of the vector field f(E, S) with respect to E (f_E). It governs the normal hyperbolicity of the system and is used to classify the stability of different manifold branches.
4. The Attracting Branch is characterized by a negative eigenvalue (\lambda_f < 0). This state allows for stable fast relaxation of the system's trajectories onto the slow manifold \mathcal{C}_0.
5. The Fold Boundary occurs when \lambda_f = 0, signifying a total loss of normal hyperbolicity. This critical point triggers a phenomenon known as shear-driven TKE collapse, representing an explosive departure from the manifold.
6. The Repelling Branch is defined by a positive eigenvalue (\lambda_f > 0). In this state, the manifold becomes unstable, actively pushing trajectories away rather than allowing them to settle.
7. The root-finding vector is used to identify the precise coordinates of saddle-node fold points (E_{\text{fold}}, S_{\text{fold}}). It functions by seeking the simultaneous roots where both the vector field f(E, S) and its derivative f_E(E, S) equal zero.
8. The initial guess is set to this value to ensure the solver begins its iterations strictly above the production cutoff. This positioning is necessary for the robust identification of fold points during the Newton-Raphson process.
9. The Timestep Safety Factor \eta is used to calculate the effective Runge-Kutta 4 (RK4) step safety. It ensures numerical stability during the integration of stiff ODEs by adjusting the timestep based on the magnitude of the eigenvalue \lambda_f.
10. The synthesis ensures dimensional consistency by embedding the regularization floor \delta into the vector field so that all fast budget terms retain true m^2 s^{-3} units. This alignment allows the system to remain mathematically closed and physically accurate.

Part 4: Glossary of Key Terms

Term	Definition
Attracting Branch	A stable branch of the manifold where \lambda_f < 0, allowing trajectories to relax onto the slow manifold.
Buoyant Sink Efficiency (c_b)	A dimensionless saturation coefficient (value: 0.50) that influences the TKE fast subsystem dynamics.
Fold Boundary	The point where \lambda_f = 0, representing the loss of normal hyperbolicity and the onset of TKE collapse.
Geostrophic Forcing (G)	The external driving force behind the evolution of the slow subsystem (mean shear).
Jacobian Derivative (\lambda_f)	The exact local fast eigenvalue obtained by differentiating the vector field f with respect to E.
Mean Shear (S)	The primary variable of the slow subsystem, moderated by external forcing and turbulent feedback.
Normal Hyperbolicity	A dynamical property governed by the eigenvalue \lambda_f; its loss at the fold boundary leads to system instability.
Regularization Floor (\delta)	A velocity scale smoothing constant (10^{-6} m^2 s^{-2}) embedded in the vector field to ensure mathematical closure.
Saddle-Node Fold Points	Critical coordinates (E_{\text{fold}}, S_{\text{fold}}) where the system undergoes a transition, identified via root-finding.
Shear Damping Coeff. (c_1)	A parameter (1.80 s \cdot m^{-2}) representing the turbulent feedback of energy back onto the mean shear.
Stiff ODE Integration	A numerical method for solving differential equations that are numerically unstable unless very small timesteps are used.
TKE (E)	Turbulent Kinetic Energy; the primary variable of the fast subsystem dynamics.
